## SHOPIFY DESCRIPTION

In 1909, a small shop in Tokyo decided to press sweet red bean paste into a fish-shaped iron mould. The fish was a sea bream — Japan's most auspicious creature, symbol of celebration and good fortune. The recipe hasn't changed in 115 years. The queue still forms at 10am. The taiyaki still costs ¥180. The iron moulds are original.

Behind every taiyaki filling is 1,200 years of wagashi — Japanese confectionery born in imperial court ceremonies and Buddhist temples, built on the philosophy that every sweet should express a specific moment in the natural year. Sakura in spring. Chestnut in autumn. Yuzu in winter. A wagashi shop that doesn't change with the seasons is not a wagashi shop.

This guide teaches you how to build both — the street-facing taiyaki window and the seasonal wagashi display case — and the tea service that connects them into Japan's most enduring dessert business model.
