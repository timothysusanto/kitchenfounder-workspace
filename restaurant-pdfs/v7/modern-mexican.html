The KitchenFounder Guide Bible

The philosophy, positioning, and non-negotiables that define a great modern Mexican dinner restaurant in New York City.

🎯 Concept DNA

Modern Mexican in NYC sits at the intersection of ancestral technique and New York ambition. Corn is sacred. Mole is theatre. Mezcal is storytelling. Every dish must have terroir — a story that your servers can tell with conviction.

💰 Financial Targets

Avg Spend: $70 per cover
Food Cost Target: 28–32%
Labor Target: 30–33%
Prime Cost Target: ≤ 62%
EBITDA Target: 12–18%

🌽 The Three Pillars

  • Corn: Heirloom masa, stone-ground in-house or sourced from Masienda
  • Chiles: 12+ varieties; dried, fresh, smoked, fermented
  • Fire: Wood-burning comal, charcoal grill, open flame finishing

✅ Non-Negotiables

  • Tableside guacamole in molcajete — every table
  • Mole negro made in-house, minimum 24h process
  • Mezcal list of 30+ expressions with server education
  • Seasonal chiles en nogada Sept–Nov only
  • Tortillas pressed to order, stone-ground masa

Kitchen Design — Comal Station + Wood Fire

🔥 Wood-Fire Station

  • Wood-burning hearth: primary for meats, chiles toreados, carne asada
  • Wood types: mesquite (beef), apple (duck/chicken), avocado (vegetables)
  • Fuel budget: $200–300/week; display wood stack in dining room for theatre
  • Install: floor drain, spark arrestor, Class II hood; NYC DOH clearance required
  • Position: open kitchen preferred — guests see the fire (instagrammable)

🫔 Comal Station

  • Two 20-inch cast iron comals minimum; one dedicated to tortillas
  • Gas comal for high-volume tortilla press-to-order service
  • Masa storage: hold at 50°F; replace after 4h for best texture
  • Tortilla output: 60–80 tortillas/hour minimum (dinner volume)
  • Elote, chiles toreados, quesabirria all use comal — sequence critical

🧊 Cold Station

Ceviche, aguachile, guacamole prep. 38°F work surface. Pre-chilled plates. Avocado holds (lime-treated, vacuum). No heat sources nearby.

🫙 Mole/Braise Station

8-quart tilting kettle for mole batches. Separate burner for birria/barbacoa holds. Immersion blender bay. Needs ventilation above.

🍦 Dessert Station

Churro fryer (375°F dedicated). Walk-in shelf for tres leches, cheesecake. Ice cream chest freezer (-10°F). Plating counter with garnish rail.

🌿 Mole Negro Story Script

Server Script — Mole Negro Presentation (with Story Card)

"This is our mole negro — our most important dish. The story begins 400 years ago in the convent of Santa Catalina de Siena in Oaxaca, where nuns were preparing a feast for the viceroy. Running out of time, they combined what they had: five types of chiles, dark chocolate, spices, and burnt tortilla. What they created is considered Mexico's greatest sauce. [Places card.] This mole takes us 24 hours to make. We think you'll taste every hour of it."

🥃 Mezcal Education Script

Server Script — Mezcal Introduction

"Can I interest you in exploring our mezcal list? Unlike tequila, which can only be made from blue agave, mezcal can be made from over 50 varieties of agave plant — and we carry expressions from seven of them. [Points to menu section.] My personal recommendation tonight would be our Del Maguey Espadín — it's smoky but not overwhelming, with notes of dried fruit and leather. Or if you're feeling adventurous, our tobalá is wild-harvested; it takes 15 years for that plant to mature — you're tasting 15 years of Oaxacan highland sun in one sip."

🌶️ Chiles en Nogada Seasonal Reveal Script (Sep–Nov)

Server Script — Chiles en Nogada Seasonal Announce

"I have wonderful news — our chiles en nogada are on the menu tonight. This dish is only available for six weeks a year, when fresh walnuts come into season in the Mexican highlands. The dish represents the Mexican flag: the green of parsley, the white of the walnut cream nogada, and the red of fresh pomegranate. It was first served on September 16th, 1821 — Mexican Independence Day — to honor Agustín de Iturbide. We only prepare twelve portions per evening. Shall I put one in for you?"
