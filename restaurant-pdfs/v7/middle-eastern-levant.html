The Middle Eastern Levant Playbook

Concept DNA

Levant hospitality is built on meze culture — abundance, sharing, and theatre. Every table tells a story of the ancient Fertile Crescent. The wood-fired taboon oven is your hero; the mezze spread your opening act.

  • Cuisine: Lebanese · Syrian · Palestinian · Jordanian
  • Regional: Moroccan · Egyptian · Yemeni · Israeli
  • Format: Dinner, 6 nights/week
  • Seats: 75 (dining room + bar)
  • Avg spend: $65 per cover
  • Service style: Sharing plates, abundance table

Brand Pillars

  • 🔥 Fire — taboon oven as centrepiece theatre
  • 🫙 Mezze — abundance as welcome ritual
  • ☕ Coffee — Arabic ceremony closes every meal
  • 🐑 The Whole Animal — shoulder carved tableside
  • 🌿 Herb — fresh mint, parsley, sumac on everything
  • 🎶 Sound — ambient oud, no intrusion

  1. Cook soaked chickpeas in unsalted water 90 min until very soft; reserve cooking liquid.
  2. Process warm chickpeas in high-speed blender 5 min until ultra-smooth; add tahini, lemon, garlic.
  3. Stream in ice water gradually; blend additional 3 min. Season with salt.

Crispy Falafel
Fresh herb, dried chickpea only — not canned — served with tahini and pickled turnip
VEGANHot · 165°F internalFC 14%
IngredientQty (serves 4 / 8 pcs)Unit CostTotal Cost
Dried chickpeas (soaked 24h, NOT cooked)200g raw$0.90/lb$0.40
Fresh parsley40g$1.50/bunch$0.40

Garlic + white onion4 cloves + 60g$0.25$0.25
Cumin + coriander seed, freshly ground5g each$0.10$0.10

MENU PRICE$15.00
  1. Drain soaked chickpeas thoroughly; pulse in food processor with herbs, garlic, onion, spices to coarse crumb — NOT a paste. Over-processing = dense falafel.
  2. Fold in baking soda; rest mixture 30 min refrigerated. Mix should hold when squeezed.
  3. Form into 40g balls using falafel press or ice cream scoop. Chill on tray.

Mansaf — Jordanian Feast Dish
Lamb in jameed (fermented yogurt) sauce · saffron rice, flatbread, almonds
170°F internalFC 29%
IngredientQty (serves 2)Unit CostTotal Cost
Lamb leg pieces, bone-in350g$9.00/lb$6.95
Jameed (dried fermented sheep yogurt) or sub: full-fat Greek yogurt + tamarind200g$3.00$3.00
Basmati rice (saffron)180g dry$0.72$0.72
Shrak flatbread (paper-thin)2 sheets$0.50/sheet$1.00
Blanched almonds, pine nuts (garnish)30g each$1.50$1.50
Parsley (chopped, garnish)10g$0.10$0.10
TOTAL FOOD COST$13.27
MENU PRICE$38.00
  1. Simmer lamb with onion, spices 1.5 hours until tender. Reserve lamb stock.
  2. Reconstitute jameed in warm water; blend smooth. Add to lamb stock with lamb pieces; simmer 20 min.
  3. Cook saffron rice separately. Layer shrak flatbread on large platter — it becomes soft from sauce.

Kitchen Design & Service Scripts

🔥 Kitchen Design Blueprint

Hero Station
Wood-Fired Taboon Oven — Centrepiece

Positioned visible from dining room through open kitchen window or pass-through. This oven is marketing — guests should SEE the fire. Dome oven, wood-fired, 800–900°F operating temp. Used for: flatbreads, roasted meats, whole vegetables, kibbeh.

Station Layout
  • Station 1 — Taboon Oven: Wood-fire management, flatbreads, roasted proteins
  • Station 2 — Mezze Cold Station: Hummus, baba ghanoush, labneh, fattoush, kibbeh nayeh — all plated cold/room temp
  • Station 3 — Hot Grill: Kafta, shawarma spit, lamb chops, grilled items
  • Station 4 — Braise/Sauce: Tagine, mansaf, maqluba, jameed sauce — all long-cook items
  • Station 5 — Pastry/Dessert: Knafeh press, baklava storage, qatayef station, plating
  • Station 6 — Beverage/Café: Dallah coffee preparation, jallab mixing, juice station
Mezze Cold Station Specs

Under-counter refrigeration at 38°F. Prep mise en place by 4:00 PM daily. Marble or granite surface for cold work — maintains temperature. Dedicated pita warming drawer adjacent.

📜 Service Script 1: Mezze Abundance Table

SERVER (arriving with full mezze spread):

"This is how we welcome guests at [Restaurant] — the way every Lebanese home welcomes visitors. Everything arrives together, everything is meant to be shared. The hummus is warm, made from scratch this afternoon. The baba ghanoush was roasted in our wood-fire oven just tonight. Pull the bread apart like this..."

[Server tears taboon bread and places pieces around the table, initiating the communal moment.]

ON DEPARTURE:

"In Levant tradition, leaving a little food means the host did their job — please don't worry about finishing everything. That's the point."

📜 Service Script 2: Whole Lamb Shoulder Tableside

SERVER (arriving with copper platter):

"Your lamb shoulder — this has been roasting since this morning, over eight hours. We're going to carve it right here. [Presents the whole shoulder.] You'll see it just pull apart... [carves dramatically with tongs] There. That's what eight hours of patience looks like."

"The rice underneath is saffron basmati — there's gold in there, literally. The fried onion and almonds on top are from a Jordanian mansaf tradition."

📜 Service Script 3: Arabic Coffee Ceremony

SERVER (arriving with dallah and finjan cups):

"Arabic coffee — this is how every meal ends in the Arab world. It's cardamom-scented, lightly roasted, poured hot. When your cup is full, I'll stop. When you've had enough, shake the cup like this [demonstrates] and I'll know."

"There's a belief that coffee shared is a promise of welcome return. We hope to see you again."

[Pour from dallah, held high — the height shows mastery and aerates the coffee. Top up every cup before leaving table.]
