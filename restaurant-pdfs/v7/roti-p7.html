5Kitchen Design — The Roti Shop Layout

Design philosophy: A roti shop kitchen is built around two non-negotiable stations: the tawa (flat cast iron griddle for roti production) and the stew station (multiple heavy pots with batch-cooked curry goat, pepper pot, stewed chicken, and oxtail held at service temperature). The tawa is the heart — everything passes through it. Speed is the point: a roti wrap assembled at a properly organised roti station should take under 90 seconds from pick-up to wrap to serve.

CRITICAL EQUIPMENT:
● Cast iron tawa (60cm minimum) — the non-negotiable tool; must be seasoned and very hot
● Overnight braise oven (runs 140–150°C unattended for oxtail + goat water + pepper pot)
● Heavy-duty charcoal grill (outdoor or ventilated — for jerk)
● Bain marie bank (6 slots minimum) — multiple stews held simultaneously
● Deep fryer (2-basket) — accra, pholourie, plantain, festival, doubles bara
● Blender (commercial) — callaloo, peanut punch, sea moss, green seasoning batches

🫓 Tawa Station — The Heart of the Roti Shop

  • Cast iron tawa heated minimum 20 minutes before service at medium-high
  • Roti dough: made fresh daily, portioned and rested; never refrigerated dough (kills the gluten structure)
  • Dhalpuri: filled, rolled, and cooked per order — maximum 90 seconds per side
  • Buss up shut: clapping the roti while hot is a 10-second technique; practice until second nature
  • Bara (for doubles): flattened to a thin disc and fried immediately in adjacent fryer
  • Never wash the tawa with soap — season with oil after each service
THROUGHPUT: A skilled tawa cook makes 1 roti every 3–4 minutes. At peak service, 2 cooks on the tawa produces 30 rotis per hour — 30 roti wraps per hour is the throughput target.

🥘 Stew Station — The Batch Engine

  • All stews batch-cooked: goat (3–4hrs), oxtail (3–4hrs), pepper pot (rolling batch), stewed chicken (45min)
  • Bain marie holds all proteins at 75°C through service — never reheated per order in a pan
  • Channa: batch-made daily. Must be thick, not soupy. Consistency check before every service
  • Pepper pot: never fully emptied. Add new oxtail / beef daily to the rolling master pot
  • Quality test for every stew before service: can you break it with the back of a spoon? If not, it needs more time

🌶 Sauce & Chutney Station

  • Shadow beni (chadon beni) chutney: blended fresh every morning; maximum 2-day refrigerated shelf life
  • Pepper sauce: scotch bonnet, garlic, lime, mustard — blend weekly; develops better flavour after 3 days
  • Tamarind sauce: batch weekly; sweet-sour balance checked against a standard before each service
  • Mango sour: made morning of service from fresh green mango; never more than 4 hours old
  • Green seasoning: batch weekly (2L); the most important mise en place in the kitchen

🥃 Rum & Drinks Station

  • House ginger beer: rolling batch (new batch every 2–3 days); always taste before service
  • Sorrel concentrate: batch weekly; used for cocktails AND dessert (efficiency)
  • Rum flight boards: pre-set with sal de gusano — sorry, with lime wedge, tasting card, and small water glass
  • Sea moss: blend morning of service; serve within 24 hours
  • Peanut punch: blend to order — 60 seconds; do not pre-batch (separates)

6Service Scripts — Caribbean Hospitality

OPENING SCRIPT — Welcome to the Roti Shop
"Welcome! So this is a roti shop — you might know it from Trinidad or Guyana or you might be discovering it today. The heart of the menu is the roti wrap: your choice of a dhalpuri roti — that's a flatbread with spiced split peas ground into the dough — or a buss up shut, which is a flaky, layered paratha that you tear and dip. Both come with curried channa, potato, and your choice of filling. Can I start you with some doubles? Two fried flatbreads with curried chickpea. They're $8.99 and they're the reason people in Trinidad wake up early."
Why it works: Covers the key menu architecture immediately so guests aren't confused. The doubles recommendation moves them to the highest-margin, highest-story-value item first. "The reason people in Trinidad wake up early" is a confident, specific recommendation that creates immediate trust.
DOUBLES EDUCATION SCRIPT
"Doubles originated in Trinidad in 1936 — a man named Emamool Deen started selling them from a cart for one cent each. Today hundreds of thousands of doubles are sold every morning across Trinidad. They're the national breakfast. We make our bara fresh — that's the fried flatbread — with turmeric and cumin in the dough, and the curried channa is made every day with whole chickpeas. The chutneys are all house-made: there's a shadow beni chutney — that's a Caribbean herb, similar to coriander but more intense — and a scotch bonnet pepper sauce. How much heat do you want? 'Slight' means almost none, 'medium' is noticeable, 'extra' will make you sweat."
Why it works: The 1936 origin story + "hundreds of thousands daily" creates immediate cultural context. The heat level vocabulary ("slight," "medium," "extra") is authentically Trinidadian and makes guests feel included in the culture. Guests who learn the vocabulary use it when ordering — that's ownership and engagement.
PEPPER POT EDUCATION SCRIPT
"The pepper pot — I want to tell you about this one. It's Guyana's national dish. The key ingredient is cassareep, which is made from bitter cassava root — the raw root is actually toxic, but when you boil it down for hours, you get this thick, dark, complex liquid that is simultaneously a sauce, a preservative, and a colouring agent. The pot is never emptied — we add to it every day. Traditional Guyanese families have pepper pot pots that have been running for decades. The cassareep is why it's almost black. It's sweet and bitter at the same time. It tastes like nothing else. I'd strongly recommend it with a piece of bake for soaking."
Why it works: The cassareep story — toxic raw, complex cooked, ancient Amerindian technology — is scientifically fascinating. "The pot is never emptied" creates immediate intrigue and a sense of participating in something ancient. The "I'd strongly recommend it with bake" is a confident upsell that feels like genuine guidance, not a sales technique.
RUM FLIGHT SCRIPT — The Three Islands Education
"For drinks — can I tell you about our rum flight? Three islands, three rums, three completely different characters. We have Angostura 7 Year from Trinidad — light, tropical, slightly spiced. El Dorado 12 Year from Guyana — this one is made in wooden pot stills from the 1600s, which is why it's rich and chocolatey and unlike any rum most people have had. And Mount Gay XO from Barbados — the oldest rum brand in the world, established 1703. The flight is $24.99, comes with a tasting card. Most tables who try it end up ordering a bottle of the El Dorado. It's that good."
Why it works: The El Dorado wooden stills from the 1600s is the most compelling single fact in the rum world. "The oldest rum brand in the world" for Mount Gay is a verified credential. "Most tables who try it order a bottle of the El Dorado" is a social proof statement that creates aspiration and sets up a bottle sale.
