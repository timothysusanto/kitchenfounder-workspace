Avocado selection is everything: Hass avocados must be ripe but not overripe. Press gently — the skin should yield slightly with firm resistance underneath. Black-green skin, not jet black. Order avocados 2-3 days before needed and ripen at room temperature. Never refrigerate uncut avocados — cold kills flavour development. A single underripe avocado ruins the batch. 2
The molcajete technique: Authentic guacamole starts by grinding salt, serrano, onion, and cilantro to a rough paste in a molcajete (stone mortar) before adding avocado. This releases the essential oils from the aromatics and distributes them evenly through the guacamole. If you don't have a molcajete, mash the aromatics with a fork before adding avocado — the result is measurably different from simply mixing everything together. The stone mortar costs $25 and signals authenticity immediately.
3
Texture — chunky, not smooth: Mexican guacamole is chunky, not a smooth paste. Mash with a fork leaving visible pieces of avocado. Add lime juice last, taste for salt, adjust. The guacamole should taste bright and acidic from the lime — underseasoned guacamole tastes flat and fatty. If it tastes flat, add more lime before adding more salt. Make to order — guacamole oxidises within 15 minutes of cutting the avocado. Never pre-make and hold.

Grill with the husk on, then pull back: Leave the husk on during the first 10 minutes on the grill — this steams the corn inside while the exterior chars slightly. Oaxacan cheese melts like no other: Quesillo (Oaxacan string cheese) is the correct cheese for queso fundido — it melts to a smooth, stretchy, pulling consistency unlike cheddar or jack cheese which can break and turn greasy. 1
Lime-curing is not cooking — food safety critical: Mexican ceviche traditionally uses lime acid to denature (opacify) the shrimp protein — the shrimp turns from translucent to opaque white through acid contact alone. This is not the same as heat cooking and does NOT eliminate all pathogens. For restaurant service in the US, use pre-cooked (blanched) shrimp and then cure in lime juice for flavour only. This eliminates food safety risk while preserving authentic flavour. Do not serve raw-cured shrimp in a commercial kitchen without explicit health department approval.
2
Rinse red onion before adding: Raw red onion in ceviche can overpower all other flavours with harsh sulphur compounds. Dice finely, rinse under cold water for 30 seconds, drain completely before adding. This removes the harsh edge while retaining the onion flavour and colour. This single step separates a balanced ceviche from an onion-forward one.
3
Add avocado last: Avocado goes in at the very end, just before plating — not during the cure or mixing stage. Early addition causes the avocado to break down, turn grey, and create a mushy texture. The avocado should be firm, bright green, and freshly cut when it hits the bowl. Serve immediately after plating.
Chef's Tip: Ceviche de camaron is your most elevated entree and signals to guests that your kitchen takes seafood seriously. This product education creates a moment of authenticity that guests remember and repeat to others.

The guajillo base — batch and hold: Toast dried guajillo chillies in a dry pan until fragrant (30 seconds). Soak in hot water 20 minutes. The trompo is your entire brand identity: Al pastor is defined by the vertical rotating spit (trompo) — layers of marinated pork shoulder stacked with pineapple slices, rotating slowly over a gas flame, the exterior caramelising and crisping continuously while the interior stays moist. The taquero shaves thin curls of meat directly from the spinning spit with a long knife, catching them in a tortilla held in the other hand. This theatre is irreplaceable. The achiote marinade — 24 hours minimum: Blend achiote paste, soaked guajillo chillies, pineapple juice, white vinegar, garlic, oregano, cumin, and salt to a smooth paste. Train your taquero to execute this theatrically. Guests will film it every time.

Carnitas is a lard confit — not a braise: The defining technique of authentic Michoacan carnitas is cooking pork shoulder in its own fat (lard) at low temperature until completely tender, then raising the heat to crisp the exterior. Cut pork shoulder into 8cm chunks. Cover completely in lard in a wide heavy pot. Add orange halves, a cup of milk (the milk proteins help with browning), cinnamon stick, bay leaves, salt. Cook at 150°C / 302°F for 2.5-3 hours until a skewer meets zero resistance. The milk sounds strange but it is traditional and produces superior browning.
2
The crisp finish — done to order: Carnitas from the batch should be roughly pulled and held warm. To order, place 150g in a hot cast iron pan or on the comal with a small amount of the cooking lard and press flat. Cook 2-3 minutes until the exterior is deeply golden and crispy on the contact side. Do not stir — you want a crust to form. This crisp-to-order technique is what produces the carnitas with both a crackling exterior and a tender, juicy interior. Carnitas that are only braised and not crisped taste steamed and flat.
3
Double tortilla is mandatory: Al pastor and carnitas tacos are always served on two small corn tortillas stacked together. One tortilla tears under the weight and moisture of the filling. Two tortillas provide structural integrity and the slight additional corn flavour from the double layer. This is not optional — it is the correct way to serve a street taco. Warm tortillas on a comal or dry pan until pliable and lightly charred. Never microwave tortillas for table service.
Chef's Tip: Carnitas is your best-seller for a reason — the combination of crispy exterior and fatty, tender pork interior with bright tomatillo salsa is one of the great flavour combinations in world cuisine. Batch on Tuesday and Friday. The lard from carnitas production is liquid gold — use it to fry your tortilla chips, cook your refried beans, and season your cast iron. Nothing is wasted in a carnitas kitchen.

Beef cheek is the correct cut: Traditional barbacoa uses the entire beef head cooked in an underground pit lined with maguey leaves. For a commercial kitchen, beef cheek is the closest and most practical equivalent — it is extremely high in collagen, becomes fork-tender after long braising, and has a rich, deep flavour that leaner cuts cannot match. 5Service Training Module

Mexican taqueria service philosophy: Your service team are cultural guides, not order takers. Every guest interaction is an opportunity to educate about Mexican food culture — the trompo, the consomme dip, the Tajin on the paleta. Guests who leave knowing more than they arrived become advocates. The taqueria that teaches is the taqueria that gets recommended.
// Table Welcome Script
"Welcome — I'm excited for you to eat here today. Can I start you with some house guacamole while you look at the menu? We make it fresh to order. And have you been here before, or is this your first time?"
Why: Guacamole offer first — before the menu is even opened — captures the impulse yes before analysis begins. The experience question calibrates the entire service interaction. First-timers need education; regulars need speed and their favourites acknowledged.
// Drinks Opening — Margarita Lead
"For drinks — our margaritas are made with fresh lime juice, no mixes. Classic or spicy jalapeno are our two most popular. We also have horchata and hibiscus water if you prefer non-alcoholic — both are made from scratch. And Modelo if you want to keep it simple."
Why: Lead with the best product (fresh lime margarita), name the two options to reduce decision paralysis, include non-alcoholic in the same breath. Never ask "alcoholic or non-alcoholic?" — it creates a binary that makes non-drinkers feel they need to justify themselves.
// Birria Education Script
"Our birria is our most talked-about dish — slow-braised beef in a guajillo and ancho chile consomme. The tacos are dipped in the red fat from the consomme before grilling, so they come out this deep red colour with a crispy outside. You dip them back into the consomme cup as you eat. It's genuinely unlike anything else on the menu."
Why: Birria described in this way creates desire before the order is placed. "Unlike anything else on the menu" is the most powerful phrase in restaurant selling — it signals uniqueness and makes the guest feel they would miss out by not ordering it. This script converts 70%+ of tables it is delivered to.
// Trompo / Al Pastor Education
"Our al pastor is made on a traditional vertical spit — the pork rotates slowly and we shave it directly onto your tortilla. The pineapple on top chars and drips down through the meat as it cooks. If you've never had proper al pastor from a trompo, this is the one to order."
Why: The trompo is visible theatre. Describing it verbally to guests who may not have noticed it directs their attention to your most powerful visual marketing asset. "If you've never had proper al pastor" is a respectful challenge — it implies the guest may have had inferior versions elsewhere, which is almost certainly true outside Mexico.
// Dessert Close Script
"For dessert — our churros are made to order, so they come out hot and crispy. Tres leches if you want something lighter — very moist, not too sweet. Or we have paletas, which is how you'd traditionally end a meal in Mexico — a frozen fruit pop, very refreshing after the spice. Which sounds right?"
Why: Three options with distinct descriptions allows the guest to self-select rather than feeling sold to. "Which sounds right?" is a soft close that assumes a yes to dessert and only asks which one. Tables that are asked "would you like dessert?" convert at 30%. Tables asked "which dessert sounds right?" convert at 55%.
// Return Driver — Text List Close
"Before you go — we do a different special taco every weekend. Last week was smoked brisket birria, this week is uni tostada. We send one text per week to let people know. If you want to be on the list, just leave your number with me — nothing else, just the weekly special."
Why: Naming specific past and upcoming specials creates concrete FOMO. "Nothing else, just the weekly special" removes the privacy objection before it forms. Collect at the peak of satisfaction — just after dessert, before the bill. This text list is your most valuable long-term marketing asset. 500 numbers on this list = predictable full restaurant every weekend.

8Customer Retention System

// The Taqueria Loyalty Architecture
The taqueria has an unusually high natural return rate because of variety within the format — guests rotate through al pastor one week, carnitas the next, birria the weekend after. The menu gives them a reason to return before they leave. Your job is to give them a specific reason: the weekend special. The SMS list turns casual guests into weekly regulars. Build it from day one.
// Weekly SMS Strategy
Every Thursday at 4pm: "This weekend at [name]: wagyu birria Friday + Saturday only. Uni tostada Saturday lunch. Reply BOOK for a table or walk in."
Why: Thursday at 4pm is the highest-converting time for restaurant SMS — guests are finishing work and beginning to think about weekend plans. Two specific items, both limited availability, creates genuine decision pressure. The walk-in option removes reservation friction for spontaneous guests while the BOOK option serves planners. One message, two conversion paths.
