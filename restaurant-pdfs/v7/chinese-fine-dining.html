🥟 Har Gow — Crystal Shrimp Dumplings

STEAM 100°CFC 22%

Ingredients (serves 4 / 12 pcs)

ItemQtyUnit Cost
Tiger shrimp, peeled300g$4.80
Water chestnut, diced80g$0.45
Wheat starch150g$0.35
Tapioca starch50g$0.18
Sesame oil1 tsp$0.12
White pepperpinch$0.05
Salt¾ tsp$0.02
Total Cost$5.97

Method

  1. Rough-chop ⅔ shrimp; mince remaining ⅓ fine. Combine with water chestnut, sesame oil, white pepper, salt.
  2. Mix wheat starch + tapioca. Pour 200ml boiling water; stir rapidly then knead to smooth dough (rest 5 min).
  3. Roll dough thin (2mm). Cut 9cm circles. Place 15g filling off-centre.
  4. Pleat 7–9 folds on one side — classic har gow pinch. Seal tightly.
  5. Steam in bamboo basket over boiling water, 6–7 min until translucent.
  6. Serve immediately with premium soy & chilli oil.
Chef Tip: Dough must be made fresh each service — it cracks if made ahead. Keep covered with damp cloth. Pleating mastery is the mark of a skilled dim sum chef.
Allergens: Shellfish, Gluten, Sesame

🦆 Peking Duck Pancakes — Tableside Carved

ROAST 220°CFC 28%

Ingredients (1 whole duck / 4–6pp)

ItemQtyUnit Cost
Whole duck (Long Island)2.5kg$28.00
Maltose syrup3 tbsp$0.90
Rice vinegar2 tbsp$0.20
Mandarin pancakes (pre-made)16 pcs$3.20
Hoisin sauce, premium60ml$0.80
Spring onion, julienne3 stalks$0.25
Cucumber, julienne½ pc$0.40
Total Cost$33.75

Method

  1. Air-dry duck 24–48hr in walk-in on rack — skin must be completely dry.
  2. Blanch duck with boiling water 3× to tighten skin. Rub maltose + vinegar mixture all over.
  3. Hang or rack-roast at 220°C for 45–55 min until skin is lacquered mahogany.
  4. Rest 10 min. Carry whole to tableside on carving trolley.
  5. Carve: first remove crispy skin in uniform slices (serve first course). Then slice breast & leg meat.
  6. Guest assembles: pancake + hoisin + duck + spring onion + cucumber.
Chef Tip: The skin is the star. If it's not crispy and lacquered, the dish fails. 48hr air-dry is non-negotiable. Carve tableside — the theatre is half the dish's value.
Allergens: Gluten, Soy, Sesame

🍜 Hot & Sour Soup — Sichuan Style

SERVE 85°CFC 18%

Ingredients (serves 4)

ItemQtyUnit Cost
Chicken stock, rich1.2L$1.80
Silken tofu200g$0.90
Wood ear mushrooms (dried)30g$0.65
Bamboo shoots, julienne80g$0.55
Pork loin, julienne100g$1.20
Chinkiang black vinegar4 tbsp$0.40
White pepper (generous)1½ tsp$0.15
Cornstarch slurry3 tbsp$0.08
Eggs, beaten2 pcs$0.60
Total Cost$6.33

Method

  1. Soak wood ear mushrooms 30 min; slice thin. Blanch bamboo shoots.
  2. Bring stock to boil. Add pork, mushrooms, bamboo shoots, tofu.
  3. Season with soy, vinegar, white pepper. Taste — should be hot AND sour.
  4. Add cornstarch slurry; stir to thicken to light coating consistency.
  5. Drizzle beaten egg in thin stream, stirring gently to create ribbons.
  6. Finish with sesame oil. Serve in individual bowls at 85°C minimum.
Chef Tip: Adjust vinegar and white pepper at service — these fade with time. The soup should make you sweat slightly from the pepper. Chinkiang vinegar only — no substitutes.
Allergens: Egg, Soy, Gluten

🌯 Spring Rolls — Crispy Pork & Mushroom

FRY 175°CFC 20%

Ingredients (serves 4 / 8 rolls)

ItemQtyUnit Cost
Spring roll wrappers (8")8 pcs$0.80
Ground pork200g$2.20
Shiitake mushrooms, sliced120g$1.80
Napa cabbage, shredded150g$0.40
Glass noodles (soaked)60g$0.30
Oyster sauce2 tbsp$0.25
Soy sauce1 tbsp$0.08
Sesame oil1 tsp$0.10
Total Cost$5.93

Method

  1. Stir-fry pork in smoking wok until cooked. Add mushrooms, cabbage — cook until wilted and dry.
  2. Add glass noodles, oyster sauce, soy. Season. Cool completely before rolling — moisture = exploding rolls.
  3. Place 2 tbsp filling near wrapper edge. Fold sides, roll tight. Seal with flour paste.
  4. Double-fry method: first fry at 160°C (set the wrapper), drain, second fry at 175°C (crisp to order).
  5. Drain on rack. Serve upright with sweet chilli dip and XO sauce on the side.
Chef Tip: Double-fry is the secret to restaurant-quality spring rolls. First fry can be done in prep; second fry at service. Never serve a soggy roll — it's a signal the kitchen is sloppy.
Allergens: Gluten, Soy, Sesame, Egg (wrapper)

🍖 Char Siu — Cantonese BBQ Pork

ROAST 200°CFC 24%

Ingredients (serves 4)

ItemQtyUnit Cost
Pork shoulder, strips600g$6.60
Hoisin sauce3 tbsp$0.40
Honey2 tbsp$0.35
Shaoxing wine2 tbsp$0.20
Soy sauce, dark1 tbsp$0.10
Five-spice powder1 tsp$0.08
Red fermented tofu1 cube$0.30
Garlic, minced3 cloves$0.15
Total Cost$8.18

Method

  1. Combine all marinade ingredients. Mix well until tofu is fully incorporated.
  2. Marinate pork minimum 24hr (48hr preferred) in walk-in.
  3. Hang pork on hooks or rack in oven at 200°C. Roast 25 min.
  4. Baste with honey glaze, turn heat to 220°C — roast 10 min for lacquered finish.
  5. Rest 5 min. Slice against grain at 5mm — slightly caramelised edges, pink centre.
  6. Serve on plate with mustard, plum sauce, cucumber.
Chef Tip: Red fermented tofu (nam yu) gives char siu its signature colour and depth — don't omit. The pork should be slightly charred at edges but moist within. Hanging roast is better than tray roast for even caramelisation.
Allergens: Soy, Gluten, Sesame

🥟 Siu Mai — Open-Top Pork & Shrimp Dumplings

STEAM 100°CFC 21%

Ingredients (serves 4 / 12 pcs)

ItemQtyUnit Cost
Ground pork, fatty250g$2.75
Tiger shrimp, rough-chopped150g$2.40
Siu mai wrappers (round)12 pcs$0.60
Water chestnut, diced60g$0.35
Oyster sauce1 tbsp$0.15
Sesame oil1 tsp$0.10
Shaoxing wine1 tbsp$0.10
Tobiko / fish roe (garnish)1 tbsp$0.85
Total Cost$7.30

Method

  1. Mix pork, shrimp, water chestnut in bowl. Add oyster sauce, sesame oil, Shaoxing wine, white pepper.
  2. Stir filling vigorously in one direction until sticky and elastic (protein development).
  3. Place wrapper in palm. Add 20g filling. Shape wrapper up around filling, leaving top open.
  4. Squeeze gently at waist to create hourglass. Top with single tobiko dot.
  5. Steam 8–9 min until pork is fully cooked (68°C internal). Serve in bamboo basket.
Chef Tip: Stirring in one direction builds the paste-like texture that holds shape. Under-mixed filling = crumbly, falling-apart siu mai. Quality control: every piece should be identical weight.
Allergens: Shellfish, Soy, Gluten, Sesame, Fish (tobiko)

🦞 Steamed Lobster — Ginger Scallion & XO

STEAM 100°CFC 32%

Ingredients (serves 2)

ItemQtyUnit Cost

Spring onion4 stalks$0.30
XO sauce (house-made)2 tbsp$0.90
Premium soy sauce2 tbsp$0.20
Neutral oil (hot pour)3 tbsp$0.15
Total Cost$29.95

Method

  1. Dispatch lobster humanely. Split, clean. Lay on steaming plate cut-side up.
  2. Top with ginger julienne. Steam over high heat 8–10 min until just opaque.
  3. Layer spring onion on top. Heat oil to smoking in wok.

🦆 Cantonese Roast Duck — Five Spice Glaze

ROAST 200°CFC 26%

Ingredients (serves 3–4)

ItemQtyUnit Cost
Whole duck2.2kg$24.00
Five-spice powder1 tbsp$0.12
Dark soy sauce3 tbsp$0.25
Shaoxing wine3 tbsp$0.28
Rock sugar30g$0.20
Star anise3 pcs$0.10
Ginger, sliced30g$0.20
Total Cost$25.15

Method

  1. Combine marinade ingredients. Stuff cavity with aromatics; seal cavity with skewer.
  2. Pour marinade into cavity; rotate to coat interior. Marinate 12hr refrigerated.
  3. Air-dry 8hr on rack. Blanch skin with boiling water to tighten.
  4. Roast at 200°C, 50–60 min, rotating halfway. Baste every 20 min.
  5. Rest 10 min. Chop into pieces (Chinese style) — back through breast. Serve with plum sauce.
Chef Tip: Cantonese duck has rich, spiced interior flavour vs Peking duck's crispy skin focus. Both have place on menu — position them differently. Serve with pickled ginger to cut richness.
Allergens: Soy, Gluten

Ginger, fine julienne30g$0.25
Spring onion, julienne3 stalks$0.20
Coriander leavessmall bunch$0.25
Vegetable oil (hot)3 tbsp$0.15
Total Cost$19.15

Method

  1. Score fish 3× each side. Place on steaming plate with ginger inside cavity and on top.
  2. Steam over vigorously boiling water, 8–10 min. Fish should be just set at thickest point.
  3. Drain and discard liquid (removes fishy taste). Top with fresh ginger, spring onion, coriander.

Method

Method

🌶️ Mapo Tofu — Tableside Sizzle Service

🔥 SICHUANSERVE 90°CFC 19%

Ingredients (serves 2)

ItemQtyUnit Cost
Silken tofu400g$1.80
Ground pork100g$1.10
Doubanjiang (chilli bean paste)2 tbsp$0.40
Black bean paste1 tbsp$0.20
Sichuan peppercorn oil1 tbsp$0.35
Chicken stock200ml$0.40
Cornstarch slurry2 tsp$0.05
Total Cost$4.30

Method

  1. Fry doubanjiang in oil 2 min until oil turns deep red. Add black bean paste.
  2. Add pork; fry until cooked. Add stock; bring to simmer.
  3. Gently add cubed tofu — do not stir hard (breaks cubes). Simmer 4 min.
  4. Thicken with cornstarch slurry — 3 additions, gentle stir each time.
  5. Transfer to sizzling cast iron. Drizzle Sichuan peppercorn oil at table. Finish with spring onion.
Signature Moment: Server brings sizzling clay pot tableside. The hum of the peppercorn oil hitting hot tofu = theatre. Must arrive sizzling. Warn guests: extremely hot. Pairs with rice.

Sugar1 tsp$0.02

Method

  1. Marinate chicken in soy, Shaoxing, cornstarch 20 min.
  2. Fry dried chillis and peppercorns in hot oil until fragrant — 30 sec (watch it).

Service Script — Peking Duck Tableside Carve

🦆 Whole Peking Duck — Tableside Carving (Server + Chef Script)

Server — bringing duck to table:
"Your Peking duck — roasted in our kitchen for 50 minutes over hardwood. Our chef will carve tableside, beginning with the skin."
[Present whole duck to table for inspection. Allow guests to appreciate the lacquered skin before carving begins.]
Chef — carving (narrating):
"The first course is the skin alone — just hoisin and a sliver of scallion. This is the traditional way. The meat follows as a second course."
[Carve skin in uniform pieces, 3–4cm. Plate on separate dish. Continue with breast and leg meat.]
Server — assembling pancake:
"Allow me to show you the traditional wrap — hoisin first, then duck, then cucumber and scallion. Roll from the bottom, and enjoy."
  1. Carry whole duck to table on carving board or trolley — guest sees the full bird
  2. Narrate briefly; carve skin course first (pure skin slices served with hoisin)
  3. Carve breast meat in clean slices; plate alongside pancakes and condiments
  4. Assemble first pancake tableside as demonstration; encourage guest participation
  5. Return to kitchen with carcass — bones become duck broth for next service

🔥 Wok Station Requirements

  • Minimum 4× wok burners at 80,000+ BTU
  • Commercial exhaust hood (high CFM)
  • Water ring on each wok for cooling/cleaning
  • Carbon steel woks only — no non-stick
  • Wok spatula + ladle per station
  • Mise en place: sauces in squeeze bottles
  • Cornstarch slurry always pre-made

🥟 Dim Sum Station Setup

  • Large floured marble/granite bench
  • Bamboo steamers stacked + labeled
  • Har gow dough: made fresh, kept moist
  • Siu mai mix: stirred in one direction
  • Portion scale: every piece = same weight
  • Dim sum chef start time: 9:00 AM
  • Trolley stocked by 11:00 AM for lunch

Customer Retention System

🫖 First Visit Ritual

  • Tea ceremony from minute 1 — sets tone
  • Complimentary XO sauce pot at table
  • Chef visit to special occasion tables
  • House-made fortune cookie + message
  • Departing gift: single mooncake (seasonal)

⭐ Loyalty Programme

  • Dragon Pass: 5 visits = 1 free duck
