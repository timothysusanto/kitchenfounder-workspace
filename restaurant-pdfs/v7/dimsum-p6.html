8Customer Retention System

Dim sum teahouses build the deepest community loyalty of any restaurant concept. The yum cha ritual is a weekly family gathering — once a family adopts a teahouse as "their place," they return every Sunday, bring extended family, celebrate birthdays, host New Year dinners. Building that loyalty requires consistency above all things: consistent har gow quality, consistent tea temperature, consistent service warmth. One bad visit breaks the bond. Ten consecutive perfect visits cement it for years.
// WeChat / SMS Template — Post-Visit
"Dear [Name], thank you for joining us for yum cha. We hope the har gow was everything you expected. Chinese New Year falls on [date] this year — our special menu is already filling up. Priority booking for returning guests opens one week early. Would you like us to reserve your table? 茶飯 [Restaurant Name]"
The "priority booking for returning guests" creates exclusivity and drives re-engagement. Chinese New Year is the highest-revenue single event of the year for any Chinese restaurant in the US — early booking drive maximises captures. The 茶飯 (tea and rice) closing signature is a Cantonese term of warmth equivalent to "bon appétit" — culturally resonant for the Cantonese diaspora and educational for non-Cantonese guests.
// Chinese New Year Feast Programme
SMS to all guests (first week of January): "新年快樂 Happy New Year 🧧 Chinese New Year falls on [date]. We're hosting a traditional Reunion Dinner on New Year's Eve — whole steamed fish (nian nian you yu — abundance every year), longevity noodles, turnip cake for prosperity, tang yuan for family togetherness. $88/person, table of 8. Limited reservations. First come first served. Reply YES to be added to the priority list."
The specific food symbolism (fish = abundance, noodles = longevity, turnip cake = prosperity, tang yuan = family togetherness) turns the reservation invitation into a cultural education. The $88/person table-of-8 format ($704/table) is your highest-revenue single booking of the year. The fortune symbolism in the number 88 (double prosperity in Chinese culture) is intentional and noticed.

Pu-erh, jasmine, or Dragon Well — choose your tea. Char siu just came out of the roasting oven. The smell alone is worth the visit. The timing details (har gow from first table, egg tarts at 11am, roasting oven aroma) make the Sunday visit feel like a live event rather than just a restaurant opening. The tea choice gives the reader a micro-decision that increases mental commitment to attending. Posting Saturday evening captures the audience planning their Sunday morning.
