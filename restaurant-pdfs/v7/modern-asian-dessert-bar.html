Chef's Philosophy · The Instagram Dessert Revolution02
A Letter from the Chef
I grew up between Taipei night markets and Sydney's Chinatown. My grandmother made tang yuan by hand every winter solstice. My uncle ran a shaved ice stall in Ximending. Food was always theatre — colour, texture, temperature, surprise.

When I trained in Hong Kong and later brought those techniques to Sydney's Haymarket precinct, I realised something: the new generation doesn't just eat dessert. They photograph it first. They share it before they taste it. And that changes everything about how you design a dish.

Every recipe in this guide is built around one principle: if it doesn't photograph beautifully, redesign it. But the taste must always justify the visual. Style without substance is just decoration.

This guide gives you both.
— Head Chef, Guide #43
The 5 Laws of Instagram Dessert
1. Height wins. Tall always photographs better than flat.
2. Contrast is king. Dark vs light, warm vs cold, smooth vs texture.
3. Steam = life. Warm elements make photos feel alive.
4. One hero colour. Choose your accent — pink, purple, matcha green.
5. Serve fast. Ice melts. Soufflé falls. Time your service to 90 seconds.
Restaurant Design Principles
✓ Bright, even lighting — no harsh shadows
✓ White or marble surfaces for dessert photography
✓ Open kitchen — let guests see the assembly
✓ Height-adjustable dessert stands at every table
✓ Dedicated "photo corner" with ring light + props
✓ Staff trained to pause service for guest photos

01
Honey Toast Tower
Japanese Milk Bread · Honey Butter · Ice Cream · Fresh Fruit
Ingredients
Japanese milk bread loaf 1 whole (400g)
Unsalted butter 80g
Raw honey 60ml
Vanilla bean ice cream 2 scoops (120g)
Fresh strawberries 80g, halved
Fresh mango 60g, diced
Whipped cream 40g
Icing sugar 5g, to dust
Method
1Score milk bread top in grid pattern without cutting base. Hollow interior leaving 1.5cm walls.
2Melt butter and honey together. Brush generously inside and outside bread including scored top.
3Toast in 180°C oven 12 minutes until golden and caramelised. Interior pieces scattered around base.
4Place on serving board. Stack interior bread pieces beside. Top with 2 scoops ice cream.
5Arrange fruit on and around ice cream. Pipe whipped cream. Dust with icing sugar. Serve immediately.
Chef Technique

Score the bread all the way through before hollowing — this gives you uniform cube pieces. The hollow must be deep enough to cradle the ice cream without tipping.

Fail-Proof Tip

Toast bread while plating everything else — assemble in under 60 seconds once out of oven. Ice cream melts fast. Have everything staged before bread goes in.

📸 Instagram Plating

Serve on a small wooden board. Ice cream must be a perfect round scoop sitting proud. Dust icing sugar in the last 5 seconds before serving — the white powder dissipates fast. Photograph from 45° angle to capture height.

Food cost: ~AUD $4.20 · Suggested price: $18–22

02
Matcha Kakigori
Fine Shaved Ice · Ceremonial Matcha · Red Bean · Mochi
Ingredients
Block ice (pure water frozen) 300g per serve
Ceremonial grade matcha 8g
Simple syrup 80ml
Sweetened red bean (tsubuan) 60g
Mochi pieces (bite size) 3 pieces
Condensed milk 15ml, to drizzle
Matcha powder 2g, to dust
Method
1Whisk matcha with 10ml warm water (not boiling — 70°C max) until smooth paste. Add simple syrup and whisk to combine. Chill.
2Shave ice block on Japanese kakigori machine to snow consistency. Pile high in chilled bowl.
3Pour matcha syrup evenly over ice in 3 layers as you build the pile.
4Top with red bean, mochi pieces. Drizzle condensed milk. Dust matcha. Serve immediately.
Chef Technique

Temperature of syrup is critical — it must be fridge cold when applied or it will melt the ice immediately. Shave ice in layers and add syrup between layers for even flavour throughout.

Fail-Proof Tip

Never use crushed ice — you need a kakigori machine for the correct snow texture. Regular blended ice is too coarse and melts too fast. If no machine, use Korean bingsu machine.

03
Strawberry Crepe Cake
20 Layers · Fresh Cream · Strawberry · Mille Crêpe Style
Ingredients — per cake (12 slices)
Plain flour 120g
Eggs 3 whole (150g)
Whole milk 400ml
Butter, melted 30g
Thickened cream 500ml
Icing sugar 50g
Vanilla bean paste 5ml
Fresh strawberries 300g, sliced
Method
1Blend flour, eggs, milk, butter until smooth. Rest 30 mins. Cook 20 thin crêpes in 20cm non-stick pan. Cool completely.
2Whip cream, icing sugar, vanilla to firm peaks. Do not over-whip.
3Layer: crêpe → thin cream → strawberry slices → repeat 20 times. Press gently.
4Refrigerate minimum 4 hours or overnight. Slice with hot knife per service.
Chef Technique

Even layer thickness is everything — use a palette knife and spread cream to within 5mm of edge. Too much cream and layers slide. The weight of 20 crêpes compresses slightly overnight — this is correct.

Fail-Proof Tip

Crêpes MUST be completely cold before assembly or cream softens and slides. Make crêpes the day before. Slice with a thin knife dipped in hot water and wiped dry between each cut.

04
Black Sesame Lava Cake
Warm Molten Centre · Sea Salt · Vanilla Ice Cream
Ingredients — per 4 cakes
Dark chocolate 70% 120g
Unsalted butter 100g
Black sesame paste 80g
Eggs 3 whole + 3 yolks
Caster sugar 80g
Plain flour 40g
Tasmanian sea salt flakes 2g
Vanilla bean ice cream 1 scoop per serve
Method
1Melt chocolate and butter together over bain marie. Stir in black sesame paste until smooth. Cool 5 mins.
2Whisk eggs, yolks and sugar until pale and slightly thickened — 3 minutes.
3Fold chocolate mixture into eggs. Sift in flour and fold gently until just combined.
4Butter and flour 4 ramekins. Fill to ¾. Refrigerate up to 24 hours.
5Bake 200°C for exactly 11 minutes. Edges set, centre soft. Rest 1 min. Turn out. Serve immediately with ice cream and sea salt.
Chef Technique

The black sesame paste creates a denser, nuttier lava than standard chocolate fondant. The fat content is higher so reduce butter by 10g if paste is very oily. Test one cake first to calibrate your oven timing precisely.

Fail-Proof Tip

Oven timing is everything — 11 mins is a guide, not a rule. Every oven varies. Do a test bake on day 1. At 10 mins check — press edge gently, it should feel set with a wobble in the very centre only.

📸 Instagram Plating

Turn out onto a dark slate or black plate — the contrast of the dark grey cake against dark background is visually striking. Cut open tableside for the guest to capture the lava flow. Flick sea salt flakes on top in the last second.

05
Taro Ball Soup
Purple Taro + Sweet Potato Balls · Coconut Soup · Crushed Ice Option
Ingredients — serves 4
Taro, cooked + mashed 200g
Sweet potato, cooked + mashed 200g
Tapioca flour 160g (80g per colour)
Caster sugar 40g
Coconut milk 400ml
Palm sugar 60g
Pandan leaves 3 leaves, tied
Sea salt pinch
Method
1Mix taro mash with half the tapioca flour and half the sugar to form smooth dough. Repeat with sweet potato. Add more flour if sticky.
2Roll into 2cm balls. Boil in salted water until they float + 2 minutes. Transfer to ice water.
3Simmer coconut milk, palm sugar, pandan leaves and salt until sugar dissolves. Remove pandan.
4Serve warm soup in bowl with balls. Or serve chilled over crushed ice for summer version.
Chef Technique

The ratio of starch to mash determines texture — too much tapioca and balls become gummy, too little and they fall apart. The correct ball should be QQ — Taiwanese slang for chewy with bounce. Test one before rolling full batch.

Fail-Proof Tip

Never add water to the dough — moisture comes only from the mash. If dough cracks when rolling, add 5ml warm water at a time. If too sticky, add tapioca flour 10g at a time. Balls can be frozen uncooked for 2 months.

06
Mango Pomelo Sago
Hong Kong Style · Fresh Mango · Grapefruit · Coconut Milk
Ingredients — serves 4
Ripe mango (Alphonso preferred) 400g
Small sago pearls 80g, cooked
Coconut milk 200ml
Evaporated milk 100ml
Sugar syrup 40ml
Pink grapefruit 1 whole, segmented
Fresh mango diced 80g, to garnish
Method
1Cook sago in boiling water 15 mins stirring constantly until translucent. Rinse under cold water. Set aside.
2Blend 300g mango with coconut milk, evaporated milk and sugar syrup until silky smooth.
3Fold cooked sago into mango base. Chill minimum 2 hours.
4Serve in glass or bowl. Top with diced fresh mango and grapefruit segments. Serve chilled.
Chef Technique

The balance of mango sweetness vs grapefruit bitterness is the dish. Taste as you go — if mango is very sweet, add more grapefruit. The pomelo (grapefruit) cuts through richness and makes this feel light despite the coconut base.

Fail-Proof Tip

Overcooked sago becomes one solid mass. Stir constantly during cooking. Rinse immediately in cold water when translucent — this stops the cooking and keeps pearls separate. Under-rinsed sago turns gluey.

Food cost: ~AUD $2.40 · Suggested price: $13–16

07
Korean Bingsu
Milk Ice · Seasonal Toppings · Condensed Milk · Shaved to Snow
Ingredients — per serve
Whole milk ice block 250g (frozen overnight)
Sweetened condensed milk 30ml
Red bean paste (pat) 60g
Mochi rice cake pieces 3 pieces
Fresh strawberries or mango 60g
Cereal flakes or rice puff 10g for crunch
Matcha or strawberry powder 2g to dust
Method
1Freeze whole milk in block moulds overnight. Do NOT use water ice — milk ice shaves to creamier snow.
2Shave milk ice on bingsu or kakigori machine to ultra-fine snow. Pile high in large bowl.
3Build toppings in distinct sections — red bean one side, fruit another, mochi on top.
4Drizzle condensed milk over entire bowl. Dust with flavoured powder. Add crunch element last. Serve immediately.
Chef Technique

Milk bingsu (uyu bingsu) is creamier and denser than water-based kakigori. The fat in milk creates a softer, almost ice-cream-like texture when shaved. Use full-fat whole milk for best results.

Fail-Proof Tip

Service window is 4 minutes maximum before it starts melting into mush. Pre-stage ALL toppings in prep bowls before shaving. Never shave ice and then prepare toppings — the order must be reversed.

Food cost: ~AUD $2.60 · Suggested price: $16–19

08
Raindrop Cake
Mizu Shingen Mochi · Roasted Soybean Powder · Black Sugar Syrup
Ingredients — per 4 cakes
Still mineral water 500ml
Agar agar powder 2g (not gelatine)
Sugar 10g
Roasted soybean powder (kinako) 20g
Black sugar (kuromitsu) syrup 40ml
Edible flowers (optional) 4 small flowers
Method
1Dissolve agar and sugar in mineral water over medium heat. Do not boil vigorously — gentle simmer only.
2Place edible flower in hemisphere mould base. Pour agar mixture carefully. Refrigerate 2 hours until set.
3Unmould gently — raindrop shape should hold. Serve on chilled plate immediately.
4Dust kinako powder beside cake. Pour kuromitsu in small pool. Serve with teaspoon to eat together.
Chef Technique

The beauty of this dish is the crystal clarity. Use the highest quality still mineral water available — tap water minerals create cloudiness. Agar must be fully dissolved before pouring or you get lumps in the set.

Fail-Proof Tip

This dish has a very short service life — within 30 minutes at room temperature it begins to weep. Keep refrigerated until the moment of service. Never prep more than 2 hours ahead.

Food cost: ~AUD $1.20 · Suggested price: $12–15

09
Japanese Soufflé Pancakes
Ultra Fluffy · Maple + Butter · Seasonal Fruit · Served Jiggly
Ingredients — per 2 pancakes
Egg yolks 2
Egg whites 3
Caster sugar 40g
Plain flour 40g
Milk 30ml
Baking powder 3g
Cream of tartar 1g
Butter + maple syrup to serve
Method
1Whisk yolks, milk, flour and baking powder to smooth batter.
2Whip whites with cream of tartar to soft peaks. Add sugar gradually to firm glossy peaks.
3Fold whites into yolk batter in 3 additions — keep as much air as possible.
4Grease ring moulds in non-stick pan on very low heat. Pipe or spoon batter to fill halfway. Cover and cook 5 mins. Add more batter. Cover 5 more mins. Flip gently. Cover 3 more mins.
5Serve immediately — they deflate within 3 minutes. Warn guest at ordering stage.
Chef Technique

The soufflé effect comes entirely from the meringue. Any trace of yolk in whites kills the foam — use a perfectly clean, dry bowl. Low heat is essential — high heat sets the outside before the inside has risen.

Fail-Proof Tip

These cannot be held. Take order → make → serve → guest photographs → guest eats. Staff must warn every table: "These deflate in 3 minutes — photograph quickly!" This becomes part of the theatre, not a problem.

Food cost: ~AUD $2.10 · Suggested price: $16–20

10
Crepe Roll
Thin Crêpe · Diplomat Cream · Fresh Mango or Strawberry
Ingredients — per 4 rolls
Crêpe batter (thin) 200ml (from Guide #03 recipe)
Thickened cream 200ml
Crème pâtissière 100g
Icing sugar 20g
Fresh mango, sliced thin 120g
Icing sugar to dust 5g
Method
1Cook thin crêpes in 22cm pan. Cool completely on wire rack.
2Make diplomat: fold whipped cream into crème pâtissière. Chill 30 mins.
3Lay crêpe flat. Spread thin diplomat layer. Place mango across bottom third. Roll tightly. Trim ends.
4Slice on diagonal into 3 pieces. Stand upright to show spiral. Dust icing sugar.
Chef Technique

The diagonal cut revealing the spiral interior is the money shot. Practice the cut angle — 45° shows the most cream and fruit. A sharp thin-bladed knife in one clean motion, no sawing.

Fail-Proof Tip

Roll and refrigerate assembled rolls for 30 mins before cutting — the cream firms up and holds the spiral cleanly. Cutting a warm freshly rolled crêpe squashes the cream out the ends.

11
Churros + 3 Dipping Sauces
Matcha · Dark Chocolate · Condensed Milk · Crispy Hot
Ingredients — serves 4
Water 250ml
Plain flour 150g
Butter 60g
Salt 3g
Eggs 2
Caster sugar + cinnamon 40g + 5g to coat
Matcha powder + white choc 10g + 100g
Dark chocolate 70% 100g + 80ml cream
Condensed milk 80ml
Method
1Boil water, butter, salt. Add flour all at once. Beat over heat until dough pulls from sides. Cool 5 mins.
2Beat in eggs one at a time until smooth shiny dough. Pipe through star tip into 170°C oil. Fry 3-4 mins until golden.
3Roll hot churros in cinnamon sugar immediately.
4Sauces: Matcha — melt white chocolate, stir in matcha powder. Chocolate — hot cream over dark choc, stir smooth. Condensed milk — serve direct from tin in warm ramekin.
Chef Technique

Choux pastry must be cooked until it leaves the sides of the pan — this drives off excess moisture which is critical for crispiness. Undercooked choux absorbs oil and goes soggy. The dough should be smooth, shiny, and just soft enough to pipe.

Fail-Proof Tip

Oil temperature must be 170–175°C throughout. Too hot and outside burns before inside cooks. Too cool and churros absorb oil. Use a thermometer. Fry in small batches to maintain temperature.

12
Hong Kong Egg Waffle Tower
Bubble Waffle · Ice Cream · Fresh Fruit · Sauce
Ingredients — per waffle
Plain flour 120g
Caster sugar 60g
Eggs 2
Evaporated milk 100ml
Vegetable oil 30ml
Baking powder 5g
Custard powder 10g
Vanilla extract 3ml
Method
1Whisk all ingredients to smooth batter. Rest 30 mins. Batter should be pourable but coat back of spoon.
2Heat bubble waffle iron. Brush generously with oil. Pour batter, close. Cook 4 mins until golden.
3Remove and immediately shape into cone while still pliable. Hold 30 seconds until set.
4Stand cone in holder. Add 2 scoops ice cream. Fruit on top. Sauce drizzle. Serve.
Chef Technique

Custard powder is the secret ingredient — it adds that characteristic egg waffle flavour and helps browning. Evaporated milk creates a richer, denser bubble than regular milk. The shaping must happen within 20 seconds of leaving the iron while still hot and pliable.

Fail-Proof Tip

Have your cone mould ready before you open the iron. Time is critical — a cooled waffle cracks when shaped. Practice the flip-and-shape motion until it's automatic. Pre-warm the mould so it doesn't drop the waffle temperature.

13 · Basque Asian Style
Matcha version: 500g cream cheese, 3 eggs, 150g sugar, 200ml cream, 15g matcha, 20g flour. Blend smooth. Bake 220°C 25 mins in lined tin. Centre must be jiggly. Cool completely — do not refrigerate until fully cool. The burnt top is intentional and essential.

Fail-proof: Every oven reads differently at 220°C. Check at 20 mins — burnt top with wobbling centre = correct. Remove immediately.
14 · Coconut Jelly Mango
Layers: Layer 1 — coconut milk (400ml) + agar (3g) + sugar (60g), pour 5mm layer into moulds, set. Layer 2 — fresh mango purée (200ml) + agar (2g), pour when layer 1 is set. Repeat 3 times alternating. Chill 4 hours. Turn out and slice.

Fail-proof: Each layer must be semi-set (tacky to touch) before adding next. Too soon and layers bleed into each other. Too late and they don't bond and split when unmoulded.
15 · Red Bean Mochi Sundae
Vanilla soft serve base (or ice cream). Top with sweetened red bean paste (60g). Add 3 mochi pieces. Dust kinako (roasted soybean powder). Drizzle black sugar syrup (kuromitsu). Serve in glass bowl to show layers.

Fail-proof: Soft serve temperature is critical — too soft and it slumps, too hard and it doesn't scoop cleanly. -6°C is ideal serving temperature for soft serve.
16 · Dango Skewers
Dough: 100g shiratamako (glutinous rice flour) + 80g tofu (silken) — knead to smooth dough. Roll 3cm balls, boil until floating + 2 mins. Thread 3 on bamboo skewer. Grill until slightly charred. Coat with mitarashi sauce (soy + mirin + sugar + starch thickened).

Fail-proof: Tofu replaces water in this recipe — it creates a softer dough that stays soft even after chilling. Water-based dango hardens quickly.

Service Training Module 05 · Staff Scripting & Instagram Theatre
Core Philosophy
In a Modern Asian Dessert Bar, Instagram theatre is not optional — it is part of the service. Every staff member is a content director as much as a server. Your guests are creating memories they will share publicly. The moment the dessert hits the table, the performance begins. A soufflé pancake that jiggles and a shaved ice tower that glows under warm light are not accidents — they are choreography. Train your team to direct the shot, extend the moment, and make every guest feel like they are the star of their own video.
"We don't just serve dessert. We create the memory of dessert."
Script 01 · Welcome & Menu Navigation
Use when seating a new table or when guests look uncertain about the menu.
"Welcome! Our menu is a tour through Asia's best sweet moments. A few highlights — if you've never had soufflé pancakes, they're ultra-fluffy and jiggly, and they need to be photographed and eaten within 3 minutes of arriving. Shaved ice is our most Instagrammable item. If you want something to share, our Dessert Tower is the one everyone talks about. Can I walk you through anything?"
Why: Many guests are unfamiliar with Asian dessert formats. Pre-empting questions builds confidence, reduces order hesitation, and plants seeds for high-ticket items (towers, sharing plates). The soufflé pancake mention creates urgency and primes them for the photography script later.
Script 02 · The Soufflé Pancake Warning
Use at the moment soufflé pancakes are placed on the table.
"Here are your soufflé pancakes — you have about 3 minutes before they start to deflate, so photograph them now while they're at full jiggle! [Pause — let them get their phones] Would you like me to hold them up so you can get the perfect shot from the front? They taste best eaten immediately. Enjoy!"
Why: The countdown creates genuine urgency — this is not a gimmick, it is true. Soufflé pancakes collapse. By naming it theatrically, you turn a food science fact into a memorable experience. Offering to hold the plate gives the guest a perfectly framed shot and almost guarantees a social media post tagging the restaurant.
Script 03 · Boba Customisation Upsell
Use when guests order any boba drink.
"For your boba — we customise everything. Sweetness: 0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, or 100%. Ice level: none, less, regular, or extra. And for toppings beyond the standard pearls, would you like to add lychee jelly, coconut jelly, or pudding for $1.50? The lychee jelly in the Lychee Pandan Tea is really popular right now."
Why: Customisation is a core part of boba culture — guests expect it and appreciate being offered it. Naming the popular option ("lychee jelly is really popular right now") reduces decision fatigue and drives the $1.50 add-on which is 95%+ gross margin. Done consistently, this adds $2–4 per table across a service.
Script 04 · Sharing Platter Recommendation
Use when 2 or more guests are ordering individually.
"Before you decide — for two people, our Asian Dessert Platter actually works out much better value. It's $38–45 and you get six items including a half-serve kakigori, honey toast, crepe rolls, mochi, and fresh fruit on a tiered slate board. It arrives with a sparkler and it's honestly the most photographed thing on our menu. Shall I put that in instead?"
Why: Sharing plates have better food cost ratios and higher average spend per head than individual desserts. The sparkler and slate board mention creates aspirational imagery. "Most photographed" is social proof that works — guests want to order what others show off. This one script, used 3x per service, can add $80+ to nightly revenue.
Script 05 · Social Media Capture & Loyalty Invitation
Use when clearing dessert plates or at payment — especially if you noticed the guest photographing their food.
"Did you get some good photos? We'd love to be tagged — we're @[handle] on Instagram. If you tag us, we'll put you in our weekly feature and you'll get 10% off your next visit. Also, if you grabbed a stamp card on the way in, 10 desserts gets you a free boba — we'll stamp it now if you have it. We'd love to see you back!"
Why — UGC: User-generated content on Instagram is your cheapest and most effective advertising. A single post from a micro-influencer (2,000–20,000 followers) in Haymarket reaches exactly your target demographic for zero cost. The 10% discount is a small price for organic reach. Feature them on your account — guests love being noticed, and it creates a community loop.
Why — Loyalty: The stamp card closes the loop on retention. Research shows guests who visit 3+ times become regular advocates. The free boba (cost ~$1.50) after 10 desserts (~$160–180 spend) is a 0.9% loyalty cost — one of the highest-ROI tools available. Digital version via QR code reduces card loss and enables data collection.

⚠ Critical Operational Rule — Soufflé Pancakes
One person CANNOT make soufflé pancakes AND perform other kitchen tasks during service. The meringue folding, griddle monitoring, and 8–10 minute cook time require undivided attention. If the Dessert Cook steps away to plate a kakigori or assist with honey toast, the pancakes will collapse or burn. During peak service (Friday–Sunday PM), the Dessert Cook is pancakes only. Schedule a floater or cross-train the Head Chef to cover if the Dessert Cook goes on break.

Weekly Prep Schedule Module 07 · Mise en Place & Production Planning
Planning Note: This schedule assumes a Wed–Mon operation (closed Tuesday for deep clean and supplier deliveries). Adjust day references to your trading week. Items marked ⚑ are time-critical — failure to prep on schedule will result in a menu 86 situation.
Task Frequency Time Req. Notes & Critical Info
⚑ Boba Pearl Cooking 2× Daily (AM & PM) 45 min/batch Cook 500g–800g dried tapioca pearls per batch. Active time 30 min boil + 15 min syrup steep. Discard after 4 hours — do not serve hard or sticky pearls. Brown sugar pearls need extra syrup coating after cooking.
⚑ Shaved Ice / Bingsu Block Freezing Daily (24-hr advance) 5 min prep Mix milk, sugar, and flavouring (if Korean bingsu style). Pour into block moulds each evening for next day. Blocks must be fully frozen — minimum 24 hours in chest freezer. Check 4 blocks frozen each morning. Matcha and strawberry milk flavours should be rotated weekly.
Crepe Batter Daily (AM) 15 min + 60 min rest Fresh batter every morning. Mix: 4 eggs, 250g flour, 500ml full-cream milk, 60g melted butter, pinch salt, 1 tsp vanilla. Rest at room temp 1 hour minimum. Do not substitute with previous day's batter — texture degrades overnight. Makes approx. 25–30 crepes.
Taro Paste Batch 3× per week (Mon, Wed, Fri) 40 min Steam 1kg taro until tender. Blend with coconut cream (100ml), pandan leaf (2 leaves steeped), sugar (80g). Pass through fine sieve. Store in sealed container max 3 days refrigerated. Used in Taro Boba Latte, Taro Ball Soup, and taro dessert items. Yield: approx. 800g useable paste.
Black Sesame Paste 2× per week (Tue, Fri) 20 min Toast 300g black sesame seeds (medium heat, 8 min, stirring constantly). Blend with 80ml sesame oil, 60g sugar, 40ml water until smooth paste forms. Store refrigerated up to 5 days. Used in Black Sesame Lava Cake filling and Black Sesame Dango glaze. Rich, nutty depth — consistency must be spreadable, not oily.
Matcha Syrup Weekly (Monday) 15 min Whisk 30g ceremonial-grade matcha with 100ml hot water (not boiling — 80°C). Add to simple syrup (200g sugar dissolved in 200ml water). Cool and bottle. Store refrigerated up to 10 days. Used in Matcha Kakigori syrup, Matcha Frappe base, Hojicha alternative. Keeps colour vibrant — do not expose to sunlight after making.
Brown Sugar Syrup 2× per week 20 min Simmer 300g Taiwanese black sugar (or dark muscovado) with 150ml water on low 15 min until thick, glossy, caramel-flavoured. Cool. The signature tiger-stripe pattern on cups comes from drizzling this on the inside of the cup before adding drink. Must be warm enough to flow — not set. Yield: ~350ml. Used heavily in Tiger Brown Sugar Boba.
Pandan Syrup Weekly (Monday) 25 min Blend 10 fresh pandan leaves with 150ml water. Strain through muslin cloth — extract only the green liquid. Combine with 200g caster sugar + 150ml water simple syrup. Refrigerate. Keeps 7–10 days. Pandan must be fresh (not paste) for correct flavour and colour. Intense green — a little goes far. Used in Lychee Pandan Tea, pandan-flavoured mochi, coconut pandan desserts.
Lychee Syrup Weekly 10 min Reduce canned lychee juice (from whole lychee tins — reserve fruit for drinks) with 100g caster sugar until slightly thickened. Cool. Add 5ml rosewater if desired for floral note. Store up to 10 days. Used in Lychee Pandan Tea, Strawberry Yakult Soda (variation), and crepe filling options.
⚑ Mochi Sourcing & Prep 3× per week or as needed 30 min (homemade) or order-in Either make fresh (glutinous rice flour + water + sugar, microwave method, roll in cornstarch) or source from local Japanese/Taiwanese supplier. If buying in: verify delivery freshness — mochi hardens quickly. Best served same day of making/delivery. Used in Matcha Kakigori topping, Mochi Sundae, Tasting Box. Varieties: plain, sesame-filled, taro-filled, strawberry.
⚑ Honey Toast Bread (Japanese Milk Bread) Daily order / delivery Supplier-dependent The entire Honey Toast Tower menu item depends on authentic shokupan (Japanese milk bread). Source from a Japanese or Taiwanese bakery supplier. Order 2 days in advance for daily delivery. Minimum order 6 loaves per trading day. If delivery fails, 86 Honey Toast — do not substitute with standard white bread; the texture and sweetness are critical. Keep supplier backup contact.
Red Bean Paste / Anko Weekly 90 min (homemade) or order Soak 500g azuki beans overnight. Boil until tender (60 min). Drain, add 250g sugar, simmer until thick paste forms. Store refrigerated up to 7 days. Used in Matcha Kakigori, Taro Ball Soup, Mochi filling, Bingsu toppings. Can source pre-made tinned anko but homemade quality is noticably superior — use for premium items.
Diplomat Cream (Crepe Filling) Daily or every 2 days 25 min + chill Pastry cream (milk, egg yolks, sugar, cornflour, vanilla) folded with whipped cream. Must be made fresh — holds 48 hours maximum. Used in Crepe Roll and Crepe Cake layers. Critical texture: should hold shape when sliced but melt on tongue. Too stiff = cheap. Too soft = will collapse in heat. Sydney summer requires slightly more gelatine stabilisation.
Supplier Management Tip: Maintain two suppliers for every high-dependency item (shokupan, fresh mango, lychee, taro). Sydney's Haymarket and Cabramatta offer excellent wholesale Asian grocery options. Build a relationship with a single Japanese/Taiwanese bakery for milk bread — consistency matters more than price. For tropical fruit (mango, lychee), Flemington Produce Market early Tuesday morning is recommended. Keep 2 weeks of dry goods (boba pearls, matcha, azuki) in stock at all times.

Customer Retention System Module 08 · Loyalty, UGC & Community Building
Instagram-First Loyalty Strategy
In 2026, your best marketing team is already in your dining room — they're called your guests. Every table that photographs their dessert and posts it is a free advertisement to an audience that already trusts them. The cost of making a dessert Instagram-worthy is built into your design philosophy, not your marketing budget. Retention starts before the guest even leaves: if they leave with a memory worth sharing, they'll be back.
📸 UGC Design Principles
Height: Dessert towers, stacked pancakes, and tall boba cups photograph better than flat plates. Design plating with vertical dimension in mind.

Colour contrast: Purple taro against white cream, matcha green on clean white, brown sugar drizzle in clear cups — these are Instagram-engineered colour stories.

Motion: The jiggling soufflé, the lava cake pour, the sparkler moment, the shaved ice drizzle — design at least one menu item with a shareable video moment in mind.

Branded elements: Serve drinks in branded cups. Tissue paper in your brand colour. Sparklers as standard for towers (not a surcharge item). A branded background wall near the entrance for planned shots.

Hashtag strategy: Choose 1 primary hashtag (e.g. #[YourBarName]) and 2–3 location-based (#HaymarketSweets, #SydneyDessert) and print them on every table card.
💳 Loyalty Program — Two Options
Option A — Physical Stamp Card:
Simple, no tech required. 10 stamps (one per dessert order, min $14) = 1 free boba drink. Cost per redemption: ~$1.50. Revenue to unlock: ~$160. ROI: exceptional.
Print double-sided: front = stamps + your logo, back = your Instagram handle, hashtag, and seasonal specials. Hand out at counter with every order.

Option B — Digital Loyalty (QR):
Use Stamp Me, Punchh, or Square Loyalty. Guest scans QR at counter to register. Auto-tracks visits. Enables push notifications: "Your favourite mango season is back — come in this week!" Digital also captures email for seasonal announcements. Slightly higher setup cost (~$30–60/month) but data value pays off quickly.

Recommendation: Start with physical for Year 1. Migrate to digital in Year 2 when you have volume and need to send targeted campaign messages.

🎂 Birthday Dessert Pre-Arrangement Program
The offer: Pre-book a Birthday Dessert Tower with a personalised name plate, arrival sparkler, and optional matching boba drinks set for the group. Tower arrives tableside with dramatic entrance — FOH dims lights if possible, sparkler lit, staff deliver with group birthday song (optional).

Booking process: Guest books online or by DM/phone 48 hours advance. Provide: name, number of guests, preferred flavours, dietary needs. Charge a $20 deposit to lock the booking (deducted from final bill).
Why this works for Instagram: A birthday tower with a name plate, sparkler, and group reaction is the single most shareable moment a dessert bar can create. Every guest at that table photographs it. That's 5–12 people posting the same image in the same hour. The group's combined audience might be 50,000+ followers.

Revenue upside: Birthday groups of 4–8 people average $280–450 in total spend. They almost always order boba drinks for the table. They tip well. They tag you. They come back for their partner's birthday three months later.

The Chef's Lens A Reflection by the Head Dessert Chef · Taiwanese-Australian
"The dish that photographs beautifully but tastes mediocre has already failed. The dish that tastes extraordinary but photographs poorly has failed differently — but failed it has."
I grew up eating at Shilin Night Market in Taipei, where my grandmother would queue forty minutes for a single scallion pancake. Nobody was thinking about Instagram then — the phones weren't in our hands yet. But the theatre was always there. The vendor would fold the pancake with two spatulas in this precise, practiced arc. The egg would crack and spread in a perfect circle. People would stop and watch. That was the original food video. We just didn't have the technology to share it at scale.
When I opened my first dessert bar in Sydney, I watched young guests pick up their phones before their spoons. My first instinct — trained in French pastry kitchens — was to feel insulted. Then I reconsidered. They weren't disrespecting the food. They were telling me something important: this moment matters enough to preserve. The challenge became giving them something that deserved both the photograph and the first bite. A soufflé pancake that photographs beautifully but deflates immediately is a broken promise. A raindrop cake that looks like nothing but dissolves into something unexpected on the tongue — that is a complete experience. Both dimensions must be honoured.
In 2026, the night market is everywhere. The theatre my grandmother watched in Taipei now unfolds in Haymarket, Burwood, Chatswood. Our guests carry Taipei in their Instagram feeds before they've ever visited Taiwan. This is not dilution — it is the most extraordinary form of cultural reach that street food has ever had. Our responsibility is to be worthy of that reach. To cook with the precision of craft and the warmth of memory, so that when the phone goes down, the taste lives up to the story.
The Two Failures
Beautiful but tasteless:
The guest photographs, posts, returns — and stops. The second visit never comes because the memory of the taste didn't match the memory of the image.

Extraordinary but invisible:
The guest eats the most transcendent dessert of their year, leaves without photographing, and tells one friend. In 2026, one friend is a fraction of what that meal deserved to inspire.
The Standard
Every item on this menu was tested against two questions:

"Is this worth photographing?"
"Is this worth eating?"

If the answer to either is no, the item doesn't reach the menu. This is the only standard that matters in a dessert bar that wants to last.
做到最好，值得被記住
Do it at your best — make it worth remembering.
— the Head Dessert Chef
