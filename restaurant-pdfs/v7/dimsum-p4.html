Method — Two Styles

1
Hong Kong style (puff pastry shell): Use a lard-and-butter water dough laminated with a fat paste — the Hong Kong egg tart shell is distinctly flaky, with visible layers, and shatters when bitten. Macanese/Portuguese style (shortcrust shell): Dense, buttery, crumbly. Both are legitimate. The HK style is more theatrical; the Macanese style is more forgiving to make. Custard: strain egg + sugar + evaporated milk + fresh milk + vanilla. Pour into blind-baked shells at 160°C until just set with a slight wobble in the centre. The custard should be smooth as silk, pale golden, and fragrant with vanilla. A properly set egg tart wobbles in the centre and is opaque rather than glassy — it still has a slight tremor when the oven is opened, and sets firm as it cools.
💡 Egg tarts are the most-ordered dessert at every Cantonese restaurant in the US. The HK egg tart, when made correctly, is one of the most elegant and precise pastry items in any Asian cuisine. Your egg tarts should be warm at service — the combination of a just-warm flaky shell and a silky, fragrant custard is irreplaceable. At $5.99 for 3 with 6.7% FC, egg tarts are your highest-margin dessert and your highest-volume dessert.

Method

1
Warm mango purée, dissolve bloomed gelatin. Add condensed milk, cream. Pour into moulds or glasses. Refrigerate 4+ hours until set. The mango pudding should be a deep golden-orange (the colour depends entirely on mango variety — use Ataulfo or Alphonso, not Mexican Tommy Atkins which is pale and flavourless). Serve in glass or demould. Offer evaporated milk as a pour-over (traditional HK café style). Garnish with fresh mango cube or pomelo pearls.
Tang Yuan — Sweet Rice Balls in Ginger Soup
Glutinous rice balls · black sesame or peanut filling · ginger sugar broth · Sell: $6.99
⏱ 4 min service (batch)FC 7%Lantern Festival TraditionVegan option
1
Glutinous rice flour mixed with water to form a soft dough. Fill with black sesame paste (sesame + sugar + lard — or coconut oil for vegan) or peanut paste. Roll into spheres. Boil in hot water until they float (about 4 minutes) — floating signals they are cooked. Serve in hot ginger sugar syrup (ginger + palm sugar + water). The warmth of the ginger broth, the silky chew of the glutinous rice skin, and the rich black sesame filling is one of the most perfect simple desserts in Chinese cuisine. Tang yuan is eaten during the Lantern Festival (15th day of Chinese New Year) and at Winter Solstice — feature it prominently on those dates. FC: $0.43 / $6.99 = 6.2%.
