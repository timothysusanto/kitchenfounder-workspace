📖 Guide Bible — The Philosophy of Greek Mediterranean Dining

Greek Mediterranean cuisine is not a trend — it is a 3,000-year-old conversation between land, sea, and fire. This guide codifies that conversation for the modern NYC operator: ingredient-forward, technique-driven, and deeply theatrical in its service.

🏛️ The Four Pillars

  1. Wood & Fire — Charcoal and wood grill as centrepiece
  2. Sea to Table — Whole fish displayed, sold by weight
  3. Mezze Culture — Sharing ethos drives per-head spend
  4. Tableside Theatre — OPA! moments create social currency

💰 NYC Economics

  • Avg spend: $80 per head
  • Covers: 80 seats, dinner 6 nights
  • Revenue target: $1.6M–$2M/year
  • Food cost target: 28–32%
  • Mezze attach rate: 2.5 dishes per table

🗺️ Concept DNA

🫒

Ingredient Integrity

Greek olive oil, imported feta PDO, Kalamata olives, authentic oregano — no substitutes for key flavour pillars.

🔥

Live Fire Mastery

Wood-burning grill gives the char that no gas oven replicates. Octopus, whole fish, and lamb chops demand real smoke.

🍋

Brightness & Balance

Lemon as both acid and aromatics. Every dish finishes with a squeeze. Citrus is not a garnish — it is structure.

🏗️ Kitchen Design — Wood Grill Centrepiece

🔥 Wood Grill Station (Centrepiece)

  • Charcoal/wood-burning grill: 48" commercial unit, visible from dining room
  • Hardwood: Oak and mesquite blend — char, not chemical taste
  • Two temp zones: Screaming hot (fish, octopus) + moderate (lamb, souvlaki)
  • Hood extraction: High-capacity — smoke is flavour at grill, not at the table
  • Positioning: Open-kitchen window or pass-through — guests see the fire

🐟 Whole Fish Display Counter

  • Location: Restaurant entrance / host stand — first impression
  • Display: Crushed ice bed, whole fish labeled by species + price/lb
  • Daily rotation: 4–6 species; whatever is best at market
  • Pricing board: Handwritten chalk; $28–$38/lb typical NYC range
  • Upsell trigger: Host walks guests past fish on the way to table — 40% order rate

🍳 Mezze Cold Station

Marble top, undercounter fridge. Hummus, tzatziki, tarama, feta all held at 38°F. Boards and dips assembled in under 90 seconds.

🔥 Saganaki Table Station

Portable butane burner cart + cast-iron pan. Kept polished and visible — guests ask "what's that?" before they order. Converts curiosity to $22 upsell.

🍩 Loukoumades Fryer

Compact deep fryer, pastry side of kitchen. 375°F maintained all service. Batter prepped fresh daily; never freeze pre-fried. The live fry theatre starts in the kitchen.

🎭 Service Scripts — The 3 Signature Moments

Script 1: Saganaki Flambé — OPA! Ritual

[Server arrives with butane cart, cast iron, cheese, and Metaxa bottle]

"I'm going to make your saganaki right here at the table. Kefalograviera cheese — aged, salty, perfect for the pan. We're going to hit it with Metaxa brandy..."

[Ignites pan. Flames rise 8–10 inches. Entire table reacts.]

🔥 "OPA!" 🔥

"Squeeze the lemon to bring it down — the lemon is part of the magic. Enjoy while it's hot — it firms up quickly."

Training note: OPA! must be said with genuine energy. Practise in team briefing every day. The quiet OPA is a missed opportunity.

Script 2: Whole Fish Tableside Fillet

[Server brings whole grilled fish on platter; displays to table]

"This is your branzino — just came off the wood grill. See the colour? That's real charcoal. I'm going to fillet this for you now..."

[Uses fish fork and spoon; opens fish along dorsal, peels skin, lifts fillet in one clean motion]

"We'll remove the bone section — you'll have two clean fillets. I'll dress them with our ladolemono — that's Greek lemon-oil sauce — right now."

Training note: Practice filleting until it takes under 90 seconds. Hesitation breaks the theatre. Every server must pass a fish-fillet test before table service.

Script 3: Ouzo Aperitif Ritual

"In Greece, every meal starts with ouzo. We serve ours with cold water on the side — pour the water in slowly and watch it cloud up. That's called louche — the oils coming out of the anise. We call it 'the mist of Greece.'"

[Pours 45ml ouzo; places small copper carafe of ice water alongside]

"Sip the ouzo first, then add water to your taste. There is no right amount — it's personal."

Training note: The ouzo ritual reframes a $14 pour as a cultural experience. Attach rate: when servers explain this, ouzo orders increase 35%.
