2Mains — The Dim Sum Table

Mains philosophy: Dim sum "mains" are the heartier dishes that anchor the table — the lo mai gai in lotus leaf, the turnip cake on the griddle, the congee that warms the soul, the char siu that defines Cantonese BBQ. These are the dishes ordered with purpose, not just grazed upon. They are also the dishes with the highest per-unit revenue and the strongest batch-cooking efficiency.

GFMOLLUSCS (oyster sauce)

Method

1
Soak dried lotus leaves in warm water 30 minutes until pliable. Season rice with oyster sauce, light soy, sesame oil, and a little dark soy. Sauté chicken, mushrooms, char siu briefly. Layer in the lotus leaf: rice, filling, rice (encase filling in rice). Fold lotus leaf into a neat parcel. Steam 20 minutes minimum — the lotus leaf flavour infuses the rice and chicken during steaming. A lo mai gai opened at the table releases a distinctive earthy, aromatic steam that signals authenticity to anyone who grew up with Cantonese food. Batch-make; steam to order (20 min) or pre-steam and reheat (5 min).

GFMOLLUSCS (hoisin/oyster)

Method — The Glaze is Everything

1
Marinate pork strips minimum 24 hours (48 hours preferred). The maltose/honey combination creates the distinctive sticky, slightly lacquered glaze. Roast at 220°C / 428°F on a rack over a water tray (prevents burning). Baste with additional glaze every 10 minutes. The finished char siu should be deeply caramelised (almost charred at edges — "char" is in the name), sticky, red-brown, with the five spice aroma. Rest 5 minutes. Slice against the grain. Serve as a main with rice and mustard, or use as filling in bao, cheung fun, and lo mai gai.
💡 Char siu is the single most versatile ingredient in a dim sum kitchen — it fills the bao, rolls in the cheung fun, stuffs the lo mai gai, tops the congee. Making it in-house rather than buying from a Chinatown supplier is a significant differentiator. The aroma of char siu roasting in the kitchen fills the entire dining room and creates anticipation. If you can only make one Cantonese BBQ item in-house, it should be this.

Method

1
Batch cook congee: rinse rice once only, add to stock at 1:10 ratio, bring to boil, reduce to lowest heat, cook 90 minutes stirring occasionally. The rice should break down completely — no individual grains visible, the congee should be thick, smooth, and glossy. Season with light soy and white pepper. At service: ladle into bowl, add century egg, preserved meat, raw ginger julienne, crispy shallots, green onion. The raw ginger and crispy shallot added at the table are essential — they provide heat and textural contrast to the silky congee base. Serve with a youtiao (fried dough stick) for dipping.

GFSHELLFISH (dried shrimp)

Method

1
Mix grated daikon with rice flour, sausage, dried shrimp, shiitake, seasoning. Pour into a greased tray. Steam 45 minutes until firm throughout. Cool, refrigerate overnight. Cut into 1cm slices. Pan-fry in oil until a deep golden crust forms on both sides. The interior should be soft and slightly sticky; the exterior should be very crispy with defined edges. Serve with hoisin sauce and chilli. Lo bak go is best appreciated by people who understand that the turnip is primarily a textural vehicle — the flavour comes from the sausage, dried shrimp, and the pan-fry crust.

Fried Rice — Yangzhou Style
Day-old rice · egg · char siu · shrimp · BBQ pork · Sell: $13.99
⏱ 6 min wokFC 14%Wok Hei EssentialGF
1
Yangzhou fried rice is the definitive Chinese fried rice — day-old jasmine rice (fresh rice fries poorly; the dried surface of overnight rice creates proper texture), scrambled egg, diced char siu, prawns, frozen peas, green onion, light soy. The critical technique: wok hei (鑊氣) — the "breath of the wok" flavour created by cooking on screaming-high heat with proper tossing. A commercial wok burner operating at full power creates this; a domestic cooktop does not. At $13.99, this is your anchor rice dish. FC: $1.96 / $13.99 = 14%.

1
XO sauce is Hong Kong's premium condiment — invented at the Peninsula Hotel in the 1980s, made from dried scallops, dried shrimp, cured ham, chilli, garlic, and shallots. It is expensive to make in-house but transformative as a flavour agent. Pan-sear fresh scallops to a golden crust, finish with XO sauce, ginger, light soy, green onion. The XO sauce amplifies the natural sweetness of the scallop with umami depth that no other sauce achieves. At $19.99 with scallops as protein, this is your premium individual dish — the one that signals serious culinary intent. FC: $4.40 / $19.99 = 22%.

Chinese eggplant (long, thin, less bitter than Italian variety) cut into thick batons, briefly deep-fried until softened and golden. Drain.
