Method — The Black Cake Tradition

1
The rum-soaked fruit: Caribbean black cake is built on fruit soaking in rum for months — sometimes years. Keep a permanent jar of mixed dried fruit submerged in dark rum. Minimum 2 weeks soaking. After 3 months the fruit is deeply alcoholic and jam-like, producing a dramatically superior cake. Cream butter and muscovado sugar 8 minutes. Add eggs one at a time. Fold in flour and spices. Add browning sauce for the characteristic dark colour. Fold in rum-soaked fruit with all the rum from the jar. Bake in a lined loaf tin at 160°C / 320°F for 50–60 minutes.
2
Post-bake rum soak: While warm, pierce all over with a skewer. Pour 50ml dark rum slowly over the surface — it absorbs into the warm cake. Repeat every other day for 1–2 weeks as the cake rests wrapped in foil. This post-bake soak creates the characteristic moist, dense black cake texture. Serve at room temperature, never warmed.
💡 The differentiator from commercial rum cake: months of fruit soaking + post-bake soaking + browning sauce for depth. When guests taste a properly made black cake, the reaction is uniform: "This is completely different from what I expected." That surprise is a retention mechanism.
Almost black in colour, dense enough to cut cleanly. First bite: burst of rum-soaked dried fruit, muscovado's molasses sweetness, warmth of mixed spice and cinnamon. Moist, slightly sticky. Rum present as warmth and depth. This is the taste of Caribbean Christmas, year-round.

Method

1
Steep dried sorrel in hot water (90°C) with ginger and cinnamon for 20 minutes. Strain. Sweeten with cane sugar. Deep crimson, tart, fragrant. Bloom gelatine. Combine equal parts strained sorrel tea and cream (warmed to 60°C) with bloomed gelatine. Pour into individual moulds. Refrigerate 4+ hours until set. Rum syrup: dark rum + cane sugar + lime juice, heat until sugar dissolves. At service: unmould, drizzle rum syrup, add lime zest.
Wobbly, silky, vivid pink-red. Floral hibiscus tartness with ginger and cinnamon warmth underneath. Cream softening the sorrel's acidity. Rum syrup dark and sweet, pooling at the base. Lime zest on top. This tastes like Trinidad at Christmas in a form that works year-round.

Method

1
Mix flour (200g), coconut milk (120ml — replaces water entirely, giving a distinctly richer, coconut-forward texture), desiccated coconut (30g), baking powder (1.5 tsp), salt, pinch of sugar. Rest 15 minutes. Shape into a 1.5cm thick round. Bake at 190°C / 375°F for 20 minutes until golden. Split and serve warm with guava jam and cold butter. The guava jam should be bright pink-orange, intensely tropical, sweet-tart.
Golden, fragrant with coconut. Torn open: soft, steam-releasing coconut interior. Guava jam: vivid pink, intensely tropical. Cold butter melting slowly. Simple, complete, and exactly right after a full Caribbean meal.

Method

1
Shell fresh tamarind. Remove fibres and seeds. Work the sticky flesh with your hands, kneading in a pinch of salt and scotch bonnet powder. Roll into 15g balls. Roll each ball in caster sugar mixed with a small amount of scotch bonnet powder. The sugar coating provides sweetness against the tamarind's intense sourness; the scotch bonnet adds the fruity Caribbean heat that defines this sweet. Make in batches — they keep 2 weeks at room temperature in an airtight container.
