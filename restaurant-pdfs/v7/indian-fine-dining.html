The Concept

Philosophy

Indian fine dining in NYC is the fastest-growing upscale segment — not curry houses, not buffets. This is composed, regional cuisine with French-level technique applied to Indian depth. Every plate tells a story from a specific region, caste tradition, or Mughal court. The guest doesn't just eat — they travel.

The Four Pillars

  • Tandoor Theatre: Visible clay oven, 700°F+, char-kissed meats and breads that arrive smoking
  • Dum Pukht: Slow-sealed cooking — biryani opened tableside, aroma first
  • Regional Specificity: Not "Indian food" — Goan, Kerala, Hyderabadi, Rajasthani, Bengali
  • Spice Intelligence: Staff trained to guide guests through heat levels and flavour profiles

Target Guest

  • Adventurous NYC diner, 28–55
  • Indian diaspora celebrating milestones
  • Corporate entertaining with international clients
  • Food media and influencer crowd
  • Date-night couples seeking theatrical dining
Operating Standards

Dining Room Standards

  • 70 seats · 6 nights/week (Tue–Sun)
  • Two turns on Fri/Sat: 6pm + 8:30pm
  • Average cover: $65 food + $22 beverage = $87 total
  • Reservations required: Resy platform
  • Music: Tabla/sitar jazz at 62 dB max
  • Candles: Brass diyas, replaced every service
  • Linen: Cream/ivory, pressed, no synthetic blends

Nut-Free Protocol

All dishes have nut-free versions. Kitchen is not nut-free by default — notify team of nut allergy on booking and ticket.

  • Pumpkin seeds replace cashews in all korma/makhani sauces
  • Dedicated nut-free prep board (green label)
  • All sauces available in nut-free batch — label clearly
  • Alert whole team from host to expeditor

Brand Voice

  • Never say "mild/medium/hot" — use regional names
  • "Laal Maas has the Rajasthani desert heat" not "it's spicy"
  • Describe texture first, spice second, aroma third
  • Train staff on 5 key regional stories

✨ The Four Signature Moments (Non-Negotiable)

1. Biryani Dum Open
Sealed with dough, cracked tableside by server. Saffron steam billows. Guest inhales first.
2. Copper Pot Gulab Jamun
Dessert brought to table in small copper pot with ladle. Server portions warm. Rose water mist optional.
3. Tableside Masala Chai
Chai brewed on induction trolley beside table. Poured from height into glass tumbler. Theatric close.
4. Tandoor Visibility: Open kitchen or window shows the tandoor at all times. Orange glow, char smell, chef in whites. This is your $0 marketing moment — every guest photographs it.

Mulligatawny / Dal Shorba
Soup · 165°F FC: 14% Yield: 8 oz cup
IngredientQtyUnit Cost

Crispy shallot garnish0.3 oz$0.08
  1. Tadka: heat ghee, bloom cumin seeds until pop. Add onion — caramelise 8 min.
  2. Add garlic-ginger, tomato, dry spices. Cook 4 min until jammy.
  3. Add rinsed lentils and stock. Simmer 20 min until lentils collapse.

Murgh Makhani — Butter Chicken
Sauce · 165°F IT FC: 26% Yield: 1 portion (12 oz)
IngredientQtyCost
Chicken tikka (pre-cooked)5 oz$1.30
Makhani sauce (batch)6 oz$0.85

Rogan Josh — Kashmiri Lamb
Braise · 195°F FC: 30% Yield: 1 portion
IngredientQtyCost
Lamb shoulder, 1.5" cubes6 oz$2.10
Mustard oil1 tbsp$0.08
Whole spices (bay, cardamom, clove)mix$0.06
Ratanjot / Kashmiri chilli paste1 oz$0.14
Fennel powder, ginger powder1 tsp ea$0.06
Whole-milk yogurt2 oz$0.14
Saffron water0.5 oz$0.22
  1. Smoke mustard oil until wisps appear. Bloom whole spices 30 sec.
  2. Sear lamb in batches — deep mahogany on all sides. Remove.
  3. Fry chilli paste 2 min. Add fennel, ginger powder, asafoetida.
  4. Return lamb. Add beaten yogurt in 3 additions — stir constantly to prevent splitting.

Goan Fish Curry — Ambot Tik
Simmer · 145°F FC: 31% Yield: 1 portion
IngredientQtyCost
Black sea bass / striped bass6 oz$2.40
Kokum (dried sour fruit)3 pcs$0.18
Coconut milk4 oz$0.28
Goan masala paste (dried chilli)1.5 oz$0.20
Onion, shallots2 oz$0.08
Basmati rice (steamed)4 oz$0.22
Mustard seeds, curry leaftadka$0.06
  1. Rehydrate kokum in warm water 15 min. Kokum is the sour agent — no tamarind.
  2. Sauté shallots until golden. Add Goan masala paste. Fry 3 min — deep red.
  3. Add coconut milk + kokum water. Simmer 5 min. Season — sour-spicy balance.

⭐ Gulab Jamun — Copper Pot Service
Warm · 110°F syrup FC: 20% Yield: 3 pcs/portion
IngredientQtyCost
Khoya (reduced whole milk)3 oz$0.60
All-purpose flour1 tbsp$0.03
Baking sodapinch$0.01
Rose water syrup4 oz$0.18
Cardamom powderpinch$0.02
Saffron strands4–5$0.10
Vanilla ice cream (optional)1 scoop$0.28
  1. Knead khoya, flour, baking soda into smooth dough. No cracks — or balls split in oil.
  2. Roll into smooth balls. Fry in ghee at 285°F — LOW temp. Slow golden over 8 min.
  3. Syrup: 1:1 sugar water + rose water + saffron + cardamom. Warm, not hot.
  4. Transfer fried balls to warm syrup immediately. Soak minimum 30 min.
  5. 🌟 SERVICE: Present warm in small copper pot with ladle. Server ladles 3 into guest's bowl.
Chef Tip: Temperature of oil is everything — too hot and outside burns before centre cooks. Use a thermometer every time.

Malai Kulfi — Rose & Pistachio (NF: Rose & Pumpkin Seed)
Frozen · -10°F FC: 22% Yield: 1 mould
IngredientQtyCost
Full-fat milk, reduced 50%6 oz$0.40
Condensed milk1.5 oz$0.22
Rose water1 tsp$0.04
Cardamom powderpinch$0.02
Pumpkin seeds, toasted (NF)0.3 oz$0.12
Dried rose petalsgarnish$0.08
  1. Reduce full-fat milk to 50% volume over low heat, stirring frequently — 45 min.
  2. Stir in condensed milk, rose water, cardamom. Cool completely.
  3. Fold in toasted pumpkin seeds (NF version). Pour into conical kulfi moulds.

Tandoor Notes

  • Pre-heat 2 hrs before service — tandoor cannot rush
  • Clay lining replaced every 6–12 months depending on use
  • Position: visible from 40% of dining room minimum
  • Chef in whites + tongs = walking theatre
  • Exhaust + fire suppression: commercial-grade required by NYC code
Service Scripts

🌶️ Spice Level Conversation

Never ask: "Do you want it mild, medium, or hot?"

Instead say:

"The Laal Maas has the desert heat of Rajasthan — it's meant to make you sweat. The Butter Chicken is warming but gentle. Where does your palate usually sit?"

Listen for: "I love spicy" vs "I enjoy flavour without too much heat." Then guide. Never let the guest self-rate on a 1–10 scale — they always underestimate.

🍛 Curry Pairing Script

"If you're choosing between the Rogan Josh and the Moilee — Rogan Josh is robust, bone-warming, built for bread. The Moilee is lighter, coconut-fragrant, better with rice. What feels right tonight?"

Guide every table toward: 1 tandoor starter + 1 main + 1 bread or rice. Upsell shared starters and add-on naan naturally.

☕ Chai Close Script

"Before you go — we brew our masala chai tableside. It takes three minutes and it's how every dinner should end. Can I bring the trolley over?"

This closes the meal emotionally. Chai = hospitality. It's not a $5 add-on — it's the thing they'll tell friends about. Comp it for parties of 4+ on first visit.
