Chef's Introduction · The Modern Thai Philosophy02
Ancient Flavours. Contemporary Hands.
Thai cuisine is one of the great balancing acts of the culinary world. Sweet, sour, salty, spicy, bitter — not in sequence, but simultaneously, in every mouthful. The ancient royal court kitchens of Ayutthaya understood this balance as an expression of harmony. The street vendor at the night market expresses the same harmony with a wok, a fire, and thirty years of muscle memory. What modern Thai fine dining asks is whether that same harmony can be expressed with precision, with refinement, with intention — without losing the soul.

The answer is yes. But only if you start with flavour, not technique. Every recipe in this guide begins with a traditional Thai preparation — the gaeng, the laab, the tom, the yam — and then applies contemporary technique: temperature control, emulsification, dehydration, gel. The technique serves the flavour. The moment the reverse is true, you have stopped cooking Thai food and started performing it. There is a difference, and the guest always knows.
— Head Chef, Guide #48

01
Laab Gai Tartare
Free-Range Chicken · Toasted Rice Powder · Kaffir Lime · Betel Leaf
Ingredients — 4 portions
Free-range chicken breast, minced 200g
Toasted rice powder (khao khua) 15g
Fish sauce 20ml
Lime juice, fresh 25ml
Bird's eye chilli, sliced fine 2
Shallots, sliced paper-thin 40g
Dried chilli flakes, toasted 3g
Kaffir lime leaf, micro-chiffonade 3 leaves
Fresh betel leaves 8 leaves
Coriander, mint (small leaves) 10g each
Method
1Flash-poach chicken mince in boiling salted water 90 seconds — just cooked, no colour. Drain. Ice bath. Dry completely on paper towel. Refrigerate.
2Khao khua: dry-toast uncooked glutinous rice in pan until golden-brown, grind to powder (not flour). This is non-negotiable — it adds flavour and textural contrast no substitute can provide.
3Dress chicken with fish sauce + lime juice. Add shallots, chilli, dried flakes, kaffir lime chiffonade, khao khua. Toss. Taste — should be sharp, herbal, savoury, with a lip-tingle from the dried chilli.
4Fine dining plating: press 40g into ring mould on betel leaf cup. Crown with single mint leaf, kaffir lime oil drop (infused neutral oil), micro coriander. Serve within 5 minutes — khao khua softens quickly.
Chef Technique

Khao khua must be freshly ground per service — pre-ground loses the nutty volatile oils within 2 hours. The betel leaf base provides a subtle peppery bitterness that frames the brightness of the laab. This is intentional balance, not decoration.

Fine Dining Presentation

3 betel cups arranged in a gentle arc on black slate. Micro herbs at exact same height. Kaffir lime oil drop placed with pipette. The minimalism of the presentation signals: this kitchen is in control. Everything here is intentional.

02
Tom Kha Foam with Scallop
Seared Hokkaido Scallop · Coconut Galangal Foam · Kaffir Oil · Crispy Shallot
Ingredients — 4 portions
Hokkaido scallop U10 4 pieces
Coconut cream 300ml
Galangal, sliced 30g
Lemongrass, bruised 2 stalks
Kaffir lime leaves 6
Fish sauce 15ml
Lime juice 20ml
Soy lecithin 3g (for foam)
Crispy shallots 10g
Kaffir lime oil 10ml
Method
1Simmer coconut cream + galangal + lemongrass + kaffir leaves 20 min. Strain. Season with fish sauce + lime. This is your tom kha base — taste it like a soup; it must be balanced.
2Foam: cool base to 60°C. Add soy lecithin (3g per 300ml). Blend with immersion blender at surface to create stable foam. White, delicate, aromatic.
3Dry scallops completely. Season. Sear in screaming hot pan with neutral oil 90 sec one side only — never flip a scallop twice. Baste with butter, garlic, thyme final 20 seconds. Rest.
4Plate: pool 30ml warm tom kha base. Place scallop centre — seared face up. Spoon foam over and around generously. Kaffir oil drops around. Crispy shallots on scallop peak. Micro herbs. Serve immediately.
Chef Technique

Soy lecithin foam is surface tension, not air — too little lecithin and foam collapses within 2 minutes. 3g per 300ml is the correct ratio. Foam must be made at service, never in advance.

Fail-Proof Tip

The single sear is everything for scallop. Wet scallops steam instead of sear — pat absolutely dry with paper towel twice. Pan must be 220°C minimum. You should hear the sizzle from across the kitchen.

07
Massaman Braised Short Rib
72hr Wagyu Short Rib · Massaman Emulsion · Fingerling Potato · Crispy Shallot
Ingredients — 4 portions
Wagyu short rib, bone-in 800g (200g/portion)
Massaman curry paste 80g
Coconut cream 400ml
Beef stock 600ml
Fish sauce 30ml
Palm sugar 40g
Star anise 3
Fingerling potatoes 300g
Crispy shallots 20g
Massaman oil (paste + neutral) 30ml
Method
1Season short ribs. Sear all sides until deep mahogany crust — 3 min per side. Remove. Toast massaman paste in same pan 2 min.
2Add coconut cream + stock + fish sauce + palm sugar + star anise. Return ribs. Braise 160°C for 6 hours covered, or 24 hrs sous vide at 72°C for maximum texture.
3Massaman emulsion: strain braising liquid. Reduce by half. Blend with 30ml massaman oil until glossy and emulsified. Strain. This is your sauce — it should coat a spoon heavily.
4Confit fingerling potatoes in duck fat 160°C 45 min. Glaze in braising reduction. Plate: potato cluster, short rib (boneless or bone-in for theatre), emulsion pooled around. Crispy shallots on rib peak. Serve with jasmine rice compressed and turned.
Chef Technique

The 72hr sous vide short rib is the premium version — the texture is fundamentally different to oven braising. Plan: Monday sous vide → Wednesday service. The massaman emulsion must be made fresh per service — it splits when reheated aggressively.

Fail-Proof Tip

Short ribs release enormous fat during braising — skim braising liquid before reducing or your sauce will be greasy. Refrigerate overnight and lift solidified fat off the surface before reducing. This one step separates a professional sauce from an amateur one.

12 · Green Curry Barramundi
Ingredients: Barramundi fillet 200g skin on, green curry paste 40g (house-made: lemongrass + green chilli + galangal + coriander root + shrimp paste + kaffir lime zest + garlic + shallot), coconut milk 350ml, fish sauce 20ml, palm sugar 10g, Thai basil 15g, pea eggplant 40g, kaffir lime leaves 3. 5Service Training Module

Fine Dining Service Philosophy

Fine dining service has one guiding principle: the guest should never have to ask for anything. Water refilled before they notice the glass is half-full. Bread offered before they look around for it. The pace of the meal managed so the next course arrives just as they're finishing the previous. This level of attentiveness requires active table reading — watching without staring, knowing without hovering. It is, in every sense, a performance.

// Menu Introduction — Tasting Menu
"This evening we're offering two experiences: the à la carte menu, where you choose freely, or our 7-course tasting menu at THB 2,800 per person. The tasting menu follows the arc of a traditional Thai royal meal — from cooling and aromatic through building heat and richness, finishing with our pandan soufflé. For a first visit, our chefs recommend the tasting menu — it was designed to tell the story of this kitchen in one sitting. And we have a Thai wine pairing to match each course if you're open to it."
Why this works: The "story of this kitchen" framing transforms the tasting menu from a price decision into a cultural experience. The Thai wine pairing recommendation adds average spend significantly and differentiates the experience from standard tasting menus. Always lead with the recommendation; let the guest decline rather than never offering.
// Tom Kha Scallop Course Delivery
"Your second course: Hokkaido scallop with tom kha foam. The foam is made to order using soy lecithin — it will hold for about 4 minutes before it settles. The scallop is seared on one face only, which is intentional — you'll see the textural difference between the caramelised sear side and the raw interior. The kaffir lime oil underneath is made here in the kitchen daily. Please don't wait — this one is best in the first 2 minutes."
Why this works: Explaining the technique without lecturing signals kitchen confidence. The 2-minute advisory creates gentle urgency that is actually true — foam and seared scallop are both time-sensitive. Guests who eat immediately have a better experience and become better advocates.
// Wine Pairing Introduction (Thai Wine)
"This is a Monsoon Valley Chenin Blanc from the Hua Hin Hills. Thailand has a small but serious wine industry — the Asoke Valley and Hua Hin Hills both produce at an altitude that creates genuine complexity. This is not a novelty wine; it won its last three international competitions against New World whites. With the scallop and the tom kha, the tropical fruit in this wine is going to track the coconut very well. Would you like a glass?"
Why this works: Removing the guest's hesitation about Thai wine (with facts, not persuasion) opens the sale. The competition wins remove the quality doubt. The specific pairing reasoning ("coconut") makes the recommendation feel personalised rather than a standard pour.

8Customer Retention System

Fine Dining Loyalty Strategy

Fine dining guests are the most valuable guests in hospitality. They spend more, stay longer, and when they find a restaurant they trust, they return for every occasion worth celebrating. Your retention system has one priority above all others: personalisation. Know their dietary preferences before they sit down. Know their regular wine before they order. Know it's their anniversary before they mention it. This information should be in your booking system and briefed to the floor team before service.

// Michelin Chef's Lens — Modern Thai Fine Dining
The question modern Thai fine dining must answer is not a culinary one. It is a philosophical one: what does it mean to refine a cuisine that was already perfect? The phad thai of a Bangkok street vendor is already an expression of perfect balance. The som tam of a market vendor is already built from 4,000 years of agricultural and culinary intelligence. What, exactly, is fine dining adding?

The honest answer, from chefs who have worked both a Michelin-starred kitchen and a charcoal wok: precision, not improvement. The lemongrass gimlet does not taste more lemongrass than a glass of lemongrass tea from a market stall — it is simply more consistent. The tom kha foam does not taste deeper than a traditional tom kha gai — it is simply more precise. The massaman short rib does not taste better than a massaman from a Bangkok home kitchen — it is simply more controlled.

This is what the fine dining guest pays for: the guarantee of consistency, precision, and control. The best modern Thai chefs carry both disciplines simultaneously: the soul of the market, the hands of the laboratory. This guide gives you the tools. The soul must come from you.
