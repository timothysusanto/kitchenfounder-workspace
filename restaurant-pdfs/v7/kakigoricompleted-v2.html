The Concept: Japanese Shaved Ice as Culinary Art
Kakigori (かき氷) has existed in Japan for over a thousand years — first enjoyed by Heian-era aristocrats who sent servants into the mountains to retrieve natural ice. The modern kakigori renaissance was driven by shops like Himitsudo in Tokyo, which elevated the form with house-made fruit syrups and fresh produce hidden inside the bowl — a discovery moment that became the shop's signature. This guide teaches you how to build that business: a kakigori specialist that operates year-round, builds customer loyalty through seasonal scarcity, and generates extraordinary margin from pure slow-frozen water and 14 proprietary syrups. The ice is free. The flavour is everything.

The Four Business Pillars

01 · Ice Quality Is Non-Negotiable
  • Pure block ice, slow-frozen 24–36hrs from filtered water — never crushed ice
  • Hatsuyuki HA-110E or Swan SI-110E kakigori machine — $3,000–$5,000. Budget this first.
  • Correct snow: dry, fluffy, holds dome 90+ seconds, disappears on tongue without going watery
  • Wrong ice: wet, dense, collapses immediately. No workaround exists.
  • Two weeks of daily practice before opening. This machine IS the restaurant.
02 · House Syrups Are the IP
  • 14 proprietary syrups from real ingredients — no commercial flavouring, no powder
  • Universal secrets in every syrup: citric acid (brightness), fine salt (amplifies sweetness 15%), glucose syrup (clings to ice)
  • Seasonal syrups available only at ingredient peak — that IS the marketing
  • Supplied to franchisees as numbered concentrates only — recipes never disclosed
03 · The Hidden Layer
  • Every bowl has a hidden layer built in before ice is shaved on top
  • Guest discovers it midway — azuki, warm amazake, fresh fruit, mochi
  • Staff never reveal it: "I won't tell you. Eat straight down through the centre."
  • Costs $0.40–$0.80 per bowl. Creates the Instagram post and the return visit.
04 · Seasonal Scarcity as a System
  • 4 seasonal bowls/year — spring (sakura), summer (mango), autumn (white peach), winter (amazake)
  • Available ~6–8 weeks only. Sell-out target: 1–1.5hrs before closing. Never discount unsold.
  • Instagram release day post + sold-out post — both generate organic reach, zero paid spend

Year-Round Sustainability

The Winter Strategy

Most kakigori shops treat the product as a summer item. That is why they fail. The shops that thrive year-round do three things:

  • Warm-tasting year-round bowls — hojicha, black sesame, kinako read as comforting even when cold. Psychological warmth overrides temperature.
  • Warm hidden layers in winter — warm amazake poured into the bowl base before ice goes on top. Guest finds it midway through. Temperature contrast is the experience.
  • Warm menu extensions — zenzai (warm azuki soup), warm hojicha latte, warm amazake drink. Converts hesitant winter guests.
  • Atelier atmosphere — warm lighting, ceramic cups, low wooden seating makes the space feel warm even when the product is cold.
Franchise Target Locations
  • City laneway / narrow-front — intimacy, mystique, visible queue
  • Near cultural precincts — Japanese garden, museum, art/fashion quarter
  • Tokyo · Sydney · Singapore · Bangkok · Los Angeles
  • Never in food courts — atelier atmosphere requires standalone space

Shiro Miso Soup & Grilled Mochi
白みそ汁＋焼き餅 — The Most Honest Dish on the Menu · $10
Proper kombu-katsuobushi dashi · shiro miso · sake + sesame oil secrets · charred kirimochi · yuzu zest
Winter #1FC 21%
Ingredients (per serve)
Dashi (proper — not powder)
Kombu cold-soak 30 min5 g + 400 ml
Katsuobushi, added at 60°C, steep 5 min, strain10 g
Soup
Dashi (per serve)200 ml
Shiro miso (Marukome white rice — gluten-free)1.5 tbsp
↳ Secret: 1 tsp sake, added AFTER miso dissolves, off heatrounds bitterness, brightens broth
↳ Secret: 2 drops toasted sesame oil, off heat onlyaromatic finish — burns off if added hot
Kirimochi × 1 — grilled until puffed + charred edges1 piece
Yuzu zest, micro-grated over bowl at service4 strokes
Method
  1. 1Proper dashi: kombu cold-soak 30 min, heat slowly to 60°C, add katsuobushi, steep 5 min, strain. Never boil — boiling makes dashi bitter and muddy. This is the flavour foundation.
  2. 2Heat dashi to 80°C. Dissolve shiro miso by whisking a small amount of hot dashi into miso first, then add back to pot. Remove completely from heat. Add sake and sesame oil — both are volatile and cook out instantly if added to boiling liquid.
  3. 3Grill kirimochi in dry pan or under grill until puffed and edges char. Place in bowl. Pour miso soup. Micro-grate yuzu zest over. Serve immediately — the charred mochi continues to absorb broth as the guest eats, softening further.
🧊 The Sake + Sesame Oil SecretBoth must be added completely off heat. Sake rounds the bitterness that miso can carry; sesame oil adds a toasted aromatic finish. Added to hot liquid, both evaporate instantly and contribute nothing. This is the step that separates a thoughtful miso soup from a standard one.

Guest finds unexpected richness mid-bowl — that's why it goes on top, not the bottom.

The Kakigori Service Standard — Curiosity as Hospitality
Every guest who enters is already curious — that is why they are here. The job of every staff member is to reward that curiosity with knowledge. A guest who leaves knowing three things they didn't know on arrival has had a cultural experience. Cultural experiences generate recommendations. Recommendations fill the shop for free. These 6 scripts are the entire training foundation.

The 6 Staff Scripts

Script When to Use What to Say
01 · Welcome Guest approaches counter for the first time "Welcome — have you been to a kakigori shop before? If not, I'd love to explain what makes ours different. It starts with the ice."
02 · The Ice When a first-timer asks what makes it different "Most places use crushed ice — it melts fast and goes watery. We use pure block ice, frozen slowly, shaved on a Japanese machine that produces something like fresh snow. It absorbs the syrup without collapsing. On your tongue it disappears — not melts. It's a completely different experience."
03 · Hidden Layer After the guest orders, before the bowl arrives "Every bowl has a hidden layer underneath the snow — you'll discover something halfway through. I won't tell you what it is. Eat straight down through the middle."
04 · Seasonal When a guest looks at the seasonal bowl "The [seasonal bowl] is only available for about six weeks while [ingredient] is at its peak. After that it's gone until next year."
05 · Drink Pairing When a bowl is ordered without a drink "Would you like something warm alongside? The hojicha latte pairs really nicely — they share the same roasted flavour. Or the yuzu soda if you'd prefer something cold and bright to contrast."
06 · Winter Reframe When a guest hesitates on a cold day "In Japan the best kakigori shops are open year-round. In winter they pair warm hidden layers — our Yuzu Amazake bowl has warm amazake hidden inside cold snow. The contrast when it's cold outside is extraordinary."

  • Release posts consistently achieve highest organic reach of the quarter

  • Every bowl, every time — unique hidden layer that changes with the seasonal rotation
  • Staff never reveal it. Policy enforced in training.
  • The surprise is simultaneously the return-visit driver and the Instagram post driver
  • 4 new discoveries per year — built into the menu architecture
3. Monthly Tasting Class

  • Guests make one syrup and one bowl — they leave as product evangelists
  • Waitlist creates social proof: "3-week wait for the class" is its own marketing

Kitchen Design

Production Zone (20–30 sqm)
  • Kakigori machine — Hatsuyuki HA-110E or Swan SI-110E · $3,000–$5,000. Budget this first. Everything else is secondary.
  • Block ice chest freezer — dedicated commercial unit. 24–36hr slow-freeze. Always 2+ days ahead.
  • Inline water filter — tap water minerals affect ice clarity and texture. Filtered water only.
  • Syrup station — induction cooker, squeeze bottles labelled #01–#14. Batch every 2 days.
  • Mise en place at shaving station — azuki, shiratama, mochi, creams, garnish within arm's reach. 90 seconds shave-to-pass maximum.
  • Machine must be visible from entrance — watching snow being created is 50% of the experience.
Café Space (30–50 sqm)
  • 35–45 seats: counter + low wooden tables + window ledge
