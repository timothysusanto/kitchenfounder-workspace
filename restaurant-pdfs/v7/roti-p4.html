Method — The Long Braise

1
Season oxtail with green seasoning (shadow beni, garlic, thyme, spring onion) and marinate overnight. Brown in batches in a Dutch oven — good colour essential. Add: allspice berries (6), scotch bonnet (1 whole), soy sauce (2 tbsp), Grace browning sauce (1 tbsp — the Jamaican browning technique gives the characteristic mahogany colour), thyme, onion, garlic. Add water to cover by 2cm. Braise covered at 150°C / 300°F for 3–4 hours until the oxtail trembles and begins to separate from the bone. Add butter beans in the final 45 minutes — they absorb the braising liquid and become creamy, fully infused with oxtail broth.
2
Rice and peas (Jamaican): Cook long-grain rice in coconut milk (replace half cooking water) with kidney beans, spring onion, thyme, scotch bonnet (whole, remove after), and allspice. Season with salt. The rice should be fragrant with coconut, slightly coloured from the kidney beans, firm-textured. The butter beans in the oxtail + the kidney beans in the rice create a bean-rich plate that anchors the meal.
💡 Oxtail is the most prestigious dish in Jamaican domestic cooking — Sunday's pot, what mothers cook for family. The bone marrow and connective tissue convert to rich gelatin during the long braise, creating a sauce of extraordinary body. Do not cut the cooking time. The dish's reputation is built entirely on the oxtail trembling when the plate is set down.
The oxtail: trembling, dark-sauced, gelatinous and deeply savoury. The braising liquid reduced to a glossy sauce from the bone marrow. The butter beans: creamy, saturated with oxtail broth. The coconut rice: fragrant, slightly sweet. The fried plantain: caramelised, sweet — the perfect contrast to the rich, dark oxtail.

Method

1
Curried crab is the signature dish of Tobago, served every Sunday at beach restaurants across Pigeon Point. Always serve the crab whole — the messy, hands-on experience IS the dish. Clean live crabs: remove apron, lift top shell, remove gills, rinse. Marinate in green seasoning and curry powder 30 minutes. "Burn the curry" — heat oil at high heat, add curry powder directly, fry 60–90 seconds until deeply fragrant and slightly darkened. This blooming technique activates fat-soluble compounds that water-based cooking cannot extract. Add crab. Coat in bloomed curry. Add coconut milk, scotch bonnet, shadow beni, thyme, and a splash of water. Cook covered 20–25 minutes until shells are bright orange-red and the curry is thick and fragrant.
2
Boiled dumplings: Simple flour + water + salt, rolled into egg-sized pieces, boiled 20 minutes until cooked through. Dense, starchy — designed specifically to soak the curry sauce. Do not substitute with bread or roti; the boiled dumpling's density absorbs the curry differently and is the correct traditional accompaniment.
💡 Weekend special only — Fri–Sun. Serves with bibs, small shell bowls, crab crackers. Tables who order it spend longer, order more drinks, have the highest satisfaction rating. The spectacle of whole crab in golden curry turning heads across the dining room sells more orders than any verbal recommendation.

Method — The Oil-Down Technique

1
Rundown gets its name from "running down" (reducing) coconut milk until it separates and the coconut oil rises to the surface. Pour coconut milk into a wide pan. Simmer 15–20 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the liquid evaporates and the thick cream begins to bubble and separate — the coconut oil appears on the surface. Add scotch bonnet, thyme, spring onion, tomato. Fry in the coconut oil 5 minutes. Add fish and a small amount of water. Simmer gently 8 minutes for saltfish, 12 minutes for fresh mackerel.
2
Boil spinners (flour + water + salt, twisted into small dumplings) separately in salted water 15 minutes. Boil green banana (in its skin) until tender, approximately 20 minutes. Serve fish in the rundown sauce with green banana and spinners alongside.
The rundown sauce: thick, intensely coconutty from the reduction, golden from the coconut oil frying, fragrant with scotch bonnet and thyme. The fish cooked directly in coconut sauce, absorbing its richness. The green banana: slightly astringent, starchy, contrasting the rich coconut. This is the Caribbean coast on a plate.

Method

1
Goat water is the national dish of Montserrat — a broth-based goat soup with the characteristic Montserratian combination of allspice, clove, cinnamon, and scotch bonnet. The papaya technique: Unripe papaya contains papain, a proteolytic enzyme that tenderises meat by breaking down collagen. Add grated unripe papaya to the marinade or braise — it virtually dissolves during cooking but its tenderising effect on the goat is significant. Brown goat with onion and garlic. Add water to cover generously — goat water is broth-forward. Add allspice, cloves, cinnamon, scotch bonnet, shadow beni, thyme, marjoram, grated unripe papaya. Simmer 2–3 hours until goat is tender. Add breadfruit cubes in the final 30 minutes — they absorb the broth and become dense and deeply flavoured.
💡 Goat water on a US menu is essentially unprecedented outside Montserratian diaspora communities. The story alone — national dish of Montserrat, the 1995 volcano that buried the entire capital city and forced two-thirds of the population to emigrate — is genuinely compelling. The unripe papaya as tenderiser is a technique guests will discuss at the table and remember for years.
The broth: clear amber, deeply flavoured with goat bones and the warm allspice-cinnamon-clove combination. The goat meat: yielding and mildly gamey, tender from the papain enzyme. The breadfruit: dense, starchy, absorbing every flavour note. The scotch bonnet: long, slow heat building across the bowl. A dish very few people in the US have tasted, which makes it unforgettable.
