// What a Michelin-trained chef sees in seafood fine casual
Seafood cookery has the narrowest margin for error of any protein category. A fish overcooked by 90 seconds is a different product. The window between raw and perfect on a piece of fresh branzino is measured in minutes — sometimes seconds. This is why seafood restaurants fail at higher rates than other categories: the technique requirement is non-negotiable and the sourcing dependency is real. Fresh fish within 48 hours is not a preference. It is the product. The beach shack fine casual format works because it resolves this tension: a limited menu built around the best of what came in that morning, executed at high technique, served in a relaxed environment.
// What this guide does
Coastal US markets are the fastest-growing segment for premium casual dining. The lobster roll at $28.99 with a 23.5% food cost is not expensive. It is correctly priced for what it is.
This guide builds the operation that earns that price point.

Entrées

Raw Oysters on the Half Shell
1. Raw Oysters on the Half Shell
Ticket Time: 4 min  |  Food Cost: 22%  |  Allergens: Molluscs
1
Source oysters from certified supplier — must be consumed within 48 hours of harvest date stamped on tag.
2
Store flat-side up on ice at 34–38°F. Never submerge in fresh water.
3
Shuck to order using a thick towel grip. Insert knife at hinge, twist, slide flat along top shell. Clean lip of any shell fragments.
4
Arrange 6 per service on crushed ice plate. Serve with mignonette, lemon wedge, and cocktail sauce.
⚑ Chef's Note: Always verify harvest tags before service. Any oyster that smells of ammonia or has a dry, receded body — discard immediately. No exceptions.
2. Ceviche — Citrus Cured Fish
Ticket Time: 6 min (cure pre-done)  |  Food Cost: 19%  |  Allergens: Fish
1
Begin cure at 07:00 — 4-hour minimum. Dice fresh white fish (sea bass or similar) to ½-inch cubes.
2
Combine with lime juice, lemon juice, finely diced red onion, serrano chile, salt. Cover and refrigerate.
3
At service: fold in diced avocado, cilantro, diced tomato. Taste and adjust salt/acid.
4
Serve chilled in a shallow bowl with plantain chips or tostadas on the side.
⚑ Chef's Note: Cure past 6 hours and fish texture becomes chalky. Pre-portion cure batches to match projected covers. Do not cure overnight.
3. Fish Tacos — Baja Style
Ticket Time: 8 min  |  Food Cost: 20%  |  Allergens: Fish, Gluten
1
Cut fresh cod or mahi-mahi into 2 oz strips. Season with cumin, smoked paprika, salt, lime zest.
2
Dredge in seasoned flour, dip in cold sparkling water batter, fry at 375°F for 3–4 min until golden.
3
Warm corn tortillas on flat-top 30 seconds each side. Build: shredded cabbage, chipotle crema, pico de gallo, fish, fresh cilantro, lime.
4
Serve 3 tacos per order with lime wedge and small cup of salsa verde.

Entrées (continued)

4. Grilled Corn — Elote Style
Ticket Time: 5 min  |  Food Cost: 11%  |  Allergens: Dairy
1
Grill corn on the cob over open flame or char-grill, turning every 90 seconds until charred in patches — approx 6 min total. Pre-grill in batch, finish to order.
2
Remove from heat. Brush generously with mayo-lime blend.
3
Roll in crumbled cotija cheese. Dust with chili powder, smoked paprika, and fine salt.
4
Finish with squeeze of fresh lime and chopped cilantro. Serve on corn holders.
⚑ Lowest food cost on the menu at 11%. This is your margin anchor. Push as a pair with oysters for table share.
5. Clam Chowder — House
Ticket Time: 6 min (base pre-made)  |  Food Cost: 18%  |  Allergens: Molluscs, Dairy, Gluten
1
Base made from 08:00 — clam juice, onion, celery, potatoes, thyme, white wine, cream, clam meat. Simmer 30 min, rest to develop.
2
At service: ladle 10 oz into sauce pot, heat gently. Do not boil — cream will break.
3
Verify potato is fork-tender. Adjust seasoning with white pepper and sea salt.
4
Serve in warm bowl with oyster crackers, chives, and a drizzle of quality olive oil.
6. Shrimp Cocktail
Ticket Time: 4 min  |  Food Cost: 21%  |  Allergens: Crustaceans
1
Poach U16/20 shrimp in court bouillon — celery, lemon, bay leaf, peppercorns — at 185°F for 3–4 min. Do not boil.
2
Ice-shock immediately. Peel and devein, leaving tail on.
3
Serve 5 per order draped over glass ramekin with house cocktail sauce (horseradish-forward) and lemon cheeks.
⚑ Pre-poach in batches during prep window. Store on ice. Never serve warm — shrimp cocktail is a cold dish. Temperature is the whole point.

★ SIGNATURE — Lobster Roll — Connecticut Style
1. Lobster Roll — Connecticut Style ★ SIGNATURE
Price: $28.99  |  Food Cost: 23.5%  |  Allergens: Crustaceans, Gluten

1
Steam live lobster at 212°F for 12 min per 1.25 lb. Shock in ice water. Extract tail and claw meat cleanly — no cartilage.
2
Cut into generous chunks. Keep cold until service. Do not shred — Connecticut style is whole pieces.
3
Butter-poach to order: melt unsalted butter in pan at 160°F (beurre monté), add lobster, warm gently 2–3 min. Season with sea salt and chive.
4
Toast split-top brioche bun in clarified butter until golden. Fill generously. No mayo — this is Connecticut, not Maine.
5
Serve with kettle chips and a lemon wedge. Keep it simple. The lobster is the star.
⚑ The $28.99 price is not a luxury markup — it is correct margin on a real product. Butter quality, brioche quality, and lobster freshness are all visible to the guest. Do not cut corners on any of the three.
2. Grilled Whole Fish ★ SIGNATURE
Ticket Time: 14 min  |  Food Cost: 23%  |  Allergens: Fish
1
Use branzino, dorade, or catch of the day (1–1.5 lb whole fish, scaled and gutted). Score skin 3x each side at 45°.
2
Season cavity with lemon slices, thyme, garlic, olive oil, salt. Exterior: olive oil, coarse sea salt, cracked pepper.
3
Grill skin-side down on oiled grate at 450°F for 7 min. Do not move. Flip carefully, grill 5–6 min. Internal temp 140°F.
4
Rest 2 min. Serve on platter with grilled lemon, herb oil, and seasonal roasted vegetables.
⚑ This is a tableside moment. Train servers to describe the fish at delivery — species, where it came from, how long ago it was caught. That story is part of the product.
7. Scallops & Brown Butter ★ SIGNATURE
Ticket Time: 10 min  |  Food Cost: 24%  |  Allergens: Molluscs, Dairy
1
Dry-pack U10 sea scallops. Pat completely dry with paper towels — moisture is the enemy of sear. Season with salt only. Do not pepper until after sear.
2
Heat stainless or cast iron pan until smoking. Add neutral oil. Scallops go flat-side down — do not move for 2.5 min. Look for golden crust creeping up the side.
3
Flip once. Add unsalted butter, baste for 90 seconds until brown and nutty. Internal 125°F.
4
Plate on cauliflower purée or pea purée. Drizzle brown butter, microgreens, sea salt finish.

Mains (continued)

3. Pan-Seared Salmon
Ticket Time: 10 min  |  Food Cost: 22%  |  Allergens: Fish
1
6 oz skin-on Atlantic salmon fillet. Bring to room temp 20 min before service. Score skin lightly to prevent curling.
2
Heat oil in pan until shimmering. Place skin-down, press lightly for first 10 seconds. Cook skin-side 6 min on medium-high — do not flip early.
3
Flip, add butter and thyme, baste 2 min. Rest 2 min. Internal 130°F for medium.
4
Serve skin-up on herb lemon cream sauce with wilted spinach and roasted fingerling potatoes.
4. Prawn Linguine
Ticket Time: 12 min  |  Food Cost: 20%  |  Allergens: Crustaceans, Gluten
1
Cook linguine al dente in salted water. Reserve 4 oz pasta water before draining.
2
Sauté garlic and chili flakes in olive oil. Add peeled prawns, cook 2 min per side.
3
Deglaze with white wine, reduce 1 min. Add pasta water, toss linguine in pan to emulsify sauce.
4
Finish with lemon zest, parsley, drizzle of EVOO. Season. Serve with crusty bread.
5. Shrimp Po'boy
Ticket Time: 8 min  |  Food Cost: 19%  |  Allergens: Crustaceans, Gluten
1
Season shrimp with Cajun spice, dredge in seasoned cornmeal flour, fry at 375°F until golden — 2.5 min.
2
Toast hoagie roll. Spread remoulade (mayo, hot sauce, capers, lemon, pickles). Add shredded lettuce, tomato slices.
3
Load fried shrimp generously, top with pickled jalapeño. Serve with seasoned fries or slaw.
6. Grilled Swordfish Steak
Ticket Time: 12 min  |  Food Cost: 24%  |  Allergens: Fish
1
8 oz swordfish steak, 1-inch thick. Marinate 30 min in olive oil, lemon, oregano, garlic.
2
Grill at 450°F: 4 min per side. Quarter-turn at 2 min for crosshatch. Do not overcook — swordfish dries out fast. Target 135°F internal.
3
Rest 3 min. Serve with salsa verde, grilled asparagus, and lemon caper butter.
8. Fish & Chips — House Batter
Ticket Time: 10 min  |  Food Cost: 18%  |  Allergens: Fish, Gluten
1
Batter: cold sparkling water, plain flour, pinch of turmeric, baking powder, salt. Keep batter cold — lumps are fine.
2
6 oz cod fillet, dredge in flour, coat in batter, fry at 375°F for 5–6 min until deeply golden and puffed.
3
Double-fry chips: blanch at 300°F, drain, finish at 375°F. Season with malt vinegar salt immediately.
4
Serve with house tartar sauce, mushy peas, and malt vinegar on the side.
⚑ Lowest food cost main at 18%. High volume expected. Ensure fryer oil is changed on schedule — degraded oil shows in batter colour and flavour immediately.

Mains (continued)

9. Tuna Poke Bowl
Ticket Time: 6 min  |  Food Cost: 22%  |  Allergens: Fish, Soy
1
Use sashimi-grade ahi tuna. Cube to ½-inch pieces. Toss in soy sauce, sesame oil, rice vinegar, ginger, scallion, chili flakes. Marinate max 10 min — acid will begin to cure longer than that.
2
Build bowl: sushi rice base (seasoned with rice vinegar, sugar, salt), tuna, edamame, cucumber ribbons, avocado fan, pickled radish.
3
Drizzle with sriracha mayo. Finish with sesame seeds and nori strips.
10. Crab Cake — Chesapeake Style
Ticket Time: 10 min  |  Food Cost: 23%  |  Allergens: Crustaceans, Gluten, Eggs
1
Use jumbo lump crabmeat. Binder: egg, mayo, Dijon, Old Bay, parsley, minimal breadcrumb. The crab is the star — do not drown it in filler.
2
Form into 4 oz patties. Refrigerate 30 min minimum to set. Pan-sear in butter at medium-high for 3 min per side — do not move until crust forms.
3
Finish in oven at 375°F for 4 min. Serve 2 per order on a pool of remoulade with microgreens and lemon.
⚑ Never use imitation crab. Chesapeake style means jumbo lump. A crab cake that is mostly filler is not a crab cake — it is a waste of a menu slot.

Service Scripts

These are trained scripts — not suggestions. Delivery should feel natural and unrehearsed, but the structure below is the baseline. Every server knows every script before first solo shift.

Script 01 — Welcome + Oyster Pitch
"Welcome to Beach Shack — I'm [name], I'll be taking care of you today. We're a seafood kitchen — everything came in this morning. While you look over the menu, can I start you off with some oysters? They're on the half shell, freshly shucked — they're one of the best things we do."
Script 02 — Lobster Roll Explanation ($28.99, Connecticut Style)
"The lobster roll is Connecticut style — that means butter-poached, not mayo-dressed. We steam live lobster in-house, extract the claw and tail meat whole, and warm it in beurre monté right before it hits the bun. It's $28.99 and it's the thing we're most proud of. A lot of places charge that and give you a roll that's half filler. Ours is just lobster and butter."
Script 03 — Fresh Fish Sourcing Story
"Our fish delivery happens at 6 in the morning, every morning. Whatever whole fish came in — that's what the chef builds the specials around. The [today's fish] came in from [local supplier / region] this morning — it was in the water yesterday. That's what we mean when we say fresh."
Script 04 — Dessert Pitch (Key Lime + Coconut Panna Cotta)
"Can I tell you about dessert? We make two in-house every morning — the key lime pie has a proper graham cracker crust and condensed lime filling, and the coconut panna cotta has a passion fruit coulis on top that sets overnight. They're both pretty light after a seafood meal. Worth it."
Script 05 — Return Visit Close
"Come back soon — the menu actually changes based on what comes in. The fish is different week to week, the specials shift with the catch. It's worth seeing what we're running next time you're in."
⚑ Scripts are trained, not read. If a server sounds like they're reciting from a card, retrain. The goal is informed confidence — knowing the product well enough to talk about it naturally.

Operations Standards

Opening Checklist

1
Receive fish delivery — inspect, log harvest tags, ice down within 10 minutes of arrival.
2
Start ceviche cure by 07:00. Mark start time on container.
3
Fire chowder base by 08:00. Mark production time on container.
4
Panna cotta sets by 09:00. Will be ready for dinner service.
5
All sauces made fresh: remoulade, cocktail sauce, chipotle crema, tartar, salsa verde.
6
Line temp check at 11:00. Sign the temperature log. Both hot and cold verified.
7
Brief front-of-house: today's catch, specials, any 86'd items. Takes 5 minutes. Worth every minute.

Closing Checklist

1
All remaining oysters: check harvest tags. Any opened or without valid tag — discard. No exceptions.
2
Remaining fish: wrap tightly, label with date and time, store on ice.
3
Sauces: refrigerate with date labels. Discard anything beyond shelf life.
4
Fryer oil: check colour and smell. Log status. Schedule change if needed.
5
Temperature log complete and signed by closing chef.
6
Revenue reconciliation: covers served vs expected, average spend check, any variance noted.
7
Confirm next-day fish order. Adjust based on tomorrow's reservation count and today's remaining stock.
