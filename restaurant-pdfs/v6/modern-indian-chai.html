// What a Michelin-trained chef sees in Indian cooking
Indian spice work is architecture. The sequence in which spices enter a dish determines the flavour as much as the spices themselves. Whole spices bloomed in hot oil release fat-soluble aromatics that ground spices cannot. The tadka — the tempering of mustard seeds, curry leaves and dried chili in hot oil that is poured over a finished dal — is not garnish. It is a second cooking stage that adds volatile aromatics lost in the initial cook. Masala chai brewed properly — whole spices simmered in water before milk is added, then boiled again together — produces a depth that a tea bag steeped in hot water cannot approach. The difference is chemistry. This guide teaches the chemistry.
// What this guide does
Indian food in America has been trapped in butter chicken and naan for thirty years. The operator who brings Bombay street food — vada pav, masala dosa, properly brewed chai — to a modern café format is entering a market with almost no direct competition. The food cost on masala chai is 11%. At 60 cups a day, that is a $266 daily gross margin on one drink.
This guide is the blueprint for the café that captures that market.

Pani Puri
Pani Puri — Mumbai Street Style
Prep: 5 min · FC: 12% · Gluten (shells) · Serves: 6 pieces
1
Arrange 6 hollow semolina shells on serving tray. Ensure shells are crisp — discard any soft ones.
2
Fill two small ramekins: one with tamarind-date chutney, one with spiced mint-coriander water (pani).
3
Prepare filling: spiced chickpeas + boiled potato, seasoned with chaat masala, cumin, black salt.
4
Present to table with verbal instruction: fill shell, add pani, eat in one bite.
💡 The pani temperature matters. Serve chilled — 4–6°C. The cold pani against room-temp shell creates the textural contrast the dish depends on.
Samosa — Punjabi Style
Prep: 6 min · FC: 14% · Contains: Gluten · Pre-assembled, fried to order
1
Pre-assembled samosas held refrigerated. Fry at 175°C for 4–5 min until golden and blistered.
2
Drain on rack 60 seconds. Do not paper-towel — steam softens the pastry.
3
Plate with green chutney and tamarind chutney. Dust with chaat masala.
💡 The Punjabi samosa is larger and more heavily spiced than the South Indian version. Filling: potato, peas, cumin, coriander, garam masala, dried mango powder (amchur).

Dal Makhani — Signature
Dal Makhani — 18-Hour Black Lentil
Prep: 12 min (day-of) · Total cook: 18 hrs · FC: 13% · Contains: Dairy · ⭐ House Signature
1
Night before (17:00): Soak whole black lentils (urad dal) + kidney beans 8 hrs. Pressure cook 45 min.
2
Transfer to heavy-bottomed pot. Add tomato purée, ginger-garlic paste, whole spices (cardamom, clove, bay). Simmer overnight on lowest heat — 10–12 hrs.
3
Morning check (06:00): Stir, check consistency. Lentils should be completely broken down and silky. Add butter and cream.
4
Continue slow simmer until service. Finish each portion to order: ladle into bowl, finish with 1 tsp butter, swirl of cream, dust of garam masala.
💡 The 18-hour cook is the product. Do not attempt to shortcut it. The Maillard reaction on the lentil proteins during the overnight simmer creates compounds that a 2-hour cook cannot replicate. This is your signature — protect it.

Masala Dosa — South Indian Crepe
Prep: 12 min · FC: 14% · Gluten-Free (rice/lentil batter) · Batter fermented overnight
1
Batter check (08:00): Fermented overnight rice-lentil batter should be aerated and slightly sour. Adjust consistency with water.
2
Heat cast iron griddle to 220°C. Wipe with oil-soaked cloth. Pour 150ml batter, spread in outward spiral to thin crepe.
3
Add spiced potato filling (masala) to centre. Drizzle ghee around edges. Cook until underside is golden-crisp.
4
Fold, plate with sambar (lentil broth) and three chutneys: coconut, tomato, green.
💡 The fermentation is non-negotiable. It provides the sour flavour and the bubbles that create the dosa's characteristic crisp-lacy texture. 8-hour minimum, 12-hour optimal.

Biryani — Chicken Dum
Prep: 15 min · FC: 20% · May contain nuts · Dum cooking method
1
Prep (11:00): Marinate chicken in yogurt, spices, saffron 2+ hrs. Par-cook basmati rice to 70% done.
2
Layer: chicken masala base → par-cooked rice → fried onions → mint → saffron milk → ghee.
3
Seal pot with dough or foil. Dum cook at 160°C 25 min. Hold in oven. Portion to order.
4
Serve with raita and mirchi ka salan (chili gravy). Garnish: fried onions, boiled egg.
💡 Dum means "breath" — the sealed steam cook. The dough seal is traditional and keeps all aromatics inside. The first lift of the lid at the table is the theatre.

Prep: 8 min · FC: 14% · Vegan base (butter optional)
1

2
Griddle-toast pav buns with generous butter until golden-crisp on cut side.
3
Plate bhaji topped with raw onion, coriander, lemon wedge, butter curl. Buns alongside.

Gulab Jamun — Sugar Syrup Dumplings
Prep: 3 min · FC: 9% · Contains: Dairy, Gluten · Pairs: Masala Chai
1
Hold gulab jamun in warm rose-cardamom sugar syrup 60°C. Do not boil — syrup will crystallise.
2
Plate 3 pieces per portion. Drizzle with syrup. Optional: small scoop of malai (clotted cream) alongside.
3
Always offer masala chai pairing. Use script: "The gulab jamun is best with the masala chai."
💡 FC 9% on a dessert is exceptional. At an average $6 sell price, gross margin per portion is $5.46. Push this dish at every dessert pitch.

Masala Chai — House Method
Masala Chai — Brewed In-House
$4.99 · FC: 11% · 60 cups/day = $266 daily gross margin
1
Spice base (07:00): Crush whole spices: green cardamom, black pepper, cinnamon stick, cloves, ginger (fresh). Toast dry 90 seconds.
2
Simmer crushed spices in water 95°C for 20 minutes. Add Assam tea leaves. Simmer 3 minutes more.
3
Add full-fat milk. Bring to boil, watching carefully — milk boils fast. Reduce, simmer 2 min.
4
Strain through fine mesh. Add sugar to taste. Serve in small steel cup (cutting chai style) or ceramic.
💡 This is not tea with milk added. The second boil after milk is critical — it extracts different compounds and rounds the spice profile. A bag steeped in hot water and splashed with milk is a different product entirely. Train staff to explain this distinction.

Service Scripts

These scripts are trained dialogue — not verbatim reads. Deliver naturally. The goal is to add context that makes the dish worth more to the guest.

Script 01 — Welcome + Masala Chai Pitch
"The chai is brewed in-house — whole spices simmered 20 minutes before the milk goes in. $4.99. It's not a bag in hot water. You'll taste the difference."
Script 02 — Pani Puri Table Experience
"The pani puri is interactive — we bring six hollow crisp shells and two chutneys, you fill them yourself and eat in one bite. It's street food the way it's eaten in Mumbai."
Script 03 — Dal Makhani Explanation
"The dal has been cooking since yesterday. 18 hours. Black lentils, butter, cream, tomato — it needs that time for the lentils to break down and absorb the spices. It's our best dish."
Script 04 — Dessert + Chai Pairing
"The gulab jamun is best with the masala chai — the sugar syrup and the spiced milk are made for each other. That's how it's served at weddings in India."
Script 05 — Return Visit
"Saturday mornings we do a full Bombay street breakfast — pav bhaji, vada pav, cutting chai. Different menu, different vibe. Worth coming back for."
💡 Script 02 (pani puri) and Script 03 (dal makhani) are your highest-value upsell moments. The interactive experience justifies a premium order. The 18-hour cook story converts first-timers into regulars — they return to verify the story is real.
