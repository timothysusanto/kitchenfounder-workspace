// What a Michelin-trained chef sees in French brasserie cooking
French classical technique is the foundation of every professional kitchen in the world — not because French food is superior, but because the French were the first to codify technique into a transferable system. Escoffier's brigade. The mother sauces. The stocks that take 8 hours because the collagen needs that long to convert to gelatin. A proper French onion soup takes 3 hours: 45 minutes to caramelise the onions properly — not brown them, caramelise them — then the stock, then the gratin. The version made in 20 minutes is not the same dish. The brasserie format demands this standard because its regulars remember the difference.
// What this guide does
The Parisian brasserie is the most democratic fine dining format ever created. Open all day. Welcoming everyone. The classics executed with precision and without ceremony. The croissant and flat white at $12.50 with a $0.82 food cost is the most efficient breakfast item in this collection. The steak frites at $28.99 closes dinner. When readers understand the all-day revenue model, the brasserie reveals itself as one of the most commercially intelligent formats in casual dining.
This guide is the blueprint.

1
Melt butter over low heat. Add sliced onions with a pinch of salt. Cook stirring every 5 min for 45 min until deeply caramelised — not brown, deeply amber and sweet.
2
Add white wine, deglaze, reduce by half. Add beef stock, thyme, bay leaf. Simmer 20 min. Season. Hold hot for service.
3
To order: ladle into oven-safe bowl. Float crouton on top. Pack with Gruyère.
4
Grill or salamander at 220°C until cheese is melted, bubbly and golden-brown. 3–4 min. Serve immediately on a plate with a napkin underneath.
💡 The caramelisation is the dish. Rush it and you get brown onion water. 45 minutes minimum — the sugars need time. Make the base the night before and it improves overnight.

1
Season bavette generously. Bring to room temp 30 min before service. Frites: cut uniform, blanch in oil at 140°C until soft, drain, hold. Second fry to order at 190°C for 3 min until golden.
2
Sear bavette on cast iron at high heat — 2 min each side for medium-rare. Add butter, thyme, garlic. Baste 90 seconds. Rest 3 min minimum.
3
Slice against the grain at 45°. Fan on plate with frites. Béarnaise in a small pot on the side. Watercress dressed with lemon.
💡 Bavette is a working muscle — it needs the rest. Cut too early and the juices run out. 3 minutes minimum. The béarnaise must be made fresh every service; never reheat.
Confit Duck Leg ⭐
Confit Duck Leg — Lentilles du Puy
10 min ticket (3 hr confit prep) · FC: 21% · $26.99 · SIGNATURE
IngredientQtyCost
Duck leg, skin-on1 whole$3.20
Duck fat (or blend)300ml to submerge$1.20
Lentilles du Puy, cooked120g$0.40
Lardon, shallot, Dijon30g / 20g / 5g$0.50
Thyme, garlic, bayaromatics$0.05
1
Salt duck legs overnight. Rinse, pat dry. Submerge fully in duck fat with aromatics. Cook at 80°C for 3 hours until meat pulls from bone.
2
Cool and refrigerate in fat — improves for 2 days. To order: remove from fat, skin-side down in hot dry pan. Render and crisp skin 4 min. Finish in oven at 200°C for 3 min.
3
Warm lentils with lardon, shallot, splash of red wine vinegar, Dijon. Plate lentils, duck leg on top, skin up.
💡 The fat can be reused up to 3 times if strained clean. The duck improves significantly after 48 hours in fat — batch cook Tuesday for Thursday/Friday service.

Crème Brûlée — Vanilla, Torch to Order
2 min ticket · FC: 10% · $9.99
IngredientQtyCost
Double cream180ml$0.50
Egg yolks3$1.05
Caster sugar45g + 15g brûlée$0.10
Vanilla bean1/4 pod$0.35
1
Heat cream with vanilla to 80°C. Whisk yolks with sugar until pale. Temper cream in slowly. Strain. Pour into ramekins.
2
Bake bain-marie at 150°C for 35 min until just set with slight wobble in centre. Refrigerate minimum 4 hours.
3
To order: dust with 5mm even layer of caster sugar. Torch in circular motion until golden caramel crust. No brown spots — even coverage. Serve immediately.
💡 The crust should crack cleanly — if it bends, the sugar layer is too thin or the torch was too fast. Tell your team: if in doubt, redo it. It takes 30 seconds.

SECTION 5 — SERVICE TRAINING

Script 01 — Welcome + Kir Royale
"We open every table with the option of a kir royale — cassis, champagne. $13.99. It's how the French start dinner. If you'd prefer, the pastis is also very good — anise spirit with cold water, very traditional. Both are on the menu."
Script 02 — Steak Tartare Table Assembly
"The tartare is assembled at the table — egg yolk, capers, cornichons, Dijon, shallot. We season it in front of you. Takes 3 minutes. Worth watching. If you'd like it spicier I can add more Tabasco. Your call."
Script 03 — Confit Duck Explanation
"The duck has been in its own fat since this morning. Three hours at 80°C. It's the most classical French technique — the fat preserves the moisture completely. You can't replicate it with a faster method. The skin gets crisped to order."
Script 04 — Crème Brûlée Theatre
"The brûlée is torched to order — you'll hear it from the kitchen. The crust should crack cleanly when you tap it. If it doesn't, send it back. We'll redo it. Takes 30 seconds."
Script 05 — Return Visit + Plat du Jour
"The plat du jour changes daily — whatever came in fresh, cooked the French way. It's always the best value on the menu and it changes every day. Worth coming back for."
