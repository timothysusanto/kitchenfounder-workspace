About This Guide: Vietnamese cuisine is one of the fastest-growing restaurant categories in the United States, driven by pho's crossover appeal and bánh mì's perfect price-to-value ratio as a premium sandwich. The cuisine's commercial strength lies in its health perception (lighter broths, fresh herbs, no heavy cream or butter), the theatrical tableside assembly of pho, and an exceptionally low food cost on the two anchor items — pho broth (bones cost $2.50/lb, yield is massive) and bánh mì (baguette at $0.45, filling cost under $2.00). The café model runs a breakfast-through-lunch window, with the pho broth as an 18-hour overnight asset that costs almost nothing in active labour. This guide covers the essential phở, bánh mì, and Vietnamese café repertoire with complete recipe cards, US market costing, and the operational structure to make it consistently profitable.
// Chef's Lens
Perspective · 01
// What a Michelin-trained chef sees in Vietnamese cooking
Vietnamese pho broth is an 18-hour argument for patience in a world that optimises for speed. The bones are blanched first — this is non-negotiable — to remove the impurities that would cloud the stock and dull the flavour. Then the charring: onion and ginger directly over the gas flame until blackened outside, soft inside. That char creates a smoky sweetness that no other technique replicates. The spice packet goes in after the beef foundation is built. Star anise, cinnamon, cloves, coriander, cardamom — added too early and they overwhelm; added at the right moment and they complete. This is not a recipe. It is a sequence that took generations to calibrate.

Method

1
Briefly dip rice paper in warm water (3–4 seconds) — it should still feel slightly firm. Lay flat on a damp surface. It will soften fully within 30 seconds. Over-soaking creates tears and sticky, gummy rolls.
2
Place halved shrimp pink-side down along the lower third of the paper. Lay thin pork slices beside them. Top with vermicelli, lettuce, herbs, and bean sprouts. Do not overfill — tight, compact rolls hold their shape and photograph well.
3
Fold the sides in first, then roll tightly from the bottom up. The shrimp should show through the translucent wrapper — this visual is a key selling point. Keep covered with a damp cloth until service.
Chef's Tip: Gỏi cuốn sell price is $10.99 with a food cost under $1.60 — one of the highest-margin items on the menu. Make them beautiful: visible shrimp halves and bright green herbs through the wrapper drive social media sharing and reorder rates.

Method

1
Mix ground pork, glass noodles, mushroom, shallot, garlic, and carrot. Season with fish sauce, white pepper, and a pinch of sugar. The filling should be moist but not wet — squeeze out any excess moisture before rolling.
2
Place 30g filling on wrapper, roll tightly, seal with water or egg wash. Rolls can be frozen in batches after rolling — pull from freezer directly to fryer.

Batch Tip: Chả giò are made to freeze. Prepare 200–300 rolls at a time, freeze on trays, then bag. Pull to order. This is your best prep efficiency play — 2 hours of rolling yields a week of service.

Method

1
Batter: rice flour, turmeric, coconut milk, scallion, water to thin. Rest 30 min. Heat a 26cm non-stick pan until smoking. Add oil, swirl to coat.
2
Add shrimp and pork to the hot pan, cook briefly. Pour in 80ml batter, tilt to spread thin. The pan should sizzle loudly — that's the bánh xèo (sizzling cake) sound. Pile bean sprouts on one half.
3
Cover and cook 2 minutes. Remove lid — cook another 2 minutes uncovered until bottom is crisp and lacy. Fold in half over the sprouts. Serve immediately — the crêpe softens within 3 minutes.

Method

1
Day-old rice is mandatory — fresh rice has too much moisture and clumps. Break up cold rice with your hands before wokking. High heat only. The wok must be screaming hot before oil goes in.
2
Oil in, swirl, add rice — spread and let sit 30 seconds (creates the slight crust). Crack eggs directly in, scramble quickly. Toss rice with egg to coat. Season with soy and fish sauce. Finish with scallion and sesame oil off heat.
