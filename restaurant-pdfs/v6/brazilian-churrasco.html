Chef's Lens

// The Philosophy of Churrasco
Brazilian churrasco is not a technique — it is a ceremony. The churrasceiro is not merely a cook; they are a keeper of fire, a sculptor of smoke, and a guardian of tradition stretching back centuries to the gauchos of Rio Grande do Sul. Each cut of meat tells a different story: the rich marbling of picanha, the slow-rendered tenderness of costela, the snap of linguiça skin over open flame.

This guide exists to protect that story. Every prep time, every temperature guide, every service script is in service of one goal: when a guest sits at your table, they should feel they are in Brazil — not a facsimile of it.

Fire is your primary ingredient. Respect it. Learn it. Master it. Everything else follows.
— Execute with intention. Every plate is a performance.
// Core Operational Principles
1. Fire First: The churrasco fire must be started 90 minutes before service. Hardwood charcoal only — no lighter fluid, ever.

2. Salt Discipline: Picanha takes coarse rock salt only. Applied at skewer, not marinade. Shaken off before service.

3. Costela Commitment: Short ribs enter the slow cooker at 09:00 without exception. 7-hour cook is non-negotiable.

4. The Skewer Technique: Hold skewer vertically at table height. Slice thin against the grain directly onto guest's plate. Control the pour — guests signal with palm-down (stop) or palm-up (more).

5. Feijoada Fridays: Begin feijoada by 11:00. It cannot be rushed. Pre-sell begins Thursday evening service.

1
What is cachaça: Cachaça is Brazilian sugarcane spirit — distilled directly from fresh sugarcane juice (not molasses like rum). The result is greener, more vegetal, and more complex than white rum. The Caipirinha is Brazil's national cocktail. It is not a mojito. The crushed ice is structural — it dilutes and chills simultaneously as you drink.
2
Muddle properly: Quarter the lime (skin on), place in a rocks glass. Add sugar. Muddle firmly — you want to extract the juice AND express the oils from the skin. The lime oil from the skin is what gives the Caipirinha its aromatic complexity. Do not strain — the lime pieces stay in the glass.
3
Build and serve: Add crushed ice to fill the glass. Pour cachaça over. Stir 5–6 times from the bottom. The drink should be cloudy from the lime juice and slightly sweet with the tartness of fresh citrus. Serve immediately — crushed ice dilutes quickly. Suggest with any of the grilled meats: "The lime in the Caipirinha cuts through the beef fat perfectly."

EOD log: Temperatures, waste, and incidents logged every close. No log, no close sign-off.
