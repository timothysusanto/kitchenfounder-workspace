About This Guide: Greek meze is one of the most commercially intelligent formats — sharing plates, high margin dips, batch-friendly prep, olive oil-forward cuisine with strong US appeal driven by the Mediterranean diet movement. The taverna format rewards operators who understand the rhythm of the Greek table: cold meze arrive first, hot dishes follow in waves, the table is never empty. This guide covers the essential Greek taverna repertoire — from tzatziki to slow-roasted lamb shoulder — with complete recipe cards, US 2025 market costing, kitchen prep flow, and the business fundamentals to run a profitable 42-seat mezedes operation.
// What a Michelin-trained chef sees in Greek cooking
Greek cuisine is built on a philosophy that Western fine dining spent decades trying to rediscover: that great ingredients, treated with restraint, produce better food than complex technique applied to mediocre produce. Horiatiki — the village salad — is tomato, cucumber, feta, olive oil, oregano. No dressing beyond the oil. No lettuce. The feta is a block, not crumbled, because the Greeks understood that texture is part of the dish. The olive oil is poured generously because in Greek cooking, olive oil is not a condiment. It is the cuisine. The cold meze advantage is real: tzatziki, taramosalata and hummus all improve after 24 hours in the fridge. Your prep is done the night before. Your morning is calm.

Method

1
The foundation of tzatziki is properly drained yoghurt. Use full-fat Greek yoghurt (strained) — the higher fat content produces a richer, creamier base. If your yoghurt is not thick enough, strain overnight in cheesecloth. A watery tzatziki is a failed tzatziki regardless of other flavours. The texture should be thick enough to hold a spoon upright.
2
Grate cucumber on the large holes of a box grater. Salt generously and let sit 10 minutes, then squeeze aggressively in a clean kitchen towel — you want to remove 60–70% of the moisture. Undrained cucumber is the most common tzatziki error: it releases water into the yoghurt within minutes, turning a thick dip into a watery soup by the time it reaches the table.
3
Combine yoghurt, drained cucumber, minced garlic, fresh dill, lemon juice, and half the olive oil. Fold gently — do not whip. Season with salt. Make this at least 2 hours before service, ideally the day before. The resting time allows the garlic and dill flavours to fully infuse the yoghurt. Fresh tzatziki is good; overnight tzatziki is excellent.
4
Serve in a shallow bowl or on a small plate. Make a shallow well with the back of a spoon and pour the remaining olive oil into the well — this presentation is traditional and keeps the oil visible and fresh. Garnish with a sprig of fresh dill and a dusting of dried oregano. Warm pita is essential: cold or stale pita is a false economy that undermines the entire experience.
Chef's Tip: Tzatziki is your highest-volume cold meze. Make a large batch daily — it holds perfectly for 3 days refrigerated. The day-two and day-three tzatziki is actually better than day-one as the garlic mellows and the flavours integrate. This is your most profitable dish: $1.25 cost on an $8.99 sell. Every table will order it. Never run out.

Method

1
Soak white bread (crustless) in cold water for 5 minutes. Squeeze out excess moisture completely — the bread acts as an emulsifying base. The bread's starch absorbs the oil during blending and creates the creamy, smooth texture of authentic taramosalata. Without bread, the dip separates into an oily, grainy mess.
2
In a food processor, blend tarama and grated onion until combined. Add squeezed bread and pulse. Begin adding olive oil in a slow, steady stream — exactly as you would make mayonnaise. The emulsification requires patience: add oil too fast and the mixture breaks. Too slow and it over-emulsifies into a paste. A slow, steady pour over 2 minutes produces the ideal silky consistency.
3
Alternate between olive oil and lemon juice — a tablespoon of each at a time — adjusting balance as you go. Taste constantly. Authentic taramosalata should be briny, rich, lemony, and slightly sharp from the raw onion. It should never taste overwhelmingly fishy or overly oily. The bread absorbs excess oil and moderates the flavour intensity.
4
Serve chilled in a shallow bowl with olive oil drizzled across the top and a black olive garnish. Taramosalata holds refrigerated for 4 days. Like tzatziki, it improves overnight as flavours meld. The onion sharpness mellows beautifully after 12 hours. Make daily, serve the day-old version — it is superior to fresh.
Chef's Tip: Source your tarama from a Greek or Middle Eastern specialty supplier. The best tarama is pale pink — heavily dyed bright-red product is inferior quality with artificial colouring. White tarama is smoked cod roe and has a more delicate flavour than pink carp roe; both are correct. Keep tarama refrigerated and use within 4 days of opening.
