Method — Kaya Jam (Double Boiler)

1
Blend pandan leaves with coconut milk. Strain firmly — you want deep green coconut milk. Whisk eggs and sugar together until sugar dissolves. Combine with pandan coconut milk in a heatproof bowl.
2
Set over a pot of simmering water (double boiler). Stir constantly with a silicone spatula. Cook 35–45 minutes until thick enough to coat the back of a spoon and a line drawn through holds. Do not rush with direct heat — you'll scramble the eggs. Cool completely. Refrigerate up to 2 weeks.

3

Method — Dough

1
Mix flour, salt, sugar, condensed milk, and warm water into a soft dough. Knead 8 minutes until smooth. Divide into 50g balls. Coat each ball generously in ghee. Cover loosely — rest at room temperature minimum 4 hours, overnight preferred. The long rest relaxes the gluten so the dough can be stretched paper-thin without tearing.
2
On a lightly oiled flat surface, press a dough ball flat. Stretch outward from the centre using the back of your fingers until it's almost translucent — about 35cm wide. Fold into thirds, then thirds again to create a square. Rest 2 minutes. For egg prata: crack an egg onto stretched dough before folding.
3
Cook on a flat top or heavy pan with a thin layer of ghee. Medium-high heat, 2 minutes each side until puffed and golden-brown with dark spots. Clap between both hands immediately after cooking — this fluffs up the layers. Serve with warm fish curry dip.

Three Sauces (batch)

SauceIngredients
Ginger sauce60g ginger + 20g spring onion + 5g salt — blended, then fried in 60ml hot oil
Chilli sauce6 red chillies + 3 garlic + 20ml lime juice + 10ml ginger juice + 5ml sugar + salt — blended
Dark soy drizzle60ml dark soy + 15ml sesame oil + 8g sugar — warm to dissolve
Make all 3 sauces daily. The chicken rice plate lives or dies by the quality of these three sauces. Do not use commercial versions — they are instantly identifiable. The ginger sauce especially should taste fiery, fresh, and aromatic. If customers are asking for more sauce, you've got it right.

Method — The Poach

1
Bring water to a full boil with ginger, spring onion, and salt. Lower whole chicken in breast-side down. Return to boil, then immediately reduce to the lowest possible simmer — barely a bubble. Poach 35–40 minutes (for a 1.7kg bird). Internal temp thigh must reach 74°C.
2
Remove chicken and submerge immediately in ice water for 10 minutes. This shocks the skin into a silky, slightly gelatinous texture — the signature of good chicken rice. Brush lightly with sesame oil and dark soy. Reserve all poaching stock.
3
Chicken Rice: Fry garlic and ginger in a little oil 1 minute. Add washed rice, stir-fry 2 minutes to coat in oil. Transfer to rice cooker. Add 700ml of the reserved poaching stock per 500g rice (ratio 1.4:1 stock to rice). Add pandan leaves and sesame oil. Cook normally. Rest 10 minutes before serving.
4
Service: slice chicken against the grain into 8mm pieces. Portion 150g chicken per serve. Add 180g chicken rice, a side of sliced cucumber, a small bowl of clear chicken broth (diluted poaching stock). Serve all 3 sauces alongside.

Method

Method

1
Pandan jelly: Whisk all jelly ingredients cold until smooth. Cook over medium heat, stirring constantly, until it thickens and pulls away from the pan — about 5 minutes. Pandan Coconut Layer Cake (Kueh Lapis)
Steamed layered coconut milk cake · Pandan + white alternating · Slice to order
Steam: 90 min totalYield: 16 slicesService: 1 minSell: $7.99 / slice
Ingredient (1 tray = 16 slices)AmountCost
Rice flour200g$0.20
Tapioca flour50g$0.08
Coconut milk (full fat)600ml$1.80
Caster sugar180g$0.16
Salt3g—
Pandan juice (fresh or extract)100ml$0.20
Green food colouring (optional, 2 drops)—$0.01

Method

1
Whisk rice flour, tapioca flour, sugar, salt, and coconut milk until completely smooth. Divide batter in half. Add pandan juice and food colouring to one half (green). Leave the other plain white. You now have two batters.
2
Grease a 20cm square tin. Steam the tin empty for 2 minutes to heat it. Pour 80ml of white batter into the tin. Steam over high heat for 5 minutes until set. Add 80ml green batter. Steam 5 minutes. Repeat alternating layers until batter is used — approximately 8 layers total. Each layer must be fully set before the next goes in. Do not rush.
3
After final layer, steam 10 more minutes. Cool completely in the tin at room temperature — minimum 2 hours. Do not refrigerate while warm. Once cold, unmould. Slice with a sharp wet knife — wipe between each cut for clean layers. Refrigerate. Serve cold or room temperature.

Salt2g—
Fresh coconut is better but desiccated coconut moistened with a little water works in a café setting. If using desiccated, mix with 2 tbsp water and a pinch of salt, microwave 30 seconds to warm and soften. It looks and tastes far better than dry desiccated.

Method

1
Mix glutinous rice flour with pandan juice to form a soft, pliable dough. If too dry, add boiling water tablespoon by tablespoon. Dough should be soft like earlobe — not sticky, not cracking.
2
Divide dough into 10g balls. Flatten each. Place 4g grated gula melaka in the centre. Seal firmly — no cracks, no openings. Roll into a smooth ball. The filling must be completely enclosed or it leaks out in the water.
3
Drop balls into simmering (not boiling) water. They are done when they float to the surface plus 30 extra seconds. Drain and immediately roll in salted coconut. Serve the same day — ondeh ondeh do not keep well overnight as the dough hardens.

5Staff Structure — 2 Kitchen + 1 Steward

Why the 2-person kitchen works here: Singapore café food is built for efficiency. Chicken rice is poached once, portioned all service. Laksa broth simmers in one pot. Char kway teow is the only wok-to-order dish. Rendang, BKT, and fish curry are all batch-cooked the day before. Your kitchen is an assembly line during service — not a cooking station.
