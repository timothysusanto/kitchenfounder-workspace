Method — Step by Step

1
In a food processor, pulse cold butter with flour and icing sugar — 8 short pulses only. It should look like fine breadcrumbs. If you pulse too long, pastry becomes tough.
2
Add egg yolk and cold water. Pulse 4 more times until dough just starts coming together. Tip onto a floured bench, press into a flat disc, wrap in cling film. Refrigerate 30 minutes minimum.
3
Roll pastry to 3mm thick. Cut circles 1.5cm wider than your tart moulds. Press gently into greased tins — do not stretch. Chill again 15 minutes. Blind bake at 170°C fan with weights for 10 minutes. Remove weights, bake 5 more minutes until base is dry. Set aside.
4
Warm milk to 50°C — just hot to touch, not boiling. Dissolve sugar in milk. Cool to 35°C (lukewarm). Whisk in eggs gently — no foam. Strain through a fine sieve twice. Pour into shells to 85% full.
5
Bake at 160°C fan for 18–22 minutes. They're done when edges are set but centre still wobbles like jelly. Do not overbake — a wobble in the oven means perfect custard on the plate. Internal temp: 72°C. Cool on a wire rack. Serve warm or room temp. Never refrigerate — pastry goes soggy.
💡 Profit tip: Bake one batch at 7am and one at 11am. Display on the counter — visible product sells itself. At $8.99 for 3 pieces with a $0.24 ingredient cost, this is your best-performing item per dollar spent.

Method — Dough

1
Mix flours, yeast, sugar, salt in a stand mixer. Add warm milk at 35°C and egg. Mix on low with dough hook 2 minutes. Increase to medium, knead 8 minutes until smooth. Do not add butter yet.
2
Add soft butter in 3 small additions, mixing well between each. Total butter time: about 6 minutes. Finished dough should be smooth and slightly tacky but not sticky. Cover and rest at room temp for 60 minutes until doubled.
3
Knock back dough. Divide into 12 equal pieces (about 65g each). Roll each into a smooth ball. Place on lined baking trays. Cover. Proof 30 more minutes at warm room temp.

Method — Topping

4
Cream butter and icing sugar until pale. Mix in egg yolk. Fold in cake flour, custard powder, milk powder until it forms a soft paste. Divide into 12 portions. Roll each between cling film to a 7cm circle. Refrigerate 15 minutes until firm.
5
Drape one topping disc over each proofed bun. Score a crosshatch pattern on top with the back of a knife — this is the signature look. Bake at 190°C fan for 14–16 minutes until golden. Cool 5 minutes. Split open and place a 25g cold butter block inside while still warm. Serve immediately.

Mango Pomelo Sago (楊枝甘露)

Method

1
Sago: Boil 3L water. Add sago in a thin stream, stir immediately. Cook 12–15 minutes, stirring every 2 minutes, until 95% translucent with only a tiny white dot remaining. Remove from heat, cover, rest 5 minutes — the dot disappears. Drain through a fine sieve. Rinse under cold water until completely cold. Store covered in cold water. Use within 24 hours — sago hardens beyond this.
2
Mango base: Blend mango flesh until completely smooth. Strain through a fine sieve. Add coconut cream and orange juice. Blend briefly. Taste — if mango is sweet and ripe, no sugar needed. Chill minimum 2 hours. Serve at 4°C.
3
Per serve: drain 40g sago. Place in a chilled bowl. Ladle 180ml cold mango base over sago. Top with 25g diced mango and 18g pomelo. Garnish with mint. Serve immediately — do not let it sit.
💡 Mango tip: Only use Ataulfo (Champagne) mangoes — fibre-free and intensely sweet. Avoid Tommy Atkins. Taste every batch before service and adjust sweetness with sugar only if needed. A great mango needs nothing added.

Double Skin Milk Pudding (雙皮奶)
Traditional steamed whole milk pudding • Silky texture • Make ahead daily
Yield: 8 bowlsPrep: 20 minSteam: 12 min + chillSell: $8.99
Ingredient (8 portions)AmountCost
Whole milk (full fat, 3.5%+)600ml$0.66
Egg whites3 pieces$0.42
Caster sugar50g$0.05

Method

1
Heat milk to 70°C — hot but not boiling. Pour into individual bowls. Leave undisturbed for 10 minutes — a thin skin forms on the surface. This is the first skin.
2
Gently pierce the skin on one side with a toothpick. Slowly pour the milk back into your mixing bowl — the skin stays in the bowl. Do this carefully. Dissolve sugar in the warm milk. Cool to 40°C.
3
Whisk egg whites lightly — do not create foam or stiff peaks. Strain into the milk. Mix gently. Strain entire mixture through a fine sieve. Slowly pour back into each bowl over the first skin. The skin will float back up.
4
Steam over medium-low heat for 12–14 minutes. The pudding is set when edges are firm but centre has a gentle wobble. Internal temp: 72°C. Cool, refrigerate. Serve cold or warm — both are correct.

Heavy cream (35% fat)500ml$2.20
Full fat milk200ml$0.22

Caster sugar90g$0.08

Method

1
Heat cream and milk to 85°C — do not boil. Add tea, steep covered for 12 minutes. The colour should be deep amber. Strain firmly, pressing the leaves. Stir in condensed milk while still warm. Cool to 40°C.
2
Whisk egg yolks and sugar until pale — do not create foam. Air bubbles appear as pits on the finished surface. Add vanilla. Pour cooled cream mixture into yolks slowly while whisking. Strain through a fine sieve twice. Skim any surface foam.

4
At service: blot surface dry. Sprinkle 8g caster sugar evenly. Brûlée with a blowtorch in circular motion keeping flame 5–7cm from surface until amber and crackled. Serve within 3 minutes — crust softens quickly.

🍵 HK Milk Tea (港式奶茶) — Hot or Iced
$5.50 hot / $6.00 iced

The most iconic HK café drink. Strong, silky, slightly bitter Ceylon tea pulled through a stocking strainer and finished with evaporated milk. Cannot be replicated at home — this is your anchor beverage.

Ingredients (1 serve)

IngredientAmountCost

Sugar (optional, to guest preference)5–10g$0.01

Method

1
Steep tea leaves in freshly boiled water for exactly 5 minutes. Strain through a fine mesh or cloth strainer. The tea should be very dark and strong — almost opaque.
2
Pull the tea — pour brewed tea through a fine strainer between two jugs from a height of 30cm, back and forth 3 times. This aerates the tea and creates a silky texture.
3
Add evaporated milk. For hot: pour into a pre-warmed cup. For iced: pour over ice in a tall glass. Never use fresh milk — only evaporated milk gives the authentic creaminess.

5Staff Structure — 2 Kitchen + 1 Steward

The 2-person kitchen model works because: Every dish in this guide is designed for batch preparation. Proteins marinate overnight. Broth simmers the night before. Wontons are frozen ahead. Sauces are made weekly. At service, the kitchen is assembling and finishing — not cooking from scratch. This is the system that allows 2 people to run a profitable service.
