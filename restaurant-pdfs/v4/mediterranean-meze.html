Table Turn Strategy

Lunch Service (1.5 Turns)

Sharing format naturally extends table time to ~75 min. Drive turns with prompt ordering, pre-set mezze boards as table arrivals, and streamlined payment. Target: 45 seats × 1.5 = 67.5 covers at lunch.

Dinner Service (1.2 Turns)

Dinner guests linger longer — 90–100 min is typical. Maximise spend per head with upsell on sharing mains, dessert pairs, and signature drinks. Target: $65+ per head at dinner.

Why Sharing Format Drives Revenue

Sharing tables consistently spend 25–35% more than individual ordering. When guests order for the table, anchoring bias kicks in — each person feels less responsible for the total. The Mezze Board ($22.99) plus one main per person at $25 avg = $47.99 before drinks. Add two drinks at $6 = $59.99 per head. Dessert upsell gets you to $65+.

Menu Storytelling — What to Say When Describing the Menu

Entrées

"Our entrées are made for the middle of the table — everything here is designed to share and start the conversation. The Mezze Board is the best way to kick things off; it gives you a taste of our house dips, and it comes with warm pita straight from the oven."

Mains

"The mains range from light and fresh — like the fattoush chicken plate — to full celebration dishes like our 6-hour lamb shoulder, which we'd recommend for two. Our chef's personal favourites are the grilled octopus and the prawn saganaki."

Desserts

"Our desserts come from across the Mediterranean — the Knafeh is warm, cheesy, and a bit addictive; the malabi is light and floral if you want something delicate. Both pair beautifully with a Turkish coffee."

Drinks

"All our drinks are non-alcoholic and inspired by the region — the Jallab is our most visual drink, deep red with pine nuts floating on top; the Limonana is fresh blended mint lemonade that guests keep ordering back. The Turkish coffee is a must to finish."

How to Answer "What's Good Here?"

"Honestly? If it's your first time, get the Mezze Board to start — it gives you a feel for the kitchen. Then I'd point you toward the Lamb Shoulder if there are two of you, or the Prawn Saganaki for something a bit more theatrical. And don't leave without trying the Knafeh — it's warm cheese pastry with rose water syrup and it's genuinely one of the best things we make."

Upsell Scripts

Script 1 — The Pairing
You've got the lamb kofta coming — can I suggest our Limonana to go alongside? It's a frozen mint lemonade and the citrus cuts straight through the spice. Most people order a second one.
Why it works: Flavour pairing creates a logical reason to add a drink. "Most people order a second one" uses social proof to pre-sell the repeat. Mention the drink name specifically — vague suggestions don't convert.
Script 2 — The Upgrade
The Mezze Board comes standard, but for just $4 more we can add a portion of our house labneh with za'atar oil — it's made fresh this morning and honestly it's the thing I'd add if it were my table.
Why it works: Personal endorsement ("if it were my table") creates authenticity. Small price increment ($4) feels trivial against a $55 spend. Freshness cue ("made this morning") adds perceived value.
Script 3 — The Dessert Plant
Here are your mains — enjoy! Just so you know, we have Knafeh on tonight — it's our warm cheese pastry with rose water syrup and it sells out most nights, so if you're thinking dessert, I'd let me know before the kitchen closes it off.
Why it works: Scarcity ("sells out most nights") creates urgency without pressure. Planting the idea at mains arrival means guests are thinking dessert while they still have appetite. No selling — just informing.
Script 4 — The Share Suggestion
Are you thinking of sharing starters? The Mezze Board and the Grilled Halloumi together is probably the best opening to the meal — they complement each other perfectly and it's how most of our regulars start.
Why it works: Suggesting two starters instead of one increases the entrée spend without feeling pushy. "How most of our regulars start" uses belonging and social proof to make the choice feel natural and endorsed.
Script 5 — The Regular Builder
Did you enjoy the lamb shoulder? That's one of our weekend specials — we do it differently each season. We'd love to have you back for the next one. If you follow us on Instagram we put the menu up on Thursdays.
Why it works: Connects a dish they loved to future visits with a reason to return. Instagram follow turns a one-time guest into a warm lead for every future special. Low-friction — no loyalty card, no sign-up, just a follow.

Objection Scripts

"It's a bit expensive..."
Staff: "I completely get that — and honestly, the best way to eat here is sharing, which makes it really good value. If two of you share the Mezze Board, one main each, and split a dessert, you're looking at around $35 each. The lamb shoulder for two at $32.99 is our best value by far." Redirect from price to value-per-experience.
"I'm not that hungry..."
Staff: "No problem at all — our menu is actually designed for that. The mezze format means you can order two or three small plates and graze. The falafel, halloumi, and a dip board would make a really lovely lighter meal without feeling like too much." Turn low appetite into a positive — the sharing format is the answer.
"Just water for me..."
Staff: "Of course! Can I just tell you about our Limonana quickly — it's a frozen fresh mint lemonade and it's non-alcoholic, around $6.50. It's genuinely one of the most refreshing things on the menu and guests often switch from water once they try it. Would you like a small taste?" Offer a sample if possible. Never push — offer once, offer gently.
"We're quite rushed..."
Staff: "No worries at all — I'll make sure you're looked after quickly. Can I suggest the Shakshuka and the Lamb Kofta? They're both out in under 15 minutes. I'll put them through as priority and check on you every few minutes. How much time do you have?" Take control, offer a solution, and set expectations. Rushed guests who have a good quick experience become regulars.

Customer Retention System
Turn first visits into lifelong regulars

The Regulars Notebook

What to Write Down

  • First name (and last if given)
  • Usual table preference (window, corner, outside)
  • Favourite dishes ordered
  • Dietary notes (GF, vegan, allergies)
  • How they take their coffee
  • Whether they come alone, with partner, with kids
  • Anything personal they mentioned (birthday, anniversary)
  • What they said they'd come back for

When to Write It

Write notes immediately after the guest leaves — never during service in front of them. Use a physical notebook kept at the host station or a shared note in your POS. Update after every visit. Review before each shift: scan for returning faces from the last 7 days.


Natural Conversation Starters to Learn Their Name

  • "Did you want to give your name for the table?" (at booking)
  • "I'll bring that to your card — what name shall I put?" (at payment)
  • "I'm [name] by the way — welcome back!" (they usually reciprocate)
  • After they pay by card: glance at name, use it at departure

The 3-Visit Rule

1
Remember the Face
Make eye contact and greet warmly on arrival. Note table, rough time of visit, and what they ordered. Even "couple, lunch, mezze board + lamb" is enough to start.
2
Remember the Name
On second visit, recognise them: "Welcome back!" Find their name naturally through payment or casual chat. Write it down tonight. Never fake familiarity you don't have.
3
Know Their Usual
By the third visit, greet by name and have a suggestion ready: "The lamb shoulder is on today — I remembered you loved it last time." This is the moment they become regulars for life.

VIP Micro-Moments — 5 Small Gestures That Create Loyalty

🫓
The Pita Refill (Without Being Asked)
When you see the bread basket getting low during a mezze meal, bring fresh warm pita before they have to ask. Say: "I noticed the pita was running low — I've brought you some more." This single gesture gets mentioned in more reviews than almost anything else. Cost: near zero. Impact: disproportionate.
☕
The Birthday Coffee
If a guest mentions they're celebrating a birthday or anniversary, bring a complimentary Turkish coffee or Sahlab at the end — no announcement, no fuss. Just set it down and say: "Compliments of the kitchen — happy birthday." One small gesture turns a dining memory into a story they'll tell.
🌿
The Dietary Memory
When a returning guest has a dietary need (GF, vegan, nut allergy) and they don't remind you — you remember and inform them proactively: "Just so you know, the new seasonal side tonight is vegan — I thought you might want to try it." Shows you were paying attention. Builds profound trust.
📸
The Photo Offer
When the Lamb Shoulder Sharer or Mezze Vegetarian Feast arrives at a table that looks celebratory, offer to take a photo for them before diving in. "Would you like me to get a photo before you start? It looks incredible right now." Guests post these — your food markets itself, their memory is framed perfectly.
🍋
The Lemon Water Upgrade
For regular guests who typically order just water, proactively bring it with fresh lemon slices, mint, and cucumber — no charge, no mention of it. It reframes "house water" into a hospitality gesture. Upgrade the experience without upgrading the bill. The regulars will notice. They always notice.

Complaint Recovery — 3-Step System

1
Step 1: Stop, Absorb, Don't Defend
When a complaint is raised, stop what you're doing and give full attention. Do not explain, justify, or involve the kitchen immediately. Say: "Thank you for telling me — I'm really glad you said something. Can you tell me what happened so I fully understand?" Let them speak without interruption. Nodding, eye contact, no crossed arms. You're absorbing, not arguing.
2
Step 2: Own It, Fix It, Tell Them What Happens Next
Say: "I'm really sorry about that — that's not the experience we want for you, and it's not the standard we hold ourselves to. Here's what I'm going to do right now: I'm going to [replace the dish / remove it from the bill / speak to the kitchen immediately]. You should have [expected outcome] in the next [timeframe]." Specificity matters — "I'll sort it" is not enough. Tell them exactly what changes.
3
Step 3: The Gesture That Seals It
After the issue is resolved, return to the table with something unexpected: a complimentary baklava, a round of Turkish coffees, or a sincere verbal follow-up: "I just wanted to check in — is everything better now? I'm glad you stayed, and I hope we can make it right fully tonight." The gesture doesn't need to be expensive. It needs to be genuine. A recovered complaint done well creates a more loyal guest than one who never had a problem.

Neighbourhood Anchor Strategy

Partnership 1
Local Wine Merchant or Bottle Shop
Partner with a nearby wine merchant to create a "BYO Wednesday" promotion. Guests bring their own bottle, you charge a modest corkage ($8–$12). The bottle shop promotes you on their board and bags; you promote them on your menu. Both businesses benefit, and guests feel like insiders.
Partnership 2
Yoga Studio or Pilates Gym
Align with a local yoga or pilates studio — your menu is naturally aligned with their health-conscious clientele (GF options, vegetarian feasts, fresh juices). Offer a "post-class lunch card" — 10% off for anyone showing their class card. They promote you in their newsletter; you display their schedule at the host stand.
Partnership 3
Local Art Gallery or Cultural Centre
Partner with a gallery for cultural event nights — "Art & Mezze" evenings where a local artist is featured on your walls for a month. Opening night is a ticketed event with food and drinks. Gallery promotes to their mailing list; you get PR and a story to tell. The cultural connection reinforces the Mediterranean identity.

Weekly Special Idea

"Sunday Feast Sundays" — Every Sunday, add one large-format sharing dish not on the regular menu. Rotate through: Egyptian koshary, Libyan bazeen, Tunisian brik. Post it on Saturday night. Creates FOMO, drives Sunday covers, and gives regulars a reason to return weekly. Price point: $28–$35 for two.

Community Engagement Tactic

Mediterranean Cooking Class (Monthly) — Host a 2-hour cooking class on a Tuesday morning (quiet cover time). Teach two dishes: one mezze dip and one main. Charge $65 per person, includes lunch. Limit to 12 guests. Creates a recurring event, free PR, content for social, and a cohort of deeply loyal regulars who feel invested in the kitchen.
