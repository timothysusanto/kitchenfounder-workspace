Concept Philosophy

We honour the New York bodega — the neighbourhood anchor, the morning ritual, the late-night lifeline. Every dish is made with deli-counter precision: house-cured, hand-sliced, never pre-packaged. The coffee is bottomless because New Yorkers deserve better.

Section 5 — Service Training Module

These are not suggestions. They are scripts. The difference between a $28 average spend and a $42 average spend is almost entirely how your team communicates. Read these, practice them out loud, then make them yours.

The 5 Upsell Scripts

Script 1 — The Pairing

"That lox board is incredible with our egg cream — the sweetness cuts right through the richness of the fish. Want one? It only takes 30 seconds to make."

Why it works: You're giving them a flavour reason, not just a sales pitch. "Incredible with" feels like a recommendation from a friend, not a server chasing a tip.

Script 2 — The Upgrade

"You want that egg and cheese with pastrami instead of bacon? It's $3 more and honestly it's a completely different sandwich. Worth it every time."

Why it works: "Honestly" signals authenticity. "Worth it every time" is social proof without being fake. The $3 framing makes it feel small.

Script 3 — The Dessert Plant

"When I put your order in — just letting you know, our cheesecake sells out by noon most days. Want me to hold a slice for you now so you're not disappointed later?"

Why it works: Scarcity is real (or should be — make it real). The offer to "hold a slice" makes the guest feel looked after, not sold to. Say this when you take the main course order, every time.

Script 4 — The Share Suggestion

"A lot of tables get the pickle plate while they're looking at the menu — it's $9.99 and it's already sitting on the counter. Want one for the table while you decide?"

Why it works: "Already sitting on the counter" reduces perceived effort. "A lot of tables" is social proof. Zero pressure — just an offer. Converts on 50%+ of tables when said confidently.

Script 5 — The Regular Builder

"Are you nearby? We do a loyalty stamp card — tenth coffee is free. Worth grabbing one if you're in the area a lot."

Why it works: Plants the idea of returning without being pushy. The card becomes a physical reminder of your cafe in their wallet. Offer to first-time customers who seem local.

Menu Philosophy — What to Say

When someone asks "what kind of food is this?" — say this: "It's New York deli food done properly — proper pastrami, real schmear, good coffee, honest prices. The kind of place that used to be on every corner."

Story Cards

When describing Entrees: "Our starters are classic New York deli culture — the pickle plate, the lox board, the liver toast. These are dishes that have been feeding this city for a hundred years. We just make sure they're done right."
When describing Mains: "The mains are the heart of it — the Reuben, the pastrami on rye, the smash burger. Everything is made to order. The brisket is 12 hours. We don't rush anything."
When describing Drinks: "Our coffee is bottomless filter — old school deli style. The egg cream is a New York institution — chocolate syrup, seltzer, whole milk. Try it once and you'll understand."

How to Answer "What's Good Here?"

Confident Answer

"Honestly? Start with the pickle plate — it comes out immediately and it's our best value. For mains, the pastrami on rye is what we're known for — 12-hour cured, sliced thick. If you want something more filling, the Reuben or the brisket sandwich. And get the egg cream — most people haven't had a real one before."

Handling Objections

"I hear you — the pastrami is $21.99 but it's 12-hour cured and sliced thick, you're genuinely getting more than you'd expect. If you want something lighter on the wallet, the egg and cheese is $11.99 and it's honestly one of our best sellers."

"I'm not that hungry..."

"No problem — the knish or the pickle plate are perfect for that. Or the matzo ball soup is light but really satisfying. Plenty of options that won't leave you overfull."

"Just water please..."

"Got it — the egg and cheese is 4 minutes flat, and the knish is 6. I'll put it in right now and flag it as priority. You'll be out in 15 minutes."

Section 6 — Customer Retention System

Acquiring a new customer costs 5x more than keeping an existing one. Your regulars are your business. These systems are simple, free, and devastatingly effective. No CRM software needed — just attention and a notebook.

The Regulars Notebook

Keep a small notebook near the POS. Every time you learn something about a regular, write it down. Name, usual order, allergies, how they take their coffee, what days they come in. That's it. Review it before service.

The 3-Visit Rule:
Visit 1: Remember their face. Smile like you recognise them even if you don't quite yet.
Visit 2: Learn their name. "I feel like I've seen you before — I'm [name], what's yours?" Natural, not scripted.
Visit 3: They are a regular now. Remember their order. "The usual?" is the most powerful two-word phrase in hospitality.

5 VIP Micro-Moments

1. The Pre-Emptive Pickle. When a regular sits down, bring the pickle plate before they ask. No charge. It costs you $0.80 and earns you loyalty worth $500 over a year.

2. "Still black coffee?" Remembering how someone takes their coffee is worth more than any loyalty card program. Write it down after visit 2. Say it naturally on visit 3.

3. "Fresh rye just came out." Text your regulars or tell them in person when something good just happened in the kitchen. It makes them feel like insiders.

4. The Free Refill. For morning regulars who are clearly working from your cafe, bring a fresh coffee refill without being asked and without charging. They will be back tomorrow.

5. The Name Drop. When a regular walks in and there is a short wait: "Hey [name], give me 2 minutes, I'll get your table sorted." They feel seen. They come back. They bring their friends.

Complaint Recovery — The 3-Step Method

Step 1 — Listen fully. Do not interrupt. Do not defend. Do not explain. Let them finish completely. Nod. Make eye contact. This alone defuses 40% of complaints before you say a word.
Step 2 — Acknowledge and own it. Say exactly this: "You're completely right and I'm sorry — that's not the experience you should have had here. That's on us." Do not blame the kitchen, the rush, or anything else. Own it fully. Guests respect that immediately.
Step 3 — Fix it and add a gesture. Fix the problem immediately — replace the dish, remake the drink, whatever it takes. Then add: "Your dessert is on us today, and I want to give you this for next time" — hand them a card for a free coffee. Total cost to you: $3-5. Value of a recovered regular: hundreds of dollars.

Neighborhood Anchor Strategy

Partner 1 — The Morning Coffee Shop: Cross-promote with a nearby coffee-only spot. You send them people who just want a quick takeaway coffee; they send you people who want a proper meal. Give each other 10% discount cards to hand out. Zero cost, mutual benefit.

Partner 2 — The Office Building: Introduce yourself to the office manager of the nearest large building. Offer a standing catering order for Friday team lunches — pre-ordered Reubens and brisket sandwiches delivered at 12:30. One building can add $400-600 to your Friday revenue permanently.

Partner 3 — The Bookshop or Independent Retailer: Partner with a nearby independent business — leave your menus there, they leave their flyers here. Run a "bring your receipt" promotion: show their receipt, get $2 off your lunch. Drives foot traffic from their loyal customers to yours.

Weekly Special: "Friday Pastrami Day" — the full pastrami platter at $4 off, available only on Fridays from 11am. Creates a weekly ritual. Regulars plan their week around it. Post it every Thursday night on social.
