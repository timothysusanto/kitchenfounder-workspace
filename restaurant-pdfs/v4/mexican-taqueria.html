Guacamole & Totopos

Method

1
Make guac to order — never batch it. Halve avocados, remove stone, scoop into molcajete or bowl. Mash with a fork leaving some chunks — a completely smooth guacamole is incorrect texture.
2
Add lime, onion, coriander, chilli, salt, tomato. Mix gently — do not over-mix or it becomes paste. Taste. The balance is acid (lime) against fat (avocado) — the lime should cut through clearly.

Avocado Strategy: Buy avocados at every stage of ripeness — "eat today" and "eat in 3 days." Rotate constantly. A restaurant that runs out of guac because of avocado ripeness is a restaurant that has not planned properly. Always have 20+ avocados in rotation.

Method

1

Make dry caramel: heat sugar in a heavy pan until deep amber. Pour into ramekins immediately and swirl to coat the base. Work fast — caramel sets in seconds.
2
Beat eggs, yolks, cajeta, vanilla until smooth. Warm milk gently, whisk into egg mixture. Strain through fine sieve. Pour into caramel-coated ramekins.
3
Bake in a bain-marie (water bath) at 160C for 35-40 minutes until set with a slight wobble. Refrigerate minimum 4 hours. To serve, run a knife around the edge, invert onto plate — the caramel pools around the flan. Scatter toasted pepitas. A showstopper unmould.
Cajeta vs Regular Caramel: Cajeta is made from goat milk — it has a slightly tangy, more complex flavour than regular dulce de leche. If you cannot source cajeta, use dulce de leche. But cajeta is the authentic choice and the story sells: "It is made with goat milk caramel from Celaya — the cajeta capital of Mexico."

4 Section Four — Drinks

Mexican beverages are some of the most distinctive in the world — and among the most profitable. Agua fresca at under 10% food cost, horchata at 12%, tepache at 8%. These are not afterthought drinks; they are the cultural centrepiece of the Mexican cafe experience.
Agua de Jamaica$5.00

Hibiscus flower agua fresca — Mexico's most iconic non-alcoholic drink. Simmer 30g dried hibiscus flowers in 1L water with sugar and a cinnamon stick for 10 minutes. Strain, cool, dilute to taste. Serve over ice in a tall glass. Deep crimson, tart, floral. Food cost $0.40 = 8%. Your single most-ordered beverage when recommended proactively.

Horchata$5.00

Traditional rice milk drink with cinnamon and vanilla. Soak 200g long-grain rice + 1 cinnamon stick in 500ml water overnight. Blend until smooth, add 500ml more water, vanilla, sugar. Strain through muslin. Serve ice cold. Creamy, naturally sweet, deeply satisfying. Food cost $0.50 = 10%. Pairs perfectly with spicy mains — recommend it with the birria tacos or chiles rellenos.

Tamarindo Fresco$5.50

Sweet-sour tamarind agua fresca with a hint of chilli. Dissolve tamarind paste in hot water, strain, sweeten, add a pinch of chilli and salt. Serve over ice with a Tajin rim. Complex and addictive — guests who try it once always order it again. Food cost $0.45 = 8.2%. This is your most interesting non-coffee beverage conversation starter.

Mango con Tajin$6.00

Fresh mango juice with a Tajin-salt-chilli rim — the quintessential Mexican street flavour combination. Blend fresh mango with lime juice and a touch of water. Pour into a Tajin-rimmed glass over ice. Garnish with a chilli slice. Vivid orange, incredibly photogenic. Food cost $0.80 = 13.3%. Your highest-performing social media drink — photograph it in natural light every time you make a fresh batch.

Mexican Hot Chocolate$5.50

Authentic Mexican hot chocolate — Abuelita or Ibarra chocolate tablets dissolved in hot whole milk with cinnamon and a touch of cayenne. Froth with a molinillo or milk frother until foamy. Slightly grainy, deeply chocolatey, warm-spiced — completely different from European hot chocolate. Food cost $0.65 = 11.8%. Your best winter seller — position it as "grandmother's recipe" and mean it.

Cafe de Olla$5.00

Traditional Mexican cinnamon-brewed coffee with piloncillo (unrefined brown sugar). Bring water, cinnamon stick, and piloncillo to boil. Add medium-ground coffee, steep 5 minutes, strain. Serve in a clay cup if available — the earthenware adds to the experience. Warm, sweet, deeply aromatic. Food cost $0.50 = 10%. Your most culturally authentic drink and an easy conversation starter for staff.

Tepache$6.00

Lightly fermented pineapple drink — naturally low-alcohol, probiotic, spiced with cinnamon and cloves. Use whole pineapple rind (do not waste it) + piloncillo + cinnamon + cloves. Ferment 2-3 days at room temperature. Strain, chill, serve over ice. Slightly fizzy, tangy, complex. Food cost $0.40 = 6.7%. You make this from pineapple waste — one of the most sustainable and profitable items on the menu.

Jamaica Spritz$6.50

Hibiscus concentrate with fresh lime and sparkling water — a bright, elegant non-alcoholic alternative to a cocktail. 60ml strong hibiscus concentrate, 15ml lime juice, 150ml sparkling water over ice. Garnish with a fresh hibiscus flower or lime wheel. Deep red, beautiful in a stemmed glass. Food cost $0.50 = 7.7%. When served in the right glassware it elevates the entire table's perceived experience.

5 Section Five — Service Training Module

Mexican hospitality is about warmth, generosity, and making guests feel like they are at a friend's table. The best service in a taqueria does not feel like service at all — it feels like someone who genuinely loves the food telling you what to order. Train your team to feel that way.

The 5 Upsell Scripts

Script 1 — The Pairing

"The birria tacos are incredible with the agua de jamaica — the hibiscus cuts right through the richness of the braised beef. Shall I bring one?"

Why it works: Flavour reasoning + specific recommendation. "Shall I bring one?" is a yes-leaning close that does not feel pushy.

Script 2 — The Upgrade

"Want to add a side of queso fundido to that? It is $4 more, comes with fresh warm tortillas, and honestly most tables wish they had ordered it first."

Why it works: "Most tables wish they had ordered it first" is social proof that lands like honest advice. The $4 framing makes it feel like a small decision.

Script 3 — The Dessert Plant

"Quick heads-up when I put your order in — our churros come out fresh to order and sell out most days. Want me to put one in now so you are not disappointed?"

Why it works: Scarcity is real (make it real — do not run too many). The "heads-up" framing positions the server as an ally, not a salesperson. Say this with every main course order.

Script 4 — The Share Suggestion

"A lot of tables grab the guac and chips while they decide — it is fresh-made right now, $12.99, and it comes out in about 3 minutes. Want to start with that?"

Why it works: "Right now" creates immediacy. "A lot of tables" provides social proof. The 3-minute timeline makes it feel effortless to say yes.

Script 5 — The Regular Builder

"Are you local? We run a Tuesday Taco Special — it is honestly the best value on the menu that day. Would love to see you back for it."

Why it works: Gives them a specific reason to return on a specific day. Creates a weekly ritual that converts into regulars. "Would love to see you back" feels genuine, not transactional.

Cuisine Philosophy

When someone asks what kind of food this is: "It is proper Mexican — not Tex-Mex, not burritos-and-nachos. We are talking birria from Jalisco, mole negro from Oaxaca, pozole from Guerrero. Dishes from specific regions with specific stories. The kind of food Mexican grandmothers actually cook."

Story Cards

On Entrees: "Our starters are Mexican street food at its best — guacamole made fresh to order, elotes straight from the grill, ceviche with this morning's shrimp. Quick, bold, vibrant."
On Mains: "The mains are where the craftsmanship shows. Our birria braises for 8 hours. Our mole negro takes 3 days. The tamales are made in batches of a hundred. None of this is fast food — it just happens to be served quickly."
On Drinks: "Our drinks are all traditional Mexican — agua frescas made from hibiscus, tamarind, rice, and fresh fruit. No syrups, no concentrates. The tepache is fermented in-house from whole pineapple. These are drinks with 500-year histories."

How to Answer "What's Good Here?"

Confident Staff Answer

"If you like beef, the birria tacos — the beef braises for 8 hours and you dip the tacos in the consomme. For something lighter, the guac and the fish tacos. If you want the full experience, get the mole negro chicken — it takes 3 days to make. And have the agua de jamaica with whatever you order — it makes everything better."

6 Section Six — Customer Retention System

Mexican hospitality is inherently relational. In Mexico, a taquero knows every regular by name, knows their order, knows their family. You cannot replicate 20 years of history overnight — but you can start building it from day one. These systems are simple and they work.

The Regulars Notebook

A small notebook near the POS. Record name, usual order, spice preference, any allergies, what days they come in. Review it before every service. A team member who remembers a guest's name and order after 2 visits creates more loyalty than any loyalty app on the market.

The 3-Visit Rule:
Visit 1: Smile like you recognise them. Note their order internally.
Visit 2: Learn their name. "I think I have seen you in before — I am [name], what is yours?" Write it immediately after they leave.
Visit 3: They are a regular. "The usual birria tacos, extra salsa?" is the most powerful phrase you can say to a guest.

5 VIP Micro-Moments

1. The Extra Salsa. When a regular orders tacos, bring extra salsa before they ask. It costs nothing and signals that you know them and anticipate their needs.

2. Spice Memory. "Still extra habanero?" — remembering a guest's spice preference after two visits is extraordinary in any restaurant. Write it down. Say it. Watch their face.

3. The Freshness Tip-Off. Tell your regulars when the birria batch is freshest. "Just so you know, the birria just came off an hour ago — it is at its best right now." Makes them feel like insiders and creates immediate urgency to order it.

4. The Complimentary Paleta. For a loyal regular, send a mango paleta with the bill. "On the house today." Cost: $0.60. Value: immeasurable. They will tell three people.

5. Their Spot. If a regular always sits at the same table, remember it. When they walk in and it is available, guide them there without being asked. "Your usual table is free." This small act communicates that you pay attention.

Complaint Recovery — The 3-Step Method

Step 1 — Listen completely. Do not interrupt, explain, or defend. Let them finish. Nod. Make eye contact. Mexican culture values genuine respect in conflict — give it fully before saying anything.
Step 2 — Acknowledge and own it. Say exactly: "You are completely right and I am sorry. That is not the experience you should have had here. That is on us." No conditions, no "but." Own it fully. The directness of this response is disarming and immediately builds trust.
Step 3 — Fix and gesture. Fix the problem immediately — remake, replace, or refund without hesitation. Then add: "Your dessert is on us today, and I would love you to come back — here is a card for complimentary churros on your next visit." Cost: $1.20. Value of a recovered regular: hundreds of dollars over a year.

Social Media Content System

Best 5 dishes to photograph and why:

1. Birria Tacos mid-dip: The action shot of a taco being dipped into the deep red consomme is one of the most viral Mexican food images on social media. Stage this shot intentionally. Hold the taco at 45 degrees, consomme in a contrasting bowl, steam if you can create it. This one image can define your brand.

2. Elotes close-up: The colour combination of yellow corn, white crema, orange Tajin, green coriander, and yellow-green lime is visually extraordinary. Photograph from directly above at close range. Zero props needed.

3. Churros in action: Churros being dusted with cinnamon sugar, the fine powder falling through the air, photographed at 1/500s shutter speed. This is an action food photograph that performs 3x better than a static shot.

4. Tres Leches cross-section: Cut the slice in half before serving and photograph the soaked interior — the density and moisture of the crumb is what communicates the quality. Side-on shot, natural light.

5. Ceviche for freshness: The glass-clear, bright ceviche in a clean white bowl signals freshness, health, and quality. Photograph within 5 minutes of assembly before the lime starts to "cook" the colours. The hibiscus spritz alongside makes this a 2-item shot.

Post Timing: Monday 7am (start of week, people planning meals), Thursday 12pm (end of week anticipation), Saturday 10am (weekend brunch desire at its highest). Never post on Sunday evening — engagement drops significantly.

Neighborhood Anchor Strategy

Partner 1 — The Latin Grocery: Introduce yourself to the nearest Latin or Mexican grocery store owner. Leave your menus there — they are sending customers to you already, they just do not know it. Offer their staff a standing 20% discount. In return, they will recommend you to every customer asking "where can I eat Mexican food nearby?"

Partner 2 — Local Schools and Universities: Young people (18-25) are your core demographic for Tuesday taco specials and Instagram-driven growth. Connect with the student union or campus food committee. Offer a student discount card. One Instagram post from a popular student influencer can bring 200 new guests in a week.

Partner 3 — Nearby Office Buildings: Mexican food is perfect for group lunch orders. Approach office managers and HR teams within a 500m radius. Offer a "Friday Taco Catering" package — pre-ordered birria and carnitas for 10-20 people, delivered at 12:30. One committed office account can add $400-700 to your Friday revenue reliably.

Weekly Ritual — Tuesday Taco Special: Every Tuesday, one featured taco at a $3 discount. Rotate the feature weekly — birria Tuesday, al pastor Tuesday, fish taco Tuesday. Post it every Monday evening on social. After 8 weeks, regulars will start asking what Tuesday's special is before they arrive. Anticipation is a loyalty mechanism.
