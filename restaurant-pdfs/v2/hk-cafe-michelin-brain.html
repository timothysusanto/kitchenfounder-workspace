Method — Pastry Shell

1
In a food processor, pulse cold cubed butter with flour and icing sugar until it resembles fine breadcrumbs — 8–10 short pulses only. Overworking develops gluten and toughens the pastry.
2
Add egg yolk and cold water. Pulse 4–5 times until dough just comes together. It should hold when pressed — do not form a ball in the processor.
3
Turn out onto a lightly floured bench. Bring together by hand, flatten into a disc, wrap in cling film. Rest in refrigerator minimum 30 minutes (overnight preferred). This relaxes the gluten and prevents shrinkage during baking.
4
Roll to 3mm thickness on a cold bench. Cut rounds 1.5cm wider than your tart moulds. Press gently into greased 8cm tart tins without stretching. Trim flush. Chill again 15 minutes before baking.
5
Blind bake at 170°C / 340°F fan for 10 minutes with baking paper and weights. Remove weights, bake a further 5 minutes until base is dry and very lightly golden. Do not fully colour — they will bake again with filling.

Method — Custard Filling

1
Dissolve caster sugar completely in warm milk over 50°C / 122°F — do not boil. Remove from heat. Add evaporated milk and vanilla. Allow to cool to 35°C / 95°F before adding eggs.
2
Whisk eggs gently — do not incorporate air. Strain milk mixture into eggs through a fine sieve. Whisk to combine. Strain entire custard through a fine sieve a second time. Two passes through the sieve is non-negotiable for a smooth set.
3
Pour custard into blind-baked shells to 85% full. Bake at 160°C / 320°F fan for 18–22 minutes. Custard is done when it has a slight wobble in the centre — it will set further on cooling. Internal temp at set: 72°C / 162°F.
4
Remove from oven. Do not touch or move for 5 minutes. Surface cracking is caused by sudden temperature change or overbaking. Cool on wire rack. Serve warm or at room temperature — never refrigerated (pastry becomes soggy).
⚡ Chef's Tip: The biggest mistake with egg tarts is overbaking. Pull them when the centre still wobbles — residual heat finishes the set. A fully set custard in the oven = a rubbery custard on the plate. Bake fresh twice daily: 7am and 12pm service batches.

Method — Bread Dough

1
Combine flours, yeast, sugar, and salt in a stand mixer bowl. Add warm milk 35°C / 95°F and egg. Mix on low (hook) 2 minutes until shaggy dough forms. Do not add butter yet.
2
Increase to medium speed. Knead 8 minutes until smooth and elastic. Test with windowpane method — stretch a small piece: it should be thin enough to see light through without tearing.
3
Add soft butter in 3 additions on medium speed. Each addition fully incorporated before adding the next — approximately 2 minutes per addition. Final dough should be smooth, glossy, and slightly tacky — not sticky.
4
Bulk ferment covered at 26°C / 79°F for 60 minutes until doubled. Knock back gently. Divide into 12 × 60g portions. Round tightly. Place on lined trays, cover. Proof at 28°C / 82°F for 30 minutes.

Method — Polo Crust Topping

1
Cream butter and icing sugar until pale — 3 minutes. Add egg yolk, mix to combine. Fold in sifted flour, custard powder, and milk powder until just combined into a soft paste. Do not overmix.
2
Divide into 12 portions. Roll each between two sheets of cling film to a 7cm circle, 4mm thick. Refrigerate until firm enough to handle — minimum 15 minutes.
3
Drape one topping disc over each proofed bun. Using the back of a knife or a dough scraper, score a crosshatch pattern across the top — this is the signature "pineapple" look. Egg wash exposed bun edges only (not the topping — it will colour unevenly).
4
Bake at 190°C / 374°F fan for 14–16 minutes until topping is golden and cracked. Internal bread temp should reach 93°C / 200°F. Cool on rack 5 minutes before service. Split and serve with a 25g cold butter block inside while still warm.
⚡ Chef's Tip: The ratio of bread flour to cake flour is deliberate — bread flour provides structure and chew, cake flour keeps the crumb tender. Using 100% bread flour gives a chewy, tough result. The dough temperature after mixing should be 24–26°C / 75–79°F — use cold milk in summer, warm in winter to control this.

Method — Filling

1
Salt and squeeze cabbage first. Toss shredded cabbage with 5g salt, rest 10 minutes, then squeeze firmly in a clean cloth to remove excess moisture. This is critical — wet filling causes soggy rolls and dangerous oil spatter during frying.
2
Heat wok over high heat. Add 15ml oil. Stir-fry pork mince, breaking up continuously, until fully cooked through — no pink remaining, internal temp 75°C / 167°F. Approximately 3–4 minutes on high heat.
3
Add ginger, mushrooms, and squeezed cabbage. Stir-fry 2 minutes on high. Add oyster sauce, soy, and white pepper. Toss to combine. Add glass noodles and sesame oil. Stir through cornstarch. Remove from heat. Spread filling on a tray to cool to below 20°C / 68°F before rolling — hot filling tears the pastry.

Method — Rolling & Frying

1
Lay pastry sheet as a diamond. Place 35g filling in a compact log shape across the lower third. Fold bottom corner up tightly over filling. Fold in both side corners. Roll firmly upward to halfway. Brush remaining flap with egg wash. Complete roll. Press to seal. Rolls must be tight — any air pockets cause bursting during frying.
2
Freeze assembled rolls on a tray, then transfer to bags once solid. Fry from frozen — do not thaw. This is the correct professional method: frozen interior prevents overcooked filling while exterior achieves maximum crispness.
3
Heat oil to 175°C / 347°F. Fry in batches of 6–8 (do not crowd — oil temp drops and rolls absorb oil instead of frying). Fry 3.5–4 minutes until deep golden. Internal temp must reach 75°C / 167°F. Drain on wire rack — never paper towel (steam makes them soggy).
⚡ Chef's Tip: Oil temperature is everything with spring rolls. Below 170°C = greasy, soggy rolls. Above 185°C = burnt exterior, cold interior. Use a probe thermometer and check between batches. Allow oil to recover to 175°C for at least 90 seconds before adding the next batch.

Method — Chicken & Sauce (Mise en Place — Batch Daily)

1
Butterfly chicken thighs to an even 2cm thickness — this ensures consistent cooking. Marinate minimum 2 hours, maximum 24 hours refrigerated. Remove from fridge 20 minutes before cooking to temper.
2
Sear chicken in a hot pan with neutral oil, presentation side down first, over high heat 2 minutes each side. Target colour: deep golden. Do not cook through — target internal temp at this stage: 60°C / 140°F. Remove and rest. It will finish in the oven.
3
In the same pan, sweat onion and garlic over medium heat until soft and translucent — 5 minutes, no colour. Add turmeric and curry powder, cook out 90 seconds until fragrant. This blooms the spices in fat and removes the raw flour taste.
4
Add coconut cream and chicken stock. Bring to a gentle simmer. Reduce by 25% — approximately 8 minutes. Season with soy, fish sauce, and sugar. Taste: the sauce should be rich, slightly sweet, gently spiced. Adjust seasoning. Cool sauce completely before service assembly.

Method — Assembly & Baking (To Order)

1
Place 150g cooked jasmine rice in an individual oven-safe dish or deep plate. Spread evenly. Slice seared chicken thigh across the grain into 4–5 pieces and arrange over rice.
2
Ladle 120ml hot sauce evenly over chicken and rice. Top with 40g shredded mozzarella. Place in preheated oven at 220°C / 428°F top grill/broil for 6–8 minutes until cheese is bubbling and golden-spotted.
3
Final chicken internal temp must reach 74°C / 165°F — probe through the cheese into the thickest part of the chicken. Rest 2 minutes before service. Garnish: spring onion fine sliced, a pinch of white pepper.
⚡ Chef's Tip: Pre-cook rice and sauce in morning prep. Sear all chicken portions at start of service, hold at 60°C / 140°F in a warm oven. At order: assemble cold rice + chicken + hot sauce + cheese → blast under grill. Ticket time: 8 minutes. This dish should never be reheated from cold to order — it becomes dry and the rice absorbs all the sauce.

Method — Braised Brisket (Prep Day Before)

1
Blanch brisket first — mandatory. Place whole brisket in cold water, bring to a boil. Boil 5 minutes. Drain, rinse under cold water. This removes impurities and blood, producing a clear broth. Skipping this step results in a cloudy, bitter soup.
2
In a large stockpot, sear blanched brisket over high heat in neutral oil until deep brown on all sides — 3–4 minutes per side. Colour = flavour. Do not rush this step.
3
Add all aromatics (star anise, cinnamon, tangerine peel, peppercorns, ginger, spring onion) tied in muslin. Add soy, Shaoxing wine, rock sugar, and stock/water to cover brisket by 4cm. Bring to a boil. Skim any foam. Reduce to a bare simmer — the surface should barely tremble.
4
Braise covered at 90–95°C / 194–203°F for 10–12 hours. Add daikon in the last 90 minutes — it becomes silky and absorbs broth without falling apart. Brisket is done when a chopstick passes through with no resistance. Internal temp: 93°C / 200°F (collagen breakdown requires this).
5
Remove brisket. Cool completely in braising liquid — this is critical. Cooling in the liquid prevents moisture loss and keeps the meat tender. Once cold, slice against the grain into 8mm portions. Portion and refrigerate. Strain and reserve broth separately.

Method — Service Assembly (5 minutes)

1
Bring broth to a rolling boil. Blanch noodles in a separate pot of boiling salted water — fresh egg noodles: 45 seconds. Drain, divide into pre-warmed deep bowls.
2
Reheat brisket slices in hot broth for 60 seconds — do not boil. Arrange over noodles with 2 pieces of daikon. Ladle 350ml hot broth. Finish with spring onion, white pepper, 3ml sesame oil. Serve with chilli oil on the side.
⚡ Chef's Tip: The clear broth (清湯) should be golden and clean — not brown or cloudy. If it clouds during service, it has been boiled too hard. Keep broth at a consistent 85°C / 185°F during service — a gentle simmer. Boiling makes it cloudy and changes the flavour profile. Make broth every 2 days maximum — day 3 broth tastes flat.

Method

1
Tenderise the chop physically first. Using a meat mallet or back of a heavy knife, pound both sides of the loin chop until thickness is reduced to 1.5cm and fibres are visibly broken. Method — Wonton Filling & Folding

1
Combine pork mince with all seasonings and egg white. Mix in one direction only — clockwise for 3 minutes by hand or 90 seconds in a stand mixer with paddle. This develops the myosin proteins and creates a bouncy, cohesive texture. Fold in chopped shrimp and water chestnuts last — do not overmix shrimp.
2
Test seasoning: fry a small ball of filling in a pan until cooked through 75°C / 167°F. Taste and adjust. Always test filling before folding 60 wontons.
3

Method — Broth (Make Every 2 Days)

1
Blanch pork bones: cover with cold water, bring to boil, boil 5 minutes, drain and rinse. This removes blood and impurities for a clean, golden broth.
2
Combine blanched bones, dried shrimp, dried flounder, ginger, and spring onion in a stockpot with 4L cold water. Bring to a boil over high heat. Skim thoroughly. Reduce to a gentle simmer — surface barely moving. Simmer 3 hours. Do not boil — boiling emulsifies fat into broth making it cloudy and greasy.
3
Strain through a fine sieve. Season with soy, white pepper, and salt. The finished broth should be pale golden, clear, and deeply savoury with a clean shrimp sweetness. Refrigerate. Skim solid fat from surface when cold before reheating for service.
⚡ Chef's Tip: The secret to exceptional wonton soup is the broth temperature at service. Bowls must be pre-warmed — cold bowls drop broth temperature by 8–12°C in under 60 seconds. Stack bowls in a warm oven (60°C) during service. Noodles must be rinsed in cold water after blanching and shaken completely dry before adding to the bowl — waterlogged noodles dilute the broth.

Chef's Quality Notes

Mango selection is everything. This dish lives or dies on mango quality. Use Ataulfo (Champagne) mangoes for their fibre-free, intensely sweet flesh. Avoid Tommy Atkins — too fibrous and acidic. The mango must be at peak ripeness: fragrant at the stem, yielding to gentle pressure, no green remaining.

Pomelo prep: Remove all white pith — it is intensely bitter and will ruin the dish. Work section by section, peel each individual membrane. The clean pomelo flesh should be translucent and juicy.

Sugar adjustment: Taste the mango base before adding any sugar. Ripe Ataulfo may need zero additional sugar — or up to 50g if underripe. Always taste, never assume.

Method — Sago Pearls

1
Bring a large pot of water (minimum 3L per 80g sago) to a rolling boil. Add sago in a thin stream, stirring immediately to prevent clumping. Cook over medium-high heat, stirring every 2 minutes, for 12–15 minutes until pearls are 95% translucent with a tiny white dot remaining in the centre.
2
Remove from heat. Cover and stand 5 minutes — residual heat finishes cooking. The white dot will disappear. Drain through a fine sieve. Rinse under cold running water until pearls are completely cold and no longer sticky. Transfer to a container with cold water to cover. Refrigerate. Use within 24 hours — sago hardens and becomes grainy beyond this.

Method — Mango Base & Assembly

1
Blend 600g mango flesh until completely smooth. Pass through a fine sieve — this removes any fibrous strands for a silky texture. Add coconut cream and orange juice. Blend briefly. Taste, adjust sugar if needed. Chill minimum 2 hours — serve ice cold at 4°C / 39°F.
2
Per serve: drain 40g sago from water, add to a pre-chilled glass bowl. Ladle 180ml cold mango base over sago. Top with 25g diced fresh mango and 18g pomelo segments. Finish with a sprig of fresh mint. Serve immediately — this dessert cannot wait.
⚡ Chef's Tip: Batch the mango base daily — do not make ahead by more than 24 hours as it oxidises and loses its vibrant colour. If service volume is high, keep mango base in an ice bath on the pass. This is your highest-margin dessert. At $9.99 with under $1.70 ingredient cost, push it on every table.

Method

1
Steep the tea hard. Combine cream, milk, and tea leaves. Heat to 85°C / 185°F — do not boil (boiling cream creates a skin and alters flavour). Steep covered for 12 minutes. The infusion should be deeply amber and strong — this is HK milk tea, not afternoon tea. Strain through a fine sieve, pressing leaves firmly. Discard leaves. Stir in condensed milk while still warm.
2
Whisk egg yolks and caster sugar until pale and slightly thickened — 2 minutes. Do not create foam — air bubbles in the custard surface will cause pitting after baking. Add vanilla and salt.
3
Temper the hot cream mixture into egg yolks: add in a slow, thin stream while whisking constantly. If added too fast, yolks will scramble. Strain through a fine sieve twice. Skim any surface foam with a spoon or blowtorch briefly.
4
Preheat oven to 150°C / 302°F fan-off. Pour custard into ramekins — fill to 5mm below the rim. Place ramekins in a deep roasting tray. Pour hot water into the tray to reach halfway up the ramekins. Cover tray loosely with foil.
5
Bake 35–40 minutes. Custard is set when the edges are firm but the centre has a 2–3cm wobble when gently shaken. Internal temp at set: 72°C / 162°F. Remove from water bath immediately. Cool to room temperature, then refrigerate minimum 4 hours, ideally overnight.
6
To serve: blot surface dry with a paper towel if any condensation present. Sprinkle an even layer of 8g caster sugar across surface. Caramelise with a blowtorch in a circular motion, keeping torch 5–7cm from surface, until an even amber crust forms with no burnt spots. Serve within 3 minutes — the crust softens quickly from the cold custard beneath.
⚡ Chef's Tip: The water bath (bain-marie) is non-negotiable — it moderates oven temperature and ensures the custard sets gently and evenly. Direct oven heat produces curdled, grainy custard. The foil cover prevents a skin forming on top. If your crème brûlée develops surface bubbles, your oven was too hot or you added the cream too quickly to the yolks. Both issues show up at baking — fix them at prep.

6Kitchen Layout Principles

🔥 Hot Kitchen — Non-Negotiable Equipment

  • 2× commercial wok burners — minimum 120,000 BTU each. Do not compromise on this. Consumer or semi-commercial woks cannot achieve wok hei.
  • 1× flat top grill — pork chops, eggs, bread
  • 1× commercial deep fryer — 18L minimum. Separate baskets for spring rolls and other items (flavour transfer).
  • 1× 6-burner range — broth maintenance, sauce work
  • 3× commercial rice cookers — run in rotation: one cooking, one holding, one on standby
  • 1× salamander / overhead grill — Portuguese chicken rice finishing
  • Pass with heat lamps — minimum 90 seconds hold time without quality loss

❄️ Cold Kitchen & Pastry

  • Dedicated pastry bench — marble preferred (stays cold for pastry work)
  • Deck oven or convection — egg tarts, pineapple buns
  • Blast chiller — cool cooked proteins and fillings to safe temp rapidly. Mandatory for food safety compliance.
  • Reach-in prep fridge — mise en place storage at point of use
  • Wonton folding station — dedicated bench, away from heat
  • Dessert display fridge — crème brûlée, chia pots visible to FOH
Flow principle: Raw proteins → prep bench → cooking station → pass. Never cross raw and cooked pathways. Design your kitchen walk-through before signing any lease.
