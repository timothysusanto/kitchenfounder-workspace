The 4 Dough Bases · Architecture · Science · Franchise SOP02
Why Four Bases — The Franchise Logic
Most donut brands run one dough. One dough means one texture, one flavour profile, one eating experience. NAMA28 is built on four distinct doughs that create genuinely different products — not just different toppings on the same base. This is the franchise advantage: a customer who visits twice in one week can have a completely different experience if they choose a different base family. It also creates an education narrative — "have you tried our potato dough yet?" — that drives repeat visits and builds the brand vocabulary. Each base is fully documented with grams, temperatures, times, and visual checkpoints so any trained franchisee kitchen can produce them identically on day one.
Dough Base A · Classic Nama
Sake Lees Brioche + Tangzhong
The original. Ultra-fine crumb, faintly floral, pale gold crust. Fries at 160°C.
Fry 160°C · 90 sec/side Families: F1, F2, F3
Ingredients (yields 16–18)
Tangzhong
Bread flour25 g
Whole milk125 ml
Main Dough
Bread flour (strong)475 g
Caster sugar55 g
Fine salt8 g
Milk powder20 g
Sake lees (酒粕) activated in 50ml water40 g
Whole eggs2 (100 g)
Egg yolks3 (60 g)
Warm milk (30°C)80 ml
Dry sake (junmai)30 ml
Vanilla bean paste1 tsp
Unsalted butter, cold, cubed150 g
Method Summary
  1. Cook tangzhong to 65°C, cool completely
  2. Activate sake lees in warm water 15–20 min
  3. Combine dry ingredients; add wet + tangzhong + sake lees
  4. Mix 5 min to shaggy dough. Add butter cube by cube over 12–14 min
  5. Knead 8 min to windowpane. Bulk proof in fridge 14–16 hrs
  6. Shape 7–8 cm rounds, 2.5 cm thick. Final proof 1.5–2 hrs RT
  7. Fry 160–165°C, 90 sec/side, pale gold only
  8. Cool completely before filling
Franchise QC CheckpointThe finished dough ball should feel like cold butter — smooth, supple, slightly tacky. The proofed round should jiggle when tray is shaken. The fried donut should be pale gold, not deep amber. Cross-section crumb: ultra-fine, even, featherlight.
Dough Base B · Potato Brioche
Riced Russet Potato + Sake Lees
Pillowy, cloud-soft, mochi-adjacent texture. Stays fresh longer. Best for heavy fillings.
Fry 162°C · 90 sec/side Families: F4, F5
Ingredients (yields 16–18)
Potato Preparation
Russet or Desiree potato (steamed, not boiled)180 g cooked weight
Riced while hot, spread to cool completely—
Main Dough
Bread flour (strong)400 g
Caster sugar50 g
Fine salt8 g
Milk powder15 g
Sake lees, activated40 g
Whole eggs2 (100 g)
Egg yolks2 (40 g) — 1 less than Base A
Warm milk (30°C)70 ml — reduced
Dry sake25 ml
Vanilla bean paste1 tsp
Unsalted butter, cold130 g — reduced
Key Differences from Base A
  • Add riced potato with the wet ingredients — it disperses into the dough without visible lumps if the potato is bone dry and completely cold
  • Reduce flour by 75g (potato contributes starch); reduce egg yolks by 1 (potato adds richness); reduce milk by 10ml and butter by 20g
  • Dough will be slightly stickier — this is correct. Do not add more flour.
  • Final proof: same 1.5–2 hrs but the donuts rise higher — potato starch ferments faster. Watch for over-proofing.
  • Fry at 162°C — potato sugars brown 2–3°C earlier than plain flour. Never exceed 165°C or the outside colours before the inside cooks.
  • Texture result: dramatically softer crumb, almost pillowy. Less bready pull, more cloud. Stays soft 6–8 hrs vs 4 hrs for Base A.
  • Best paired with: heavy ganache, thick compotes, miso caramel — fillings that benefit from a softer structural contrast
Franchise QC CheckpointSteam potato, never boil (no added water). Rice immediately while hot. Spread on tray and refrigerate uncovered 1 hour until completely dry. Any moisture in the potato = dense, gummy crumb.

Dough Base C · Almond Enriched
Almond Meal Brioche
Dense, moist, nutty depth. Slightly heavier crumb. Pairs with bold chocolate and dark flavours.
Fry 165°C · 2 min/side Families: F4, F6
Ingredients (yields 14–16 — slightly fewer as dough is denser)
Almond Component
Blanched almond meal (fine ground)100 g
Almond butter (pure, no salt)30 g
Main Dough
Bread flour (strong)400 g
Caster sugar65 g — slightly more: almond is bitter-neutral, needs sugar
Fine salt9 g — slightly more: enhances almond flavour
Sake lees, activated40 g
Whole eggs3 (150 g) — extra egg for emulsification with almond fat
Egg yolks2 (40 g)
Warm milk (30°C)90 ml
Amaretto (optional, 1 tbsp replaces 1 tbsp sake)20 ml sake + 15 ml amaretto
Vanilla + almond extract1 tsp vanilla + ¼ tsp almond
Unsalted butter, cold120 g — less: almond butter adds fat
Key Differences + Method Notes
  • Mix almond meal and almond butter with the dry ingredients first — if added with the wet, the fat inhibits gluten development
  • Almond meal absorbs water differently — dough will feel slightly sandier initially. Continue mixing; it smooths out after 5 minutes
  • Amaretto replaces part of the sake for a deeper almond note — optional but recommended for Family F4 (chocolate) pairings
  • Gluten development is slightly slower — almond fat coats gluten strands. Windowpane test may take 10 min longer
  • Fry at 165°C — almond meal browns slightly faster due to natural oils. Watch first batch carefully to calibrate
  • Crumb result: denser, moister, with a faint nutty background flavour. The cross-section looks more opaque than Base A. The bite is more substantial — less feather, more body
  • Best paired with: dark chocolate ganache, espresso, miso, bold umami fillings where the almond depth adds without competing
Franchise QC CheckpointUse fine blanched almond meal only — not almond flour with skin. Coarse almond meal creates an uneven crumb and visible brown flecks. Store almond meal in freezer at franchise location to prevent rancidity.

Why This Is the Franchise Signature

Base D combines the best of Bases B and C. The potato delivers the cloud softness and extended freshness. The almond meal adds depth, moisture retention, and a subtle nutty undertone that makes the dough taste like it contains more than it does. The sake lees yeast ties it together with its floral, complex fermentation note.

This dough cannot be easily replicated from taste alone — the combination and ratios are proprietary to NAMA28. This is the franchise moat. Used exclusively for Family F7 (Seasonal/Limited), it creates a tier of donut that is only available at NAMA28 locations, only in limited quantities, only during specific seasonal windows. The scarcity and the unique texture reinforce each other.

  • Add potato with wet ingredients, almond meal with dry
  • Expect dough to be noticeably stickier — do not compensate with flour
  • Extended knead time: 10 min after butter addition
  • Cold proof 16 hrs minimum for Base D — extra flavour development

Hero 01 · Family 1 · Base B Potato
Miso Caramel Kinako
味噌キャラメルきなこ
White miso caramel custard · kinako cloud cream · sesame tuile · fleur de sel — Donut #04
Caramel 118°C · Custard 80°C6 donuts · Base B
Ingredients
Miso Caramel Custard
Caster sugar120 g
Double cream, warm180 ml
White miso (Marukome Shiro)35 g
Unsalted butter40 g
Egg yolks3
Cornflour15 g
Fleur de sel½ tsp
Kinako Cloud Cream
Double cream, cold200 ml
Mascarpone60 g
Kinako (roasted soybean flour)20 g
Icing sugar25 g
Fine saltpinch
To Finish
Kinako + icing sugar (dusting mix)3:1 ratio
Sesame tuile (pre-made)1 per donut
Fleur de sel crystals3–4 per donut
Caramel drizzle (reserved)1 tsp per donut
Method
  1. 1Miso caramel: dry caramel in heavy pan. Cook 120g sugar without stirring to deep amber (170°C). Off heat, add warm cream — bubbles violently. Return to heat, stir until smooth. Cool to 65°C. Add miso and butter — the miso's umami transforms the caramel into something layered and extraordinary. Add fleur de sel. Reserve 30ml for drizzle. Cool remaining to 50°C.
  2. 2Miso caramel custard: whisk egg yolks and cornflour. Temper warm miso caramel into egg mixture. Return to pan, cook to 80°C until thick and glossy. Deep amber-brown with savoury-sweet aroma. Cool with cling on surface. Refrigerate 4 hrs.
  3. 3Kinako cream: whip double cream, mascarpone, kinako, icing sugar, and salt to firm peaks. Kinako adds a roasted, almost peanut-butter depth. The cream will be very faintly beige — beautiful against the dark caramel.
  4. 4Fill Base B (potato) donuts: pipe miso caramel custard into each donut via side nozzle (35g). The cloud potato crumb absorbs a small amount of the caramel at the interface — this is intentional and delicious. Then cut a shallow top well. Pipe kinako cream in an open dome over the well (40g).
  5. 5Dust cream generously with kinako-icing sugar mix. Drizzle reserved miso caramel over the cream in a slow arc. Press 3–4 fleur de sel crystals into the cream. Insert sesame tuile upright. The base of the donut gets a light kinako dusting too.
Chef's NoteThe miso caramel must reach proper amber — pale caramel with miso tastes flat. Dark amber + miso is the combination that produces the complex, almost meaty depth that makes guests pause and say "what is that?" That pause is the Michelin moment.

Hero 02 · Family 5 · Base B Potato
Pandan Coconut Diplomat
パンダンココナッツ
Jade pandan diplomat · butterfly pea colour-change glaze · kaffir lime · toasted coconut — Donut #17
Diplomat 80°C · Set 4 hrs6 donuts · Base B
Ingredients
Pandan Diplomat
Fresh pandan leaves, blended + strained6 leaves → 80 ml juice
Coconut milk (full fat, Ayam)180 ml
Full-cream milk80 ml
Egg yolks3
Caster sugar50 g
Cornflour18 g
Unsalted butter25 g
Gelatine leaves1.5 sheets
Double cream, cold, whipped soft120 ml
Butterfly Pea Glaze
Dried butterfly pea flowers10 g steeped in 60ml hot water
Icing sugar, sifted180 g
Lemon juice (colour-change trigger)½ tsp added at dipping
To Finish
Toasted coconut flakes (large)40 g
Kaffir lime leaf, microplaned1 leaf per batch
Freeze-dried pandan powderlight dusting
Method
  1. 1Pandan infusion: blend pandan leaves with 100ml cold water. Strain through fine sieve pressing hard — extract every drop of vivid green liquid. Combine with coconut milk and regular milk. Heat to steaming.
  2. 2Soak gelatine. Whisk egg yolks, sugar, cornflour. Temper hot pandan coconut milk into eggs. Return to pan, cook to 80°C until thick. Off heat: squeeze gelatine, add. Add butter. Stir smooth. Colour: luminous jade green. Cling on surface, cool, refrigerate 4 hrs.
  3. 3Whisk set cream to loosen. Fold in whipped cream (diplomat method) — lighter than plain pastry cream. Fill Base B donuts generously via side nozzle. The cloud potato crumb + jade cream = the softest eating experience in the collection.
  4. 4Butterfly pea glaze: steep flowers 10 min, strain — deep violet-blue. Mix with icing sugar to thick glaze. At the moment of dipping, add lemon juice — glaze shifts violet to vivid purple-magenta. Dip immediately before it stabilises.
  5. 5While glaze is still wet, press toasted coconut flakes standing upright into the surface. They will set in the glaze at various angles — architectural and natural. Microplane fresh kaffir lime leaf over the entire batch at service. The fragrance is immediate and extraordinary.
Chef's NoteThe kaffir lime microplane happens at the moment of service — not during prep. The volatile aromatic oils in kaffir lime evaporate within minutes. If guests can smell it before they see the donut, the experience has already begun.

Hero 03 · Family 4 · Base C Almond
Almond Dark Chocolate Ganache
アーモンドビターチョコ
Valrhona 70% ganache · almond praline · dark mirror glaze · cacao nib · fleur de sel — Donut #14
Ganache 45°C · Praline 165°C6 donuts · Base C Almond
Ingredients
Valrhona Ganache
Valrhona Guanaja 70% dark chocolate180 g
Double cream100 ml
Unsalted butter20 g
Fine saltpinch
Amaretto (1 tbsp — almond echo)15 ml
Almond Praline Layer
Blanched almonds, whole120 g
Caster sugar80 g
Fine salt½ tsp
Almond butter (pure)20 g
Dark Mirror Glaze
Valrhona Guanaja 70%120 g
Double cream60 ml
Glucose syrup20 g
Neutral oil1 tsp
To Finish
Cacao nibs1 tsp per donut
Fleur de sel3–4 crystals
Gold powder or leaftouch
Almond flakes, toastedsmall cluster
Method
  1. 1Almond praline: toast whole blanched almonds at 160°C for 12 min until golden. Dry caramel: cook sugar to deep amber without stirring. Immediately pour over almonds on silpat. Sprinkle fine salt. Cool completely until hard (20 min). Break into rough pieces. Process in food processor: first to a coarse rubble (praline), then continue until it releases its oil and becomes a thick, flowing paste. Add almond butter to encourage this. Store in an airtight jar. This praline is used as a layer inside the donut and as a flavour base for future batches.
  2. 2Dark chocolate ganache: heat cream until steaming. Pour over chopped Valrhona. Sit 1 minute. Stir from centre. Add butter, salt, and amaretto. Mix until completely emulsified and glossy. The amaretto echoes the almond dough base — the flavour conversation between dough and filling. Set at room temperature 2 hrs or refrigerate 1 hr. It should be pipeable but hold its shape.
  3. 3Double-fill Base C donuts: make a top well in each donut. Spoon 15g almond praline paste into the base. Pipe dark chocolate ganache over the praline in a generous dome — 45g. The praline oozes slightly around the ganache edges — a deliberate effect.
  4. 4Dark mirror glaze: heat cream with glucose. Pour over chocolate. Add oil. Mix until smooth. Keep at 38°C for dipping. Dip only the side and base of the donut (not the top — the ganache dome is the visual). Allow to set on rack.
  5. 5Press cacao nibs into the ganache dome — scattered, not formal. Arrange toasted almond flakes in a small cluster. Add fleur de sel crystals. Apply the finest possible dusting of gold powder using a dry brush across the entire surface. The donut should gleam softly, not sparkle — gold dust at this scale is a whisper, not a shout.
Chef's NoteThe almond dough (Base C) and almond praline create a double almond message — one in the crumb, one in the filling. Combined with the bitter Valrhona ganache, this is the most sophisticated flavour architecture in Family 4. The key is restraint in sweetness: this donut should taste like a great dark chocolate bar, not a confection.

Hero 04 · Family 7 · Base D Hybrid · Autumn Limited ✦
Roasted Potato & Black Truffle
黒トリュフポテト · Autumn Limited
Truffle potato cream · black truffle shaving · roasted garlic honey · herb dust — Donut #26 · $15.00
Potato cream 75°C · Truffle cold6 donuts · Base D Hybrid
Ingredients
Truffle Potato Cream
Roasted Russet potato (skin on, roasted 200°C 1hr)200 g flesh only
Double cream, warm100 ml
Mascarpone80 g
Black truffle paste (quality, not oil)20 g
Truffle oil (Urbani or similar)1 tsp finish only
Roasted garlic (4 cloves, roasted until soft)4 cloves
Fine salt + white pepperto season
Gelatine leaves2 sheets (for set)
Roasted Garlic Honey
Acacia honey60 g
Roasted garlic cloves (soft, squeezed out)3 cloves
Fresh thyme leaves½ tsp
Fine saltpinch
To Finish
Fresh black truffle (Périgord or Melanosporum), shaved paper-thin2–3 shavings per donut
Chervil or flat parsley micro leavessmall pinch
Fleur de sel3 crystals
Gold leaf1 fragment
Method
  1. 1Roasted potato vs steamed: for this recipe specifically, roasting at 200°C for 1 hour concentrates the potato's natural sugars and creates a caramelised, earthy depth that steamed potato does not have. Split roasted potatoes while hot. Scoop flesh only. Pass through a tamis or fine drum sieve while hot.
  2. 2Soak gelatine. Warm the roasted potato flesh with cream over gentle heat until smooth and pourable. Add roasted garlic (squeeze cloves from skin — they should be soft and sweet, not sharp). Add truffle paste. Season well with salt and white pepper. Blend or whisk until silky. Add squeezed gelatine. Stir. Pass through fine sieve if needed. Off heat, add truffle oil. Mix. Press cling on surface. Cool to room temperature then refrigerate until set — 4 hrs minimum.
  3. 3Once set, whisk the truffle potato cream with mascarpone until smooth and pipeable. Transfer to piping bag.
  4. 4Roasted garlic honey: warm honey gently. Add squeezed roasted garlic and thyme. Muddle together with a fork — the garlic should break down into the honey rather than remaining as chunks. Fine strain to remove garlic solids. Cool. Transfer to small squeeze bottle.
  5. 5Assembly: cut well in top of each Base D donut. Pipe truffle potato cream generously — 55g per donut. The cream is intentionally savoury and earthy — nothing like any other donut filling in the collection. Drizzle roasted garlic honey over the top in a slow spiral (½ tsp). The honey gloss is the glaze — no additional glaze needed.
  6. 6Lay 2–3 paper-thin black truffle shavings over the cream — they should drape naturally. Scatter 3–4 micro herb leaves. Add fleur de sel crystals. Press one gold leaf fragment to the truffle surface at the highest point. The visual: ivory cream, earth-black truffle, green herb, white salt, gold. Restaurant quality on a donut.

Sudachi & Shiso
酢橘と紫蘇
Sudachi citrus curd · fresh shiso cream · green citrus glaze · pickled shiso · almond dough — Donut #22
Curd 82°C · Shiso cream cold6 donuts · Base C Almond

Sudachi Curd

Icing sugar180 g
Sudachi juice3 tbsp
Green gel colouring2 drops — pale green only

Sudachi zest, microplaned freshat service

Method
  1. 1Sudachi curd: sudachi is a small Japanese citrus from Tokushima prefecture — intensely tart, more complex than lemon, slightly floral. Cook over bain marie: whisk eggs, yolks, sugar, juice, and zest together. Stir constantly until 82°C and thick enough to coat the spoon clearly. Whisk in cold butter cubes one at a time. The curd should be pale yellow-green from the zest. Strain. Cool. Refrigerate 3 hrs.
  2. 2Shiso cream: the shiso must be fresh and added at the last moment — it oxidises quickly and turns brown. Chiffonade very finely. Fold into whipped cream and mascarpone just before filling. The shiso distributes green flecks through the cream and imparts a faint peppery, anise-adjacent, herbal freshness that is entirely unique. There is no substitute for fresh shiso.
  3. 3Fill almond dough donuts via side nozzle with sudachi curd (25g). Cut top well. Pipe shiso cream in a dome (45g). The curd provides the citrus shock; the cream provides the cooling, herbal lift. The almond dough underneath adds depth and nuttiness — this is why Base C is correct for this donut.
  4. 4Dip donut sides in pale green sudachi glaze. Set on rack. Place one pickled beni shiso leaf on top of the cream — deep red-purple against the green cream. Microplane fresh sudachi zest over the top at service. Scatter a small cluster of toasted almond flakes into the cream.
Chef's NoteThe pickled red shiso (beni shiso) leaf against the green cream creates a colour contrast that is both striking and classically Japanese — the red and green pairing of shiso in Japanese cuisine is centuries old. The acidic, floral sudachi against the herbal shiso against the nutty almond dough is a three-layer flavour architecture that rewards slow eating.

Method
  1. 1Amazake: amazake (甘酒) is a traditional Japanese fermented rice drink made from rice koji — it is mildly sweet, with a faintly yeasty, rice-wine umami undertone, and zero alcohol. It is given to children at winter festivals in Japan. The flavour is unique: sweeter than rice, more complex than milk, with a slight fermented depth that pairs extraordinarily with almond and warm spice. Bring amazake and milk to a simmer. Steep 5 min. This is the infusion base.
  2. 2Soak gelatine. Whisk egg yolks, reduced sugar, and cornflour. Temper hot amazake milk into eggs. Return to pan, cook to 80°C until thick. Add almond butter — it incorporates into the warm custard smoothly, adding body and nutty richness. Add butter and gelatine. Mix until smooth. The cream will be pale cream-ivory with a complex, warm, rice-and-almond fragrance. Refrigerate 3 hrs. Fold in whipped cream diplomat-style.
  3. 3Fill Base D donuts via side nozzle (30g amazake cream inside). Cut top well. Pipe open rosette on top (40g). Spoon 1 tsp almond praline into the cream at an angle — the praline has texture and bitterness that cuts through the gentle sweetness of the amazake.
  4. 4Dip donut sides in warm spice glaze (amber, cinnamon-forward). Set on rack. Apply gold leaf fragment to praline. Add small cinnamon stick piece at an angle. Dust lightly with ground Ceylon cinnamon at service. The spice fragrance should reach the guest before the donut does.

Why NAMA28 Is Franchise-Ready From Day One
The 28-variant, 4-dough-base architecture was designed with franchising as the end goal, not an afterthought. Every element that makes replication possible is intentional: the dry blend ratios are pre-mixed and supplied by the franchisor, removing the risk of variable ingredient sourcing; the Base D Hybrid formula is proprietary and not disclosed in franchisee documents, creating a protected IP moat; the seasonal Family 7 rotation is controlled centrally and released quarterly, ensuring all locations serve identical products simultaneously; the frying temperatures are specified to ±2°C for consistent product across locations. This is not a recipe collection. It is a franchise system disguised as a menu.
