Shopify 2.0

The Store That Sells While You Sleep

Shopify 2.0 gives you Section Everywhere, metafields, and app blocks — tools that make your store behave like a custom-coded site without touching a developer. Here's the exact architecture to build, in priority order.

Priority 1
Homepage — The Trust Machine
1–2 hours to build

Your homepage has one job: turn a curious visitor into someone who believes you know what you're talking about. In that order. Not sell first — believe first. The new generation skips anything that feels like an ad. They stay for proof.

Homepage section order (Dawn theme, drag-and-drop in Shopify editor):

  • Hero banner: Dark background. Headline: "Turn any cuisine into a profitable restaurant." Sub: "$29.99 · 28 recipes · Full P&L · Instant download." CTA button: "Browse all guides." — NO carousel, NO animation. One clean statement.
  • Credential strip: "59 cuisines · 28 recipes per guide · 22 free tools · Built by a Head Chef with 10 years in hotel F&B" — single line, dark background, small text. This is the trust anchor.
  • Featured collection: Show 6 best-selling guides. Title: "Most popular right now." Use Shopify metafields to add "P&L: $2.9M · Margin: 38%" under each product.
  • What's inside section: 8-item grid (use multicolumn block). Each item = one thing inside every guide (recipes, P&L, floor plan, scripts, SMS templates etc.)
  • Social proof: 3 quote cards. No fake reviews. Use real reactions from social media or write from genuine chef perspective.
  • 22 tools strip: "Every guide includes 22 free operator tools. No account needed. Works on any device." Link to tools page.
  • FAQ accordion: 5 questions. Use Shopify native FAQ section (Dawn has one built in).
  • Final CTA: Full-width dark section. "$29.99 · Instant download · Browse all 59 guides."
Priority 2
Product Pages — The Conversion Engine
30 min per product

Every product page follows this exact structure. Do NOT use the default Shopify product template — create a custom one:

  • Above the fold: Product title + cuisine flag emoji + price ($29.99) + "Instant download" badge + Add to Cart button. Nothing else competes with the button.
  • The hook paragraph: 2 sentences. Specific to the cuisine. "The Argentine Asado guide documents the exact system behind a $2.9M/year concept — fire-cooked proteins, chimichurri build from scratch, silver service floor scripts for 80 covers." Not generic. Specific.
  • What's inside accordion: Expand to show full 28-recipe list. This answers "what exactly am I buying?" without overwhelming above the fold.
  • P&L summary box: Projected revenue + net margin. Dark background. Makes the $29.99 feel like an investment, not a cost.
  • 22 tools list: Collapsed by default. "Also includes 22 free operator tools — expand to see." This adds perceived value without cluttering.
  • FAQ (product-specific): 3 questions relevant to that cuisine. Use Shopify metafields so each product has different FAQ content.
  • Related guides: "Customers also bought" — manually curate (not Shopify's default algorithm). Group by region: "Also in the Asian series: Korean BBQ, Japanese Izakaya, Thai Street Food."
Priority 3

20 min

  • Collection banner: Each collection has a short description: "The Asian Series — 22 guides covering the most profitable Asian restaurant concepts in the US market."
  • Sort options: Enable "Best selling" as default. Buyers trust what other buyers chose.

Sales Funnel

The New Generation Sales Funnel

The old funnel was: run an ad → land on product page → buy. That funnel is dead for 25–35 year olds. The new funnel is: earn trust → educate → convert → retain → upsell. Each stage has specific tools. Every stage must exist before the next one works.

1
AWARENESS — "Who is this and why should I care?"
Primary tool: TikTok organic. 30-second videos from your hotel kitchen. Credential bomb format: "I'm a Head Chef running a $6M hotel kitchen. Here's what most restaurant owners get wrong about food cost." The viewer doesn't know you yet. They need to understand your authority in the first 3 seconds. Speak to their exact pain: they want to open a restaurant and they're scared of failing. Show them you understand that fear from the inside.
↓
2
INTEREST — "This person knows things I need to know"
Primary tools: TikTok profile → link in bio → landing page. After 3–5 videos, the viewer follows. They check your profile. Your bio converts them to the store: "59 restaurant operation guides · Built by a real Head Chef · $29.99 each · Link below." The landing page (the Higgsfield-style page we built) is the bridge between social trust and purchase intent. It answers: what exactly am I buying, who made this, and why should I trust it?
↓
3
CONSIDERATION — "I want this but I need to be sure"
Primary tools: Product page + email capture + Klaviyo welcome sequence. The buyer lands on a product page. They read. They hesitate. Two things push them over: (1) The P&L box — seeing "$2.9M projected revenue" next to $29.99 reframes the price completely. (2) The email capture popup — "Not ready? Get the free Restaurant Opening Checklist" → they opt in → 5-day Klaviyo sequence nurtures them → Day 3 email converts 40% of hesitators. This is where your email list becomes your most valuable asset.
↓
4
CONVERSION — "I'm buying this right now"
Primary tools: Shopify checkout + post-purchase upsell + order confirmation email. At checkout: no friction, one-click where possible, clear "Instant download" reassurance. Post-purchase upsell (use ReConvert app, free tier): immediately after checkout show "Add the Asian Bundle for $39.99 more (save $20)." 15–25% of buyers take the upsell. Order confirmation email: clear download instructions at the top + "You also get 22 free tools — here's how to access them." Reduces support emails by 80%.
↓
5
RETENTION + UPSELL — "I trust this brand. What else do they have?"
Primary tools: Klaviyo post-purchase sequence + new guide announcement emails. Day 7 after purchase: "How's the guide going? Here's the 3 biggest mistakes first-time operators make using it." Day 14: "New guide just dropped — [latest guide]. As a KitchenFounder customer, you get 20% off for 48 hours." Day 30: bundle offer. Repeat buyers have a 3× higher lifetime value than first-time buyers. The email list is the machine that converts one $29.99 sale into a $200+ lifetime customer.
The new generation insight: 25–35 year olds have the highest BS detector of any consumer generation in history. They grew up with ads. They can smell a funnel from 3 pages away. The way to sell to them is to not sell — to educate so thoroughly that buying feels like the logical next step, not a persuasion outcome. Every TikTok is education. Every email is education. The sale happens naturally when they trust you enough.

TikTok-First Content Engine · 5 Formats · Weekly Schedule04
TikTok · New Gen Marketing

TikTok-First: The Only Organic Channel That Matters in 2026

Pinterest is evergreen. Instagram is discovery. YouTube is depth. But TikTok is the only platform where a zero-follower account can reach 500,000 people on day one with the right video. For KitchenFounder, TikTok is not a marketing channel — it is the top of the entire funnel. Everything else is secondary.

The 5 TikTok Formats — Rotate Weekly

F1
The Credential Bomb
Hook: authority first · Best for: new followers · Post: Mon/Tue

Formula: "I [specific credential]. Here's what [audience fear]." Open in the kitchen. Fast cut. No intro, no "hey guys", no setup. The first 3 words must contain either a number, a dollar amount, or the word "mistake".

Example scripts:

  • "I've run a $6M hotel kitchen for 10 years. The #1 reason new restaurants fail in month 3 has nothing to do with the food."
  • "I built 59 restaurant guides from scratch. Here's the one thing every guide has that no YouTube tutorial will ever show you."
  • "I'm a Head Chef. A customer asked me last week if they should open a restaurant. Here's exactly what I told them."

End frame: "I documented all of it. Link in bio." — never say "buy my guide". Say "I documented it."

F2
The Number Reveal
Hook: specific data · Best for: virality · Post: Wed

Formula: Reveal one specific number that surprises people. Food cost %, revenue per cover, how much a chef earns vs how much the restaurant earns, how many kilos of protein per service.

  • "A Korean BBQ restaurant with 40 covers can do $1.8M in year one. Here's the exact model." → show a P&L on screen for 3 seconds
  • "The average restaurant throws away $3,200 of food every month. Here's why and how to fix it in one afternoon."
  • "I pay my Head Chef $75K a year. The restaurant generates $6M. Here's where the other $5.9M goes."
F3
The Day in My Life (Kitchen)
Hook: authenticity · Best for: parasocial bond · Post: Thu/Fri

Formula: 60–90 seconds. Film your actual shift. B-roll of mise en place, plating, service. Voiceover with one insight per scene. No scripting — stream of consciousness from someone who knows their craft. This format builds the deepest trust because it's real and unteachable by AI.

  • "5:30AM kitchen setup. Here's what I check before any chef arrives." (panning fridge check, mise en place stations)
  • "Saturday service — 200 covers. Here's the 3 things that went wrong and how we recovered."
  • "I'm plating 40 covers of wagyu right now. Here's why the doneness is everything." (action shot)
F4
The Guide Reveal
Hook: the product as content · Best for: direct conversion · Post: Sat

Formula: Open the guide on screen. Scroll through it at moderate speed. Voiceover explains 3 specific things you see. Never say "it costs $29.99" in the video — say the price on the end card only or in the caption.

  • Open the Japanese Mochi guide. "This is 28 mochi recipes with full costing, a floor plan for a 300sqft shop, and the P&L for a $200K/year business. Built for someone who's never made mochi before."
  • Open the NYC Steakhouse guide. Show the silver service scripts page. "This is the exact script my wait staff uses for a 12-cover private dining room. Word for word."
F5
The Myth Buster
Hook: disagreement · Best for: shares and comments · Post: Sun

Formula: Start with a common belief → disagree with it → explain why with specific expertise → end with the correct version. Disagreement drives comments. Comments drive algorithm. Algorithm drives reach.

  • "Everyone says passion is enough to open a restaurant. They're wrong. Here's what actually matters."
  • "TikTok keeps showing me restaurant business advice from people who've never run a restaurant. Let me fix that."
  • "The biggest lie in the restaurant industry: 'Location is everything.' Here's what the data actually says."

New Generation Sales Psychology · Gen Z + Millennial Buying Behaviour · 202608
New Gen Psychology

How the 25–35 Buyer Actually Makes Decisions in 2026

This is the generation that grew up watching every sales tactic invented. They've seen every countdown timer, every "only 3 left in stock", every fake testimonial. The tactics that worked in 2015 actively repel them now. Here is the psychology that actually works.

What They Respond To

  • Specificity over claims: "38% net margin" beats "high profit business." Numbers feel real. Adjectives feel like marketing.
  • The anti-pitch: "I'm not sure this is right for everyone" creates more desire than "this is for everyone."
  • Behind-the-scenes: Film the actual work. The messy kitchen prep shot converts better than a polished ad.
  • Peer proof: Someone like them who bought and used it. Not a celebrity. Not an expert. A peer.
  • Instant gratification: "Instant download" must be visible before the buy button. This generation doesn't wait.

What Kills Conversions

  • Fake scarcity: "Only 2 left!" on a digital product. They know. It destroys trust instantly.
  • Long-form sales copy: They scroll. The first 3 lines matter. Everything after must earn the next scroll.
  • Stock photography: They can smell it. Your iPhone in your kitchen beats a $5,000 commercial food shoot.
  • Complicated checkout: Every extra field costs you 10% of buyers. Email only at signup.
  • Upsell popups at checkout: Acceptable post-purchase. At checkout = rage-inducing.

The Reframe That Changes Everything

  • $29.99 vs $30K: A restaurant consultant charges $300/hour. 100 hours = $30,000. Your guide = $29.99. State this math explicitly in your copy.
  • Not a course, a tool: They've been burned by courses. "A working file you operate from daily, not a course you'll never finish" — this exact line converts.
  • You're not selling information — you're selling time: "This took me 10 years to learn. You're getting it in 2 hours."

The 4 Emotional Triggers for Your Buyer

01
Fear of wasting savings on a failed restaurant
Primary emotion. Address it first.

Your buyer has $50–200K saved. They are terrified of losing it. Your guide doesn't just promise profit — it prevents catastrophic failure. "The restaurant industry has a 60% failure rate in year one. The main cause is operators who didn't know what they didn't know. This guide is what they didn't know." This copy converts because it speaks to their real fear, not their aspiration.

02
Desire to escape the 9–5 through something they love
The dream. Make it feel achievable.

They don't just want a restaurant — they want freedom. The restaurant is the vehicle. Speak to the freedom: "Run your own kitchen. Set your own hours. Build something you're proud of." Then immediately ground it in reality: "With the right systems, it's possible. Without them, it's just hope." You're the person who gives them the systems.

03
Imposter syndrome — "I don't have the credentials"
Address the internal objection before they voice it.

"You don't need culinary school. You need the system. The system is in this guide." Your credential (Head Chef, 10 years, $6M operation) lends them credibility by association. When they follow your system, they're operating at your level. Make that explicit.

04
FOMO — "Someone else is already doing this"
Real urgency, not fake.

In 2026 the restaurant industry is genuinely recovering and new concepts are opening. "The Japanese dessert café market in the US is underserved. The operators who move in the next 12–24 months will take the best locations at the best rents." Real market urgency, grounded in real data. Not a countdown timer. A genuine market window.

The sentence that converts better than anything else: "This is not a course. It is a working file built by someone who has run exactly this kind of restaurant, built for someone who hasn't — yet." Put this on your homepage, your product pages, and your TikTok bio. Test it. It speaks to every emotional trigger simultaneously.
