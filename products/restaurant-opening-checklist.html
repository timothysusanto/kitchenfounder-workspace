Phase 1 — Concept & Planning
6–12 months before opening
  • Define your concept clearly — cuisine type, service style, price point, target customer
    Write it in one sentence. If you can't, your concept is not clear enough yet.
  • Research your target market — who lives and works in your area, what they spend, what's missing
  • Analyse your top 3 competitors — visit them, eat there, study their menu and pricing
  • Write your business plan — concept, market, menu, financial projections, funding needs
  • Set your financial targets — revenue, food cost %, labour cost %, rent %, net profit target
  • Determine your opening budget — fit-out, equipment, working capital, contingency (add 20%)
  • Choose your legal structure — sole trader, LLC, partnership, corporation
    US: LLC is the most common for small restaurants — limited liability, flexible taxation.
Phase 2 — Location & Lease
4–8 months before opening
  • Define your location criteria — foot traffic, parking, demographics, visibility, competing concepts nearby
  • Review at least 5 potential sites before committing to any
  • Negotiate rent at % of revenue — target under 10% of projected revenue
    Ask for: rent-free fit-out period, break clause at year 2, % of revenue cap clause.
  • Get a lawyer to review the lease — never sign without independent legal review
  • Confirm the space fits your concept — kitchen size, ventilation, gas supply, power capacity
  • Check zoning and use permits — confirm the space is approved for food service and liquor if needed
Phase 3 — Licences & Compliance
3–6 months before opening
  • Business licence — register with your city/county
  • Food handler permits — required for all food-handling staff in most US states
  • Food service licence / health permit — from your local health department
  • Liquor licence — apply early, can take 60–120 days in many states
    Most expensive and slowest licence. Apply before everything else if you plan to serve alcohol.
  • Fire safety inspection — hood system, suppression, extinguishers, exits
  • Sign permit — many cities require permits for exterior signage
  • Music licence — BMI and ASCAP if playing background music
  • Employer Identification Number (EIN) — required for US payroll and taxes
Phase 4 — Kitchen & Equipment
2–4 months before opening
  • Design your kitchen layout — flow from receive to prep to cook to plate to pass
    Good kitchen design prevents 90% of service bottlenecks. Hire a kitchen designer if budget allows.
  • Buy vs lease decision on major equipment — ovens, refrigeration, dishwasher
  • Source all smallwares — pots, pans, knives, cutting boards, containers, labels
  • Set up your cold storage system — walk-in, reach-in, labelling, FIFO rotation
  • Install and test all equipment — before your first staff member arrives
  • Set up your cleaning schedule — daily, weekly, monthly tasks assigned by role
Phase 5 — Menu & Suppliers
2–3 months before opening
  • Finalise your opening menu — maximum 20 items for a first-time operator
    Small menus execute better. You can always add items. You cannot un-complicate a menu mid-service.
  • Calculate food cost % for every dish — target 18–25% blended average
  • Source minimum 2 suppliers per key ingredient — never single-source your proteins or produce
  • Negotiate credit terms with suppliers — 30-day net terms keeps cash flow positive early
  • Create standard recipes with gram weights — every dish, written down, non-negotiable
  • Build your ordering par levels — how much of each item to hold in stock at all times
  • Design your menu (print and digital) — pricing, layout, allergen information
Phase 6 — Hiring & Training
4–6 weeks before opening
  • Define every role — job descriptions, hours, pay rates, responsibilities
  • Hire kitchen team first — head chef or lead cook minimum 4 weeks before opening
  • Hire floor team 2 weeks before opening — servers, host, manager
  • Run full kitchen training — every dish cooked by every cook, every recipe followed exactly
  • Run floor training — menu knowledge, service scripts, allergen awareness, POS system
  • Run at least 2 full mock services with friends and family before opening day
  • Set up payroll system before first pay period — Gusto, ADP, or QuickBooks Payroll
Phase 7 — Technology & Systems
4–6 weeks before opening
  • Choose and install your POS system — Toast, Square, or Lightspeed are most common for US restaurants
  • Set up online ordering if applicable — Toast, Square, or third party (DoorDash, Uber Eats)
  • Set up reservations system — OpenTable, Resy, or Google Reservations
  • Build your website — minimum: menu, location, hours, booking link
  • Claim all social media handles — Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, Google Business
  • Set up Google Business Profile — critical for local search visibility
  • Set up accounting software — QuickBooks or Xero, connected to your bank account
Phase 8 — Pre-Opening & Launch
1–2 weeks before opening
  • Do a full health inspection walk-through yourself before the official inspection
  • Run a soft opening — invitation only, 50% capacity, test your full service flow
  • Debrief after every soft opening service — what broke, what was slow, what confused guests
  • Fix the top 3 problems before opening day — don't open with known issues
  • Stock your bar and cellar — opening night stock plus 2 weeks buffer
  • Announce your opening date on social media — 1 week out, build anticipation
  • Set up your SMS/email list capture from day one — every customer is a future marketing channel
  • Prepare your team mentally — opening week will be hard, messy, and exhausting. That is normal.
