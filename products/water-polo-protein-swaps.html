Moroccan Lamb Stew
Mrouzia-inspired · Slow-braised · Harissa on side · $55 Lunch
Lamb shoulder diced · ras el hanout · preserved lemon · chickpeas · tomato · slow-braised 2.5 hours · finished with fresh coriander and pomegranate seeds
GF Halal ✓ ~42g protein
Ingredients — Batch (serves 20)
The Lamb
Lamb shoulder, diced 4cm (Halal certified)4 kg
Neutral oil for searing3 tbsp
The Stew Base
Brown onion, finely diced4 large
Garlic cloves, crushed10 cloves
Fresh ginger, grated40 g
Ras el hanout spice blend4 tbsp
Ground cumin2 tsp
Ground coriander2 tsp
Ground cinnamon1 tsp
Smoked paprika1 tsp
Canned crushed tomato2 × 400g tins
Beef stock (Halal)1.5 L
Preserved lemon, rind only, finely diced3 pieces
Cooked chickpeas400 g
Finish + Garnish
Fresh coriander, roughly chopped1 bunch
Pomegranate seeds100 g
Harissa paste — served on the side in small ramekinsto table
Steamed couscous or jasmine rice alongside (pre-existing side)service
Method
  1. 1Sear in batches: pat lamb dry — this is critical. Wet lamb steams instead of searing. High heat, small batches. Deep brown crust on all sides. Remove each batch and set aside. Do not crowd the pan. Total searing time: 20–25 min for 4kg.
  2. 2Build the base: sweat onion in the same pan 8 min until soft and golden. Add garlic + ginger, cook 2 min. Add all dry spices — ras el hanout, cumin, coriander, cinnamon, paprika. Stir constantly for 90 seconds to bloom the spices in the oil. The kitchen will smell extraordinary.
  3. 3Braise: return lamb to pot. Add crushed tomato + beef stock + preserved lemon rind. Bring to simmer, cover tightly. Braise at 160°C for 2 hours. Check at 1.5 hrs — lamb should be beginning to yield. Add chickpeas at the 2-hour mark. Continue uncovered for final 30 min to reduce sauce to a thick, coating consistency.
  4. 4Service: taste and adjust salt. Transfer to hotel pan for buffet service. Scatter fresh coriander + pomegranate seeds just before guests arrive — not in advance. Set harissa ramekins alongside. The pomegranate seeds add colour, acidity and texture that cuts through the richness of the lamb.
Kitchen NoteStart this at 9:00am for a 12:00 lunch service. The 2.5hr braise is non-negotiable — rushed lamb stew is the most obvious failure in buffet catering. Hold at 75°C in hotel pan. If sauce tightens too much on hold, add a splash of stock to loosen. Never add water — dilutes flavour.
Why This Works on the MenuThe existing Day 3 menu has beef meatballs in tomato ragù at lunch. The Moroccan lamb stew is a completely different protein (lamb vs beef), completely different technique (slow braise vs meatball), and a richer, spiced Moroccan flavour profile vs the Italian ragù. They sit on the same buffet without competing. Athletes get genuine choice — European Italian vs North African Moroccan — both within the Day 3 Middle Eastern / Mediterranean theme.

🔥 Fish Addition · Chinese Cuisine · Day 2 or Day 4
HK Crispy Skin Barramundi
豉汁脆皮魚 — Black Bean & Ginger Sauce · Wok-Tossed · $55 Lunch / $72 Dinner
Barramundi fillet skin-on · dried skin technique · wok-seared skin-side down until glass-crispy · black bean + ginger + chilli sauce spooned over · spring onion oil finish
GF Halal ✓ ~34g protein
Ingredients — Per Batch (serves 20)
The Fish
Barramundi fillet, skin-on, portioned 160–180g20 portions
Fine salt — for drying the skin onlylight sprinkle
Neutral oil (rice bran or vegetable) for searing3 tbsp per pan
Chinese Black Bean Sauce (make in advance)
Fermented black beans (douchi), rinsed and roughly chopped60 g
Fresh ginger, very finely minced30 g
Garlic cloves, minced6 cloves
Long red chilli, sliced (seeds in for heat, remove for mild)2 chillies
Shaoxing rice wine (halal substitute: dry apple juice or omit)60 ml
Light soy sauce3 tbsp
Oyster sauce2 tbsp
Chicken stock (Halal)120 ml
Sesame oil — added off heat only1 tsp
Cornstarch slurry (1 tbsp cornstarch + 2 tbsp cold water)to thicken
Finish + Garnish
Spring onion, cut into 5cm batons — blanched 30 sec in boiling oil3 bunches
Fresh red chilli, sliced thin — garnish only2 chillies
Steamed jasmine rice alongside (pre-existing)service
Method
  1. 1Dry the skin — this step cannot be skipped: score the skin 3–4 times with a sharp knife (prevents curling). Salt the skin lightly. Leave uncovered on a rack in the cool room for minimum 1 hour — ideally 2 hours. The surface moisture must be completely gone. Wet skin never crisps. This is the difference between a great fish dish and a mediocre one.
  2. 2Make the black bean sauce ahead: heat wok to high. Add 1 tbsp oil. Fry black beans + ginger + garlic + chilli for 90 seconds — let them catch slightly, the toasty note is important. Add Shaoxing wine (or substitute), let it sizzle and reduce by half. Add soy + oyster sauce + stock. Simmer 3 min. Add cornstarch slurry to thicken to a light coating sauce. Off heat: sesame oil. Hold warm in bain-marie.
  3. 3Cook to order at the station: heat heavy-based pan (not non-stick) with oil until smoking. Place fish skin-side down. Press gently with a fish slice for the first 30 seconds to prevent curling. Cook skin-side 3.5–4 min — do not move it. When the skin is glass-crispy and golden, flip once. Cook flesh-side 90 seconds only. The fish should be just cooked through at 60°C — still moist inside.
  4. 4Plate and serve: place fish skin-side UP on the plate. Spoon 2 tbsp warm black bean sauce over the flesh side only — never over the skin (it goes soft immediately). Blanch spring onion batons in very hot oil for 30 seconds, lay over the fish. Garnish with chilli slices. Serve immediately.
Crispy Skin RuleSauce goes on the FLESH side only. Never on the skin. Once the sauce touches the crispy skin it turns soft within 30 seconds and the entire point of the dish is lost. Brief the service team specifically on this. If doing buffet scale — cook in batches and serve skin-side up in hotel pans with the sauce in a separate insert alongside for guests to self-serve.
Buffet Scale AdaptationFor 400 covers — sear fish in batches of 8–10 portions, transfer to oven trays skin-side up, hold at 160°C for max 8 min. Do not stack. Do not cover. Keep sauce in a separate bain-marie. Staff at the station spoon sauce over flesh as they plate — never pre-sauced. Replenish in 20-portion batches every 15–20 min. Fish sitting longer than 20 min in a hotel pan loses the skin texture entirely.
Why This Is Distinctly Chinese — Not Just "Asian Fish"Douchi (fermented black beans) is one of the oldest Chinese condiments — used in Cantonese cooking for over 2,000 years. The combination of douchi + ginger + garlic is the defining flavour base of Hong Kong-style fish dishes. It is immediately recognisable to Chinese and Hong Kong athletes as home cooking. The crispy skin technique comes from Cantonese roast culture — the pursuit of crackling, glass-crispy skin is a Chinese culinary obsession. This dish is specifically Chinese, not generically Asian. That specificity is what makes it respectful and memorable.
