72-Hour Braised Beef Short Rib
Red wine & thyme jus · horseradish cream · crispy shallots · buttered mash
Braise 160°C · 6–8 hrs Prep 45 min · Braise overnight Serves 4
Ingredients
The Short Rib
Beef short rib (bone-in)1.2 kg (4 ribs)
Olive oil30 ml
Salt, cracked black pepperto season
Brown onion, halved1 large
Carrot, rough chop2 medium
Celery stalks2 stalks
Garlic cloves, crushed6 cloves
Tomato paste30 g
Red wine (shiraz or cab sav)500 ml
Beef stock800 ml
Thyme sprigs5
Bay leaves2
Horseradish Cream
Crème fraîche150 g
Fresh horseradish, grated2 tbsp
Lemon juice1 tsp
Chives, finely sliced1 tbsp
To Serve
Mashed potato (see tip)200 g/plate
Crispy shallots (store-bought fine)handful
Watercress or micro herbssmall bunch
Method
  1. 1Preheat oven to 160°C. Season short ribs generously on all sides with salt and cracked pepper. Heat oil in a large heavy-based braising pan over high heat. Sear ribs until deep mahogany on all sides — 3–4 minutes per side. Do not rush this step. Remove and set aside.
  2. 2In the same pan, add onion cut-side down. Char until deeply caramelised, 4 minutes. Add carrot and celery. Cook 3 minutes. Add garlic and tomato paste. Stir and cook 2 minutes until paste darkens slightly.
  3. 3Deglaze with red wine. Scrape all the fond off the base. Reduce wine by half over high heat. Add beef stock, thyme, and bay leaves. Return ribs to pan — liquid should come three-quarters up the ribs.
  4. 4Cover tightly with a lid or double layer of foil. Braise in oven 6–8 hours until the meat is yielding and pulls cleanly from the bone. For overnight: reduce temp to 130°C and braise 10–12 hours.
  5. 5Remove ribs carefully. Strain the braising liquid through a fine sieve. Return liquid to high heat and reduce by 60–70% until it coats the back of a spoon and has a glossy, sticky jus consistency. Season to taste.
  6. 6Horseradish cream: fold grated horseradish and lemon juice through crème fraîche. Season. Keep cold. Add chives at service.
  7. 7To plate: place a generous mound of buttered mash slightly off-centre. Rest one short rib against the mash. Spoon 60 ml jus around the plate. Quenelle of horseradish cream to the side. Crispy shallots over the rib. Watercress to finish.
Chef's Tip The mash here is non-negotiable — it must be Robuchon-style: equal parts potato and butter by weight, passed through a tamis. Don't serve a dry mash under a 6-hour braise. The jus is the star so don't over-season it — it will concentrate further when reheated at service. Short ribs braise better the day before and reheated gently in their jus.

Pan-Roasted Duck Breast
Confit leg croquette · cherry & red wine gastrique · celeriac purée · wilted spinach
Duck breast 57°C core · Skin side down first Prep 30 min · Cook 20 min Serves 4
Ingredients
Duck Breast
Duck breasts (Muscovy or Pekin)4 × 220 g
Salt, white pepperto season
Thyme sprigs4
Garlic cloves, skin on4
Cherry Gastrique
Fresh or frozen cherries, pitted250 g
Red wine150 ml
Red wine vinegar40 ml
Caster sugar60 g
Chicken or duck stock200 ml
Butter20 g
Celeriac Purée
Celeriac, peeled and diced500 g
Butter60 g
Double cream80 ml
Salt, white pepperto taste
To Finish
Baby spinach120 g
Butter15 g
Confit leg croquette (optional)1 per plate
Method
  1. 1Score duck skin in a fine crosshatch — cuts should reach the fat but not into the flesh. Season well with salt and white pepper. Place skin-side down in a cold, dry frying pan. Turn heat to medium. Render the fat slowly for 10–12 minutes until skin is deep golden and crisp and most of the fat has rendered out.
  2. 2Flip. Add thyme and garlic to the pan. Tilt pan and baste with the rendered duck fat for 2 minutes. Transfer to oven at 180°C for 5–6 minutes to a core temp of 57°C (medium-rare to medium). Rest on a rack for 8 minutes minimum — non-negotiable.
  3. 3Cherry gastrique: in a small saucepan, combine sugar and red wine vinegar over medium heat without stirring until it becomes a light amber caramel. Immediately add red wine — it will bubble furiously. Add cherries and stock. Reduce by half. Season. Mount with cold butter off the heat for gloss.
  4. 4Celeriac purée: simmer celeriac in salted water until completely tender, 15–20 minutes. Drain well. Blend with butter and cream until silky smooth. Pass through a fine sieve. Season aggressively — celeriac needs salt.
  5. 5Wilt spinach in butter in a hot pan. Season, drain excess moisture.
  6. 6To plate: smear celeriac purée across one side of the plate with the back of a spoon. Slice duck breast into 4–5 slices on a bias, fan on the purée. Nest spinach alongside. Spoon cherry gastrique over and around the duck. Garnish with micro herbs or a few reserved fresh cherries.
Chef's Tip Always start the duck breast in a cold pan — this slowly renders the thick fat layer without burning the skin. A hot pan will char the outside before the fat renders. The resting time is critical: duck tightens dramatically if carved too soon and the juices flood the plate rather than the meat. If doing confit leg croquettes, make them the day before — they hold much better cold-crumbed then fried at service.

Crispy-Skin Pan-Roast Salmon
Crushed pea & mint purée · brown butter & capers · charred lemon · cucumber ribbon
Salmon 50–52°C core · Skin crispy Prep 20 min · Cook 12 min Serves 4
Ingredients
Salmon
Atlantic salmon fillet, skin on4 × 180 g
Neutral oil (rice bran)30 ml
Salt, white pepperto season
Brown Butter & Capers
Unsalted butter100 g
Baby capers, drained3 tbsp
Lemon juice2 tbsp
Flat-leaf parsley, chopped2 tbsp
Crushed Pea Purée
Frozen peas400 g
Butter40 g
Fresh mint leaves10 g
Crème fraîche3 tbsp
Lemon zest½ lemon
Salt, white pepperto taste
To Serve
Lemon halves, for charring2
Cucumber, peeled into ribbons½ cucumber
Dill sprigs or micro herbsto garnish
Method
  1. 1Pat salmon skin completely dry with paper towel — this is the single most important step for crispy skin. Season the flesh side, not the skin side. Press the skin gently with your hand to ensure total contact with the pan.
  2. 2Heat a heavy stainless or cast iron pan over high heat until it just starts to smoke. Add oil. Place salmon skin-side down. Immediately press each fillet flat with a fish slice for 10 seconds to prevent curling. Cook skin-side down for 6–7 minutes without moving — 80% of the cooking happens on this side.
  3. 3Flip gently. Cook flesh side 2 minutes. Remove from heat. The residual heat will carry it to 50–52°C core (medium — just translucent in the very centre). Rest 3 minutes.
  4. 4Pea purée: blanch peas in boiling salted water 2 minutes. Drain. Blend with butter, crème fraîche, mint, and lemon zest. Blend until smooth but with some texture remaining — this is crushed pea, not soup. Season well. Keep warm.
  5. 5Brown butter: in a light-coloured pan, melt butter over medium heat. Swirl continuously until it turns golden brown and smells nutty — 4 minutes. Watch it carefully; it goes from golden to burnt in 20 seconds. Off heat, add capers, lemon juice, and parsley. It will spit. Season.
  6. 6Char lemon halves cut-side down in a dry hot pan for 2 minutes.
  7. 7To plate: place pea purée in the centre. Lay salmon skin-side up on the purée. Spoon brown butter caper sauce over and around. Cucumber ribbons alongside. Dill to garnish. Charred lemon to the side.
Chef's Tip Dry skin = crispy skin. There are no shortcuts. If the skin sticks to the pan, it simply means the skin wasn't ready — leave it another 30 seconds and it will release. Forcing it tears the skin. The brown butter must be made to order — it breaks and tastes different if held. The pea purée holds well for 2 hours at service temperature.

Herb-Crusted Lamb Rump
Dijon & herb crust · smashed minted peas · roasted root vegetables · rosemary jus
Lamb 58–60°C core · Medium Prep 25 min · Cook 25 min Serves 4
Ingredients
Lamb Rump
Lamb rump (fat cap on)4 × 220 g
Salt, cracked pepperto season
Neutral oil30 ml
Herb Crust
Dijon mustard4 tbsp
Fresh rosemary, finely chopped2 tbsp
Fresh thyme leaves2 tbsp
Flat-leaf parsley, chopped3 tbsp
Panko breadcrumbs60 g
Garlic, minced2 cloves
Olive oil2 tbsp
Rosemary Jus
Lamb or beef stock (good quality)500 ml
Red wine150 ml
Rosemary sprigs3
Garlic cloves2
Butter30 g
To Serve
Frozen peas, blanched300 g
Fresh mint, torn10 g
Butter20 g
Roasted root veg (parsnip, carrot)200 g
Method
  1. 1Score the fat cap on the lamb rump in a crosshatch. Season all over. Sear fat-side down in a hot ovenproof pan with oil for 4 minutes until the fat renders and browns. Sear remaining sides, 2 minutes each. Transfer pan to oven at 200°C.
  2. 2Roast 10–12 minutes to a core of 52°C. Remove from oven. Brush top and sides generously with Dijon mustard immediately while hot.
  3. 3Mix herbs, panko, garlic, and olive oil together into a coarse paste. Season. Press firmly onto the mustard-coated lamb — the mustard acts as glue. Return to oven (or under a hot grill/salamander) for 4–5 minutes until the crust is golden and set. Core should now be 58–60°C. Rest on a rack 8 minutes.
  4. 4Rosemary jus: reduce red wine by half in a small saucepan. Add stock, rosemary, and garlic. Reduce by 60% until glossy and lightly syrupy. Strain. Mount with cold butter off the heat. Season. Keep warm.
  5. 5Smashed minted peas: warm blanched peas with butter and mint in a pan over medium heat. Season. Roughly crush with a fork — leave some whole for texture. Not smooth, not whole. In between.
  6. 6To plate: place smashed peas to one side. Rest lamb rump slightly against the peas showing the crust. Arrange roasted roots around. Pour rosemary jus around the plate. Finish with a small sprig of fresh rosemary.
Chef's Tip Lamb rump is forgiving and highly consistent on a pub menu — it holds well under a salamander and responds better to high-heat roasting than lamb rack. The Dijon crust application must happen the moment the lamb comes out of the oven — the heat helps the mustard set and grip the crumb. If the lamb has cooled, the crust slides off.

Slow-Roast Pork Belly
Perfect crackling · apple & calvados purée · sage-braised lentils · calvados jus
Belly 160°C 3 hrs · Blast 240°C crackling Prep 30 min · Total 4 hrs Serves 4–6
Ingredients
Pork Belly
Pork belly, skin on, scored1.2 kg
Sea salt (for skin)2 tbsp
Fennel seeds, crushed1 tbsp
Garlic powder1 tsp
White wine200 ml
Chicken stock300 ml
Onion, sliced1 large
Apple, halved1
Bay leaves, thyme3 each
Apple & Calvados Purée
Granny Smith apples, peeled, diced4 large
Calvados (or apple brandy)60 ml
Butter40 g
Caster sugar1 tbsp
Lemon juice1 tsp
Calvados Jus
Reserved pork cooking juices300 ml
Calvados50 ml
Butter25 g
To Serve
Puy lentils, cooked200 g
Sage leaves, fried crispy8 leaves
Apple, thinly sliced½
Method
  1. 1Score the pork skin deeply — cuts all the way through the fat to the flesh, spaced 1 cm apart. Rub sea salt, fennel seeds, and garlic powder vigorously into the cuts and all over the skin. Pat the flesh side dry. Leave uncovered in the fridge overnight or minimum 4 hours — the drier the skin, the better the crackling.
  2. 2Preheat oven to 160°C. Place onion, apple, bay, and thyme in the base of a deep roasting tray. Pour in white wine and stock. Place pork belly skin-side up on top of the aromatics — the belly should sit above the liquid, not submerged. The steam from the liquid keeps the flesh moist while the skin stays dry and blisteringly hot above.
  3. 3Roast at 160°C for 3 hours. The flesh should be completely tender and yielding — probe it; it should offer no resistance. Carefully pour off the cooking liquids and reserve. Now blast the belly at 240°C (no fan) for 20–25 minutes until the skin bubbles, blisters, and crisps into full crackling. Watch it carefully — it can char quickly after 20 minutes.
  4. 4Apple calvados purée: cook diced apple with butter and sugar in a covered saucepan over low heat until completely soft — 15 minutes. Remove lid, add calvados. Cook 2 minutes. Blend completely smooth. Season with lemon juice and salt. Silky and slightly sharp — this cuts through the fat perfectly.
  5. 5Calvados jus: strain reserved cooking juices. Skim fat. Reduce by half. Add calvados. Reduce 2 more minutes. Mount with cold butter. Season.
  6. 6To plate: slice pork belly into thick portions. Spoon apple purée to one side. Place lentils (warmed with a little butter and seasoning) alongside. Rest pork belly on the purée. Spoon jus around. Crispy sage leaves over the top. Apple slices for freshness.
Chef's Tip The overnight dry salt cure is not optional — it is the difference between glass-like crackling and leathery skin. The flash-blast technique (low slow then 240°C) produces more consistent crackling than starting hot. If the crackling isn't crisping at 240°C, the skin still has moisture — give it another 10 minutes. Puy lentils are the ideal pairing: they absorb the pork fat beautifully and add textural contrast.

Portobello & Porcini Wellington
Wild mushroom & truffle duxelles · golden puff pastry · roasted cherry tomatoes · red wine reduction
Oven 200°C · 25 min · Golden pastry Prep 40 min · Bake 25 min Serves 4
Ingredients
Wellington
Large portobello mushrooms4 × 100 g
Puff pastry, good quality500 g
Egg yolk + splash cream (egg wash)2 yolks
Porcini Duxelles
Mixed mushrooms (chestnut, field, shiitake)600 g
Dried porcini, soaked 20 min25 g
Shallots, finely diced3 large
Garlic, minced3 cloves
Butter50 g
Olive oil2 tbsp
Dry sherry or white wine80 ml
Thyme leaves1 tbsp
Truffle oil (optional finish)1 tsp
Salt, white pepperto season
Red Wine Reduction
Red wine300 ml
Vegetable stock400 ml
Shallot, sliced1
Rosemary, thyme2 sprigs each
Butter30 g
To Serve
Cherry tomatoes, on the vine2 vines
Wilted spinach or French beans120 g
Watercresssmall bunch
Method
  1. 1Prepare the portobello mushrooms: remove stalks. Brush caps with olive oil, season. Sear in a very hot pan, 2 minutes per side. They must be completely dry after searing — place on a wire rack and cool completely. Pat dry with paper towel. Moisture is the enemy of crispy pastry.
  2. 2Porcini duxelles: drain soaked porcini, reserve the soaking liquid (strain to remove grit). Finely chop all mushrooms — they should be almost minced. Sweat shallots and garlic in butter and oil over medium heat until soft, 5 minutes. Add all mushrooms. Cook over high heat, stirring frequently, until completely dry — 15–20 minutes. This is critical. Add sherry and porcini soaking liquid. Reduce until completely evaporated. Add thyme. Season aggressively. Cool completely. Add truffle oil if using.
  3. 3Roll puff pastry to 3–4 mm thickness. Cut into 4 portions large enough to wrap one mushroom with 3 cm overlap. Place a generous spoonful of duxelles in the centre of each pastry rectangle. Set a portobello mushroom on top gill-side down. Cover with more duxelles. Fold pastry up and over, sealing edges with egg wash. Press firmly. Flip seam-side down. Score the top decoratively. Refrigerate 30 minutes minimum — this is crucial for holding the shape.
  4. 4Brush generously with egg wash. Place on a baking paper-lined tray. Bake at 200°C for 20–25 minutes until deep golden brown and the pastry sounds hollow when tapped.
  5. 5Red wine reduction: sweat shallot with herbs. Add red wine. Reduce by two-thirds. Add vegetable stock. Reduce by half until glossy and slightly syrupy. Strain through fine sieve. Mount with cold butter. Season.
  6. 6Roast cherry tomato vines in the oven for the last 8 minutes of the Wellington baking time — 180°C until just blistered.
  7. 7To plate: place Wellington slightly off-centre. Wilted greens or French beans alongside. Roasted cherry tomato vine draped over or beside. Pour red wine reduction around. Watercress for height and freshness.
Chef's Tip The duxelles must be completely bone dry before it goes into the pastry — any residual moisture will steam inside and make the pastry soggy. The refrigeration rest before baking is not optional — it firms the butter in the pastry and prevents it ballooning unevenly in the oven. This is your vegetarian showpiece — it should look as impressive as the beef rib. If using shop-bought puff pastry, use all-butter puff only. The cheap vegetable-fat versions don't colour properly and taste flat.

Chef's Notes — Mise en Place
  • Short rib and pork belly must be started the night before service — minimum 6 hours each
  • Duck breasts seared, rested, and held at 52°C — finish to order under salamander
  • Wellington assembled and refrigerated — baked to order (25 min)
  • All sauces made in bulk each morning — held hot in bain marie at service
  • Pea purées made fresh each service — hold no more than 2 hours
  • Brown butter for salmon made to order — takes 4 minutes, cannot be held
Menu Engineering Notes
  • Star (High profit, High popularity): Salmon, Lamb Rump
  • Ploughorse (High profit, Lower popularity): Wellington, Pork Belly
  • Puzzle (Low profit, High popularity): Beef Short Rib
  • Dog (Low profit, Low popularity): None — this is a clean menu
  • Short Rib's high food cost % is offset by its role as the signature draw — it sells everything else on the table (wine, sides, desserts)
  • Promote Wellington more actively — lowest food cost, highest margin
The Philosophy of the Pub Fine Dining Menu
A great pub kitchen does not try to be a restaurant. It tries to be the best possible version of itself. These six dishes are built on that principle — every one of them has classical British or European roots, every one has been refined into something worth driving for, and every one can be executed consistently by a team of four on a busy Friday night. No ingredient requires specialist sourcing. No technique requires special equipment beyond a heavy braising pan and a reliable oven.

The short rib is the soul of the menu. The wellington is the proof that this kitchen takes its vegetarian guests seriously. The salmon is the dish that brings in the guest who wasn't sure they'd like pub food. The duck is the surprise — it outperforms its price point every time. The lamb is the reliable bestseller that pays the wages. The pork belly is the one that makes people come back.

Six dishes. One kitchen. One standard. No exceptions.
