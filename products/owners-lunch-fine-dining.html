FRENCH · MAIN 01
Duck Confit — Pomme Sarladaise
Cherry jus · Wilted cavolo nero · Crispy skin
Ingredients · Per Person
Duck Marylands (200g each) 12 pcs
Duck fat 2kg
Fresh thyme, bay leaf, garlic as needed
Desiree potatoes 3kg
Shallots, sliced 300g
Cavolo nero 600g
Morello cherry jam 200g
Chicken stock, red wine 500ml each
Unsalted butter 150g
Method
  1. Season duck, cure overnight with thyme, garlic, salt
  2. Confit in duck fat at 85°C for 8–10 hours until tender
  3. Pomme sarladaise: layer sliced potato, shallots, duck fat — roast 180°C 45 min
  4. Cherry jus: reduce red wine, stock, cherry jam, mount with cold butter
  5. Before service: crisp duck skin skin-side down in dry pan 4 min
  6. Wilt cavolo nero in butter, season with lemon and fleur de sel
🍽️ Plating

Press pomme sarladaise into a round mould, centre plate. Rest duck confit on top, skin facing up — lacquered, burnished. Cascade cherry jus around the base. Place cavolo nero to the left. Finish with a single sprig of fresh thyme and fleur de sel on the skin.

FRENCH · MAIN 02
Sole Meunière — Beurre Noisette
Capers · Lemon · Haricots verts · Pomme purée
Ingredients · Per Person
Sole fillets (180g) 12 pcs
Plain flour, seasoned 200g
Clarified butter 300g
Unsalted butter (noisette) 400g
Capers, lemon juice, parsley as needed
Haricots verts (fine beans) 600g
Désirée potatoes (purée) 2.5kg
Cream, butter (for purée) 400ml / 300g
Method
  1. Pomme purée: boil potatoes, pass through tamis, fold in warm cream + cold butter — silky, not stiff
  2. Blanch haricots verts in salted water, refresh, finish in butter to order
  3. Dust sole lightly in seasoned flour, shake off excess
  4. Cook in clarified butter, 2 min each side — golden, no more
  5. Beurre noisette: foam unsalted butter until hazelnut aroma, add lemon juice, capers, parsley — pour tableside
🍽️ Plating

Quenelle of pomme purée to the left. Sole fillet laid across at an angle, skin-side up. Haricots verts stacked neatly beside. Beurre noisette poured from a small copper saucepan tableside — the aroma does the talking. Finish with a thin lemon wheel and micro parsley.

Chef's Note This is one of the most technically honest dishes in French cooking — nowhere to hide. The beurre noisette poured tableside is a moment of pure theatre. Owners will remember the smell before they remember the taste.

FRENCH · MAIN 03
Beef Tenderloin — Sauce Bordelaise
Pomme fondant · Gratin dauphinois · Bone marrow
Ingredients · Per Person
Beef tenderloin (200g portions) 12 pcs
Bone marrow, soaked 12 rounds
Red Bordeaux wine 750ml
Beef demi-glace 500ml
Shallots, thyme, bay as needed
Désirée potatoes (fondant) 12 rounds
Chicken stock (for fondant) 500ml
Butter, thyme, garlic 200g / as needed
Watercress (garnish) 1 bunch
Method
  1. Bordelaise: sweat shallots, add Bordeaux, reduce by ¾, add demi-glace, reduce, strain, mount with cold butter
  2. Pomme fondant: cylinder-cut potatoes, sear flat sides golden in clarified butter, add stock, thyme, garlic — roast 180°C until tender and glazed
  3. Season and tie tenderloin, sear all sides in very hot cast iron — hard crust, rare inside
  4. Rest 5 min minimum — non-negotiable
  5. Bone marrow: poach in salted water 5 min, season with fleur de sel, parsley
🍽️ Plating

Tenderloin sliced on the bias, two pieces fanned slightly. Pomme fondant behind, standing tall. Bone marrow round resting on top of the beef — classical and imposing. Bordelaise poured in a clean arc around the plate. Three watercress leaves. Nothing more.

Food Cost ~32% Prep: Day before Service: 10 min GF
FRENCH · MAIN 04
Chicken Ballotine — Morel Cream
Truffle stuffing · Asparagus · Sauce suprême
Ingredients · Per Person
Free-range chicken breasts 12 pcs
Dried morel mushrooms 60g
Chicken mousse (breast + cream) 300g
Black truffle paste 40g
Asparagus spears 36 spears
Chicken stock, cream 500ml / 300ml
Shallots, tarragon, butter as needed
Method
  1. Butterfly chicken breast, fill with truffle mousse, roll tight in clingfilm
  2. Poach ballotine at 72°C for 18 min — precise temperature, perfect result
  3. Rehydrate morels in warm water, sauté in butter, add cream, reduce, finish with tarragon
  4. Sauce suprême: reduce chicken stock with shallots, cream, mount with butter — velvety
  5. Asparagus: blanch 90 seconds, finish in foaming butter, season
  6. Sear ballotine in butter to colour before service
🍽️ Plating

Ballotine sliced on a bias — two rounds showing the truffle cross-section inside. Asparagus laid alongside in a tight bundle. Morel cream spooned generously to one side. Sauce suprême in a thin elegant pool. Finish with a shaving of fresh truffle if budget allows — or truffle oil drops.

Food Cost ~24% Wow factor: HIGH Prep: Morning of
FRENCH · MAIN 05
Lamb Rack — Persillade Crust
Ratatouille niçoise · Lamb jus · Tapenade
Ingredients · Per Person
Lamb rack (3 cutlets/person) 4 racks
Dijon mustard 80g
Sourdough breadcrumbs 200g
Flat parsley, garlic, lemon zest as needed
Eggplant, zucchini, capsicum, tomato 300g each
Kalamata olive tapenade 150g
Lamb bones for jus 1kg
Method
  1. Lamb jus: roast bones, deglaze, reduce with rosemary and thyme — deep and glossy
  2. Ratatouille: dice vegetables uniform, cook separately, combine — never mushy
  3. Persillade: blitz parsley, garlic, breadcrumbs, lemon zest, EVOO to bright green paste
  4. Sear lamb rack hard on all sides. Brush with Dijon, press persillade crust
  5. Roast 200°C for 8–10 min — internal 57°C for perfect pink
  6. Rest 5 min, portion into 3-cutlet servings
🍽️ Plating

Ratatouille mounded in a ring mould, centre plate. Three lamb cutlets crossed and resting upright against the ratatouille, bones pointing skyward — elegant and architectural. Lamb jus in a clean pool. Tapenade quenelle to the side. Finish with micro basil and a thread of Provençal olive oil.

SPANISH · MAIN 01
Secreto Ibérico — Romesco
Smoked paprika · Padron peppers · Patatas bravas
Ingredients · Per Person
Pork secreto (shoulder cut, 180g) 12 portions
Smoked paprika (pimentón de la Vera) 30g
Roasted red capsicum 400g
Blanched almonds (romesco) 150g
Sourdough, sherry vinegar, garlic as needed
Padron peppers 36 pcs
Kipfler potatoes (bravas) 1.2kg
Bravas sauce: tomato, paprika, cayenne 300ml
Method
  1. Romesco: blitz roasted capsicum, almonds, sourdough, sherry vinegar, garlic, pimentón — thick, textured
  2. Marinate secreto in pimentón, garlic, EVOO 4 hours minimum
  3. Grill secreto on very high heat — 3 min each side, rest 3 min. Medium-rare.
  4. Patatas bravas: fry kipfler rounds until crispy, toss in bravas sauce
  5. Padron peppers: blister in smoking olive oil, fleur de sel immediately
🍽️ Plating

Secreto sliced thinly against the grain, fanned across the plate. Romesco smeared boldly with a spoon to one side — rustic but deliberate. Bravas stacked in a tight pile. Three padron peppers resting across the meat. Finish with smoked olive oil drops and a pinch of flaky sea salt.

Chef's Note Secreto ibérico is Spain's most underrated cut — hidden between the shoulder blade and fat cap. It's what the Spanish keep for themselves. Owners who know food will recognise it immediately. Owners who don't will ask what it is — and that's the start of a great conversation.

SPANISH · MAIN 02
Arroz Negro — Saffron Alioli
Squid ink rice · Seared prawns · Aioli · Lemon
Ingredients · Per Person
Bomba rice 720g total
Squid ink sachets 4 sachets
Prawn bisque / fish stock 2L
King prawns (2 per person) 24 pcs
Squid, cleaned and scored 400g
Garlic, shallots, white wine as needed
Saffron threads 2g
Egg yolks, EVOO (alioli) 4 yolks / 300ml
Method
  1. Saffron alioli: hand-emulsify egg yolk, saffron (bloomed in warm water), garlic, EVOO — thick and golden
  2. Sofrito: slow-cook shallots, garlic, tomato 20 min — deep and sweet
  3. Toast bomba rice in sofrito, add squid ink + hot stock in stages — paella style, no stirring
  4. Cook until socarrat forms on bottom — the crispy layer is non-negotiable
  5. Sear prawns and squid in smoking oil 90 seconds — charred, not rubbery
🍽️ Plating

Deep black rice in a warm shallow bowl — glossy, dramatic. Two king prawns crossed on top, tails up. Squid laid alongside. Saffron alioli in a quenelle to the side, bright gold against jet black. Lemon half. Micro herbs. The contrast is the presentation.

SPANISH · MAIN 03
Bacalao a la Vizcaína
Salt cod · Slow-cooked Basque red pepper sauce · Confit potato
Ingredients · Per Person
Salt cod, desalted 48hrs (180g) 12 portions
Choricero peppers (dried) 8 pcs
White onion, garlic, tomato 500g / as needed
Chicken stock, white wine 400ml each
Désirée potatoes (confit) 1.5kg
EVOO, parsley, fleur de sel as needed
Method
  1. Desalt cod 48 hours in cold water, changing water 3 times
  2. Vizcaína sauce: rehydrate choricero peppers, scrape flesh. Slow-cook onion until melted (45 min), add tomato, pepper flesh, stock — deep red, silky
  3. Confit potatoes: slice 5mm, submerge in EVOO with garlic and thyme — 90°C, 1 hour
  4. Gently poach cod in EVOO at 55°C for 10 min — flaking, barely set
  5. Layer potato in a ring mould, place cod on top, sauce around
🍽️ Plating

Confit potato stack in centre. Salt cod draped on top — white, glistening, just flaking. Deep red Vizcaína pooled around the base. Drizzle of grass-green EVOO. Flat parsley leaf. Simple. Ancient. Unforgettable.

Chef's Note This is Basque grandmother cooking elevated to fine dining. The patience of the sauce and the precision of the cod temperature are everything. Owners who have eaten in San Sebastián will recognise this immediately.
Food Cost ~22% GF · DF Prep: 2 days ahead
SPANISH · MAIN 04
Cochinillo Asado — Sherry Jus
Slow-roasted suckling pig · Apple purée · Pickled fennel
Ingredients · Per Person
Suckling pig belly portions (200g) 12 pcs
Garlic, lard, thyme, rosemary as needed
Dry sherry (Fino or Manzanilla) 300ml
Chicken / pork stock 500ml
Granny Smith apples 6 pcs
Butter, cream (apple purée) 100g / 100ml
Fennel bulb, white wine vinegar 2 bulbs / 100ml
Method
  1. Rub suckling pig portions with lard, garlic, salt — roast 160°C for 2.5 hours
  2. Blast at 240°C final 10 min — crackling must shatter, not bend
  3. Sherry jus: deglaze pan with sherry, add stock, reduce by half, mount with butter
  4. Apple purée: cook peeled apple in butter until soft, blitz smooth with cream — pure white
  5. Pickled fennel: slice paper thin on mandoline, brine in vinegar, sugar, salt 30 min
🍽️ Plating

Pork belly portion placed proudly — skin facing up, crackling unbroken. Apple purée swooped to the left in a long stroke. Sherry jus in a clean moat around. Pickled fennel draped across the crackling for acidity and drama. Micro thyme finish.

SPANISH · MAIN 05
Txuleta — Aged Beef Côte de Boeuf
Basque-style bone-in rib · Smoked salt · Piquillo pepper · Chimichurri verde
Ingredients · Shared (per 2 pax)
Aged côte de boeuf (700–800g) 6 pieces
Coarse sea salt, smoked salt as needed
Piquillo peppers (jarred) 24 pcs
Flat parsley, garlic, EVOO (verde) as needed
Sherry vinegar, dried chilli flakes as needed
Baby gem lettuce, EVOO, lemon 6 heads
Method
  1. Bring côte de boeuf to room temp 1 hour. Season generously with coarse salt only.
  2. Grill on charcoal (or griddle) on very high heat — 4 min each side for rare, build crust
  3. Rest 8 min minimum — the resting IS the cooking
  4. Chimichurri verde: blitz parsley, garlic, sherry vinegar, EVOO, chilli — keep texture
  5. Char piquillo peppers briefly in dry pan. Baby gem: halve, char cut-side in smoking oil.
  6. Slice at table — bone-in, generous, impressive
🍽️ Plating — Shared Board

Serve the full bone-in côte on a warm wooden board, sliced tableside by the chef. Charred baby gem and piquillo arranged around. Chimichurri verde in a small clay pot on the side. Smoked salt in a pinch bowl. Let the owners serve themselves — shared food at a formal table is the most powerful move you can make.

Chef's Note The txuleta is Basque cattle country's answer to the perfect steak. No sauce. No distraction. Just aged beef, coarse salt, and fire. Serving it shared on a board turns the owners' table into a communal moment. They will talk about this meal differently after this dish.

Head Chef Recommendation

For an owners' lunch, I recommend serving both — not as competing menus, but as a curated progression. Open with two French mains as the first course rotation — Sole Meunière and Chicken Ballotine — then transition to two Spanish mains — Arroz Negro and the Txuleta shared board — as the centrepiece.

This structure gives owners the intellectual pleasure of the contrast, the visual drama of two very different plating philosophies, and the communal energy of the txuleta board as the finale. It also shows them the full range of what this kitchen is capable of.

France opens the meal. Spain closes it. Both are remembered.
