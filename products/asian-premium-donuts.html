The Foundation · Brioche Donut Dough · Master Recipe02
The Dough Is Everything
A premium donut at Michelin level is judged on one thing first: the dough. Not the filling. Not the glaze. The moment the tooth breaks through the crust and meets the crumb — that's the verdict. This is a brioche-style enriched dough: high butter, high egg, slow ferment. It produces a dough that is featherlight, faintly sweet, with a delicate pull and a crumb that tears into gossamer strands. It fries golden without absorbing excess oil. It holds any filling without collapsing. Everything else is decoration on this foundation.

Brioche Donut Dough
Cold butter method · overnight cold proof · 170°C fry · yields 16–18 donuts
Fry 170°C · 2 min per side Active 45 min · Cold proof 12–16 hrs Yields 16–18 donuts
Ingredients
Dry
Bread flour (strong)500 g
Caster sugar60 g
Fine salt8 g
Instant dry yeast7 g
Wet
Whole eggs3 large (150 g)
Egg yolks2 (40 g)
Full-cream milk, warm (32°C)120 ml
Vanilla bean paste1 tsp
Butter (cold, cubed)
Unsalted butter, cold, 1cm cubes180 g
For Frying
Neutral oil (rice bran or vegetable)2 litres
Sugar Coat (all donuts)
Caster sugar200 g
Fine saltpinch
Method
  1. 1Combine flour, sugar, salt, and yeast in the bowl of a stand mixer fitted with a dough hook. Keep salt and yeast on opposite sides — direct contact can kill the yeast. Mix briefly to combine.
  2. 2Add warm milk, eggs, egg yolks, and vanilla. Mix on medium speed for 5 minutes until a shaggy dough forms and begins to pull away from the sides of the bowl. The dough will look rough — this is correct.
  3. 3With the mixer running on medium-low, add cold butter one cube at a time — wait for each cube to be mostly incorporated before adding the next. This process takes 10–12 minutes. Do not rush. This is where the gluten structure is built and the butter is emulsified into the dough.
  4. 4Increase to medium-high. Knead 6–8 minutes until the dough is smooth, glossy, and passes the windowpane test — stretch a small piece thin enough to see light through without tearing. This is full gluten development.
  5. 5Shape into a ball. Place in a lightly oiled bowl. Cover tightly with cling film. Refrigerate overnight (12–16 hours). Cold fermentation develops flavour and makes the dough much easier to handle. Do not skip this step.
  6. 6Next day: remove dough. It will have doubled. Turn onto a lightly floured surface. Roll to 2.5 cm thickness. Cut rounds with a 7–8 cm cutter (for filled donuts, no hole). Place on individual squares of baking paper on trays. Cover loosely. Proof at room temperature 1.5–2 hours until puffy and slightly jiggle when the tray is shaken.
  7. 7Heat oil to 170°C in a deep, wide pot. Use a thermometer — temperature control is everything. Lower donuts (on their paper squares) into the oil. The paper releases naturally; remove with tongs. Fry 2 minutes per side until deep golden. Do not go darker — the inside is still cooking after you remove them.
  8. 8Drain on a wire rack 2 minutes. While still warm (not hot), roll in caster sugar. Cool completely before filling — filling a warm donut = condensation = soggy bottom.
Michelin Chef's Note The cold butter addition is the difference between a brioche donut and a supermarket donut. It builds a laminated fat structure within the gluten network that creates the characteristic feathery pull. Oil temperature consistency is the other non-negotiable — if the oil drops below 160°C, the donut absorbs oil. If it climbs above 180°C, the outside burns before the inside sets. A clip-on thermometer and a heavy pot with good heat retention solve both problems.

Black Sesame & Yuzu
Silky black sesame custard · yuzu glaze · edible gold · toasted sesame brittle
Custard 82°C · Set 4 hrs Prep 30 min · Set overnight Fills 6 donuts
Ingredients
Black Sesame Custard
Black sesame paste (pure, no sugar)80 g
Full-cream milk250 ml
Double cream100 ml
Egg yolks4 large
Caster sugar60 g
Cornflour20 g
Unsalted butter30 g
Fine sea salt1 pinch
Yuzu Glaze
Icing sugar, sifted200 g
Yuzu juice (fresh or bottled)3 tbsp
Yuzu zest (if available)½ tsp
White food colouring (optional)1 drop
Sesame Brittle
Caster sugar100 g
Toasted black & white sesame seeds40 g
Butter10 g
Finishing
Edible gold leaf or gold dustto garnish
Yuzu zest microplaned fresh1 yuzu
Method
  1. 1Black sesame custard: whisk egg yolks, sugar, and cornflour in a bowl until pale. Heat milk, cream, and sesame paste together until steaming — whisk constantly to keep the sesame from settling. Once steaming, slowly pour into the egg mixture while whisking (tempering). Return to the pan.
  2. 2Cook over medium heat, stirring constantly with a rubber spatula, until the custard thickens and large bubbles pop on the surface — 82°C. This kills the starch taste and sets the custard. Remove from heat. Add butter and salt. Stir until butter melts. The colour should be deep charcoal grey — extraordinary and unexpected. Press cling film directly on the surface. Refrigerate minimum 4 hours or overnight.
  3. 3Once set, transfer to a piping bag fitted with a long filling nozzle. Use a skewer to pierce a cavity in the side of each donut. Insert nozzle and fill generously — you should feel slight resistance when the donut is full (approx 40–50 g filling per donut). The hole can be sealed with a tiny dot of glaze.
  4. 4Yuzu glaze: sift icing sugar into a bowl. Add yuzu juice one tablespoon at a time until you reach a thick, flowing consistency — it should coat the back of a spoon and fall off in a thick ribbon. Too thin = slides off. Too thick = cracks when set. A few drops of white colouring gives a clean, pale ivory-white finish that contrasts beautifully against the dark filling inside.
  5. 5Sesame brittle: dry caramel method — heat sugar in a light-coloured pan without stirring until it reaches a deep amber caramel. Add butter. Immediately pour over sesame seeds on a silpat or baking paper. Spread thin. Cool completely. Break into shards. Store in an airtight container with silica gel — it absorbs moisture and turns soft quickly.
  6. 6To finish: dip the top third of each filled donut into yuzu glaze. Allow excess to drip. Set on a wire rack. While glaze is still wet, press one shard of sesame brittle into the glaze. Apply a micro-fragment of gold leaf with tweezers. Microplane fresh yuzu zest over the finished donut at the moment of service.
Michelin Plating Moment

Serve on a piece of polished black slate or a dark ceramic tile. The ivory glaze, charcoal sesame brittle, and gold leaf against the dark background is visually stunning. Cut one donut in half for display — the deep grey custard interior creates a visual "reveal" that photographs at Michelin level. Add a small pot of extra yuzu curd on the side for guests who want to intensify the citrus.

Pandan Coconut Crème
Pandan diplomat cream · butterfly pea glaze (colour-change) · toasted coconut flakes · kaffir lime zest
Crème 80°C · Whip cold Prep 35 min · Set 4 hrs Fills 6 donuts
Ingredients
Pandan Diplomat Cream
Fresh pandan leaves (or 2 tsp extract)6 leaves
Coconut milk (full fat, Ayam brand)200 ml
Full-cream milk100 ml
Egg yolks3 large
Caster sugar50 g
Cornflour18 g
Unsalted butter25 g
Gelatine leaves1.5 sheets (3 g)
Double cream, cold, whipped to soft peaks120 ml
Butterfly Pea Glaze
Dried butterfly pea flowers10 g
Hot water60 ml
Icing sugar, sifted180 g
Lemon juice½ tsp (for colour-change effect)
To Finish
Toasted coconut flakes (large)40 g
Kaffir lime zest, microplaned1 leaf
Freeze-dried pandan powder (optional)dusting
Method
  1. 1Pandan infusion: blend pandan leaves with 100 ml cold water. Strain through a fine sieve — press to extract every drop of the vivid green liquid. Combine with coconut milk and regular milk. Heat to steaming.
  2. 2Soak gelatine sheets in cold water 5 minutes. Whisk egg yolks, sugar, and cornflour until pale. Temper the hot pandan coconut milk into the egg mixture. Return to pan and cook to 80°C stirring constantly until thick. Remove from heat. Squeeze out gelatine and whisk in. Add butter. Mix until smooth. The colour: a pure, luminous jade green. Press cling film on surface. Cool to room temperature then refrigerate until completely set.
  3. 3Once cold and set, whisk the pastry cream to loosen it. Fold in cold-whipped cream in two additions — this is the diplomat technique that makes it lighter than a pure crème pâtissière. Transfer to piping bag with long filling nozzle. Fill each donut generously.
  4. 4Butterfly pea glaze: steep dried flowers in hot water 10 minutes. Strain — the liquid is a stunning deep violet-blue. Mix with sifted icing sugar to a thick glaze. The moment you add the lemon juice, the glaze shifts from violet to a vivid magenta-purple — this is the colour-change effect. Use immediately before the colour stabilises.
  5. 5Dip filled donuts in the butterfly pea glaze. Allow to set 2 minutes. While still slightly tacky, press toasted coconut flakes onto the glaze so they stand upright. Microplane kaffir lime zest directly over the top at service — the fragrance is immediate and striking.
Michelin Plating Moment

The colour contrast here is the visual story: the deep violet-to-purple glaze, the pale ivory coconut flakes, and the vivid green pandan filling visible when cut. Present on a banana leaf segment on a white ceramic plate. If serving 2–3 donuts as a dessert plate, cut one in half to show the jade interior. The fragrance of fresh kaffir lime zest microplaned tableside elevates this to an interactive sensory experience.

Ceremonial Matcha & White Chocolate
Matcha white choc ganache · ruby chocolate drizzle · mochi piece · freeze-dried raspberry dust
Ganache 45°C · Set 2 hrs Prep 25 min · Set 2 hrs Fills 6 donuts
Ingredients
Matcha White Choc Ganache
Quality white chocolate (Valrhona Opalys)200 g
Double cream100 ml
Ceremonial grade matcha powder12 g
Unsalted butter20 g
Fine salt1 pinch
Matcha Glaze (exterior)
White chocolate, melted150 g
Vegetable oil1 tbsp (for fluidity)
Ceremonial matcha8 g
Ruby Chocolate Drizzle
Ruby chocolate (Callebaut RB1)60 g
Vegetable oil½ tsp
To Finish
Fresh mochi cubes (store-bought fine)1 small cube per donut
Freeze-dried raspberry powderdusting
Matcha powder (extra, for dusting)to garnish
Method
  1. 1Matcha ganache filling: sift matcha thoroughly into cream (lumps = bitter green spots). Heat cream and matcha together until steaming — do not boil. Pour over chopped white chocolate in a bowl. Let sit 1 minute. Stir from the centre outward in slow circles until fully emulsified. Add butter. Mix until glossy. A pinch of salt sharpens the matcha flavour dramatically. Set at room temperature 1 hour then refrigerate until pipeable — soft enough to flow but firm enough to hold inside the donut.
  2. 2Fill donuts using the long nozzle method. The ganache is richer and denser than custard — fill to about 35–40 g per donut. More will make the donut feel heavy.
  3. 3Matcha glaze: melt white chocolate gently over a bain marie. Add vegetable oil (prevents the glaze from setting too hard and cracking). Sift in matcha and whisk until completely smooth. Keep at 40–45°C for dipping — too cool and it sets unevenly, too hot and it runs off.
  4. 4Dip the top of each filled donut into the matcha glaze. The colour should be a clean, vivid green. Set on wire rack.
  5. 5Ruby drizzle: melt ruby chocolate with oil. Transfer to a small piping bag or squeeze bottle. While matcha glaze is set but not fully hardened, drizzle ruby chocolate in parallel lines or a deliberate zigzag across the top. Ruby chocolate is naturally pink — the green and pink contrast is visually spectacular.
  6. 6To finish: press one small mochi cube into the top (it should half-sink into the glaze — the glaze holds it). Dust lightly with freeze-dried raspberry powder using a fine sieve. The pink dust settles on the matcha glaze and frames the ruby drizzle.
Michelin Plating Moment

The colour palette on this donut is the whole story: deep ceremonial green glaze, pink ruby chocolate lines, a snow-white mochi cube, and the blush of freeze-dried raspberry dust. Present on a white ceramic surface with a small mound of sifted matcha powder beside it. When cut, the ivory ganache interior spills slightly — pair this moment with a matcha tea service for the full sensory experience.

Thai Tea Brûlée
Thai tea crème brûlée filling · caramelised sugar dome · condensed milk drizzle · star anise dust
Custard 80°C · Brûlée to order Prep 30 min · Set 6 hrs Fills 6 donuts
Ingredients
Thai Tea Custard
Thai tea leaves (ChaTraMue brand)25 g
Full-cream milk300 ml
Double cream80 ml
Egg yolks4 large
Caster sugar70 g
Cornflour20 g
Condensed milk2 tbsp
Unsalted butter25 g
Brûlée Topping
Caster sugar (for brûlée)1 tsp per donut
Thai Tea Glaze Base
Thai tea liquid (from infusion)50 ml
Icing sugar, sifted200 g
Orange food colouring1–2 drops (deepens the colour)
To Finish
Condensed milk, for drizzle2 tbsp
Star anise (ground fine)light dusting
Thai tea powder (for extra colour)light dusting
Method
  1. 1Thai tea infusion: steep Thai tea leaves in hot milk and cream for 12 minutes. Strain very thoroughly — the liquid should be a deep, vivid orange-amber. The flavour: spiced, vanilla-adjacent, slightly floral. This is the most distinctly Asian flavour in the collection.
  2. 2Whisk egg yolks, sugar, cornflour, and condensed milk until pale. Reheat the strained Thai tea milk until steaming. Temper into the egg mixture. Return to pan and cook over medium heat to 80°C, stirring constantly, until thick and smooth. Add butter. Mix until glossy. The colour: a rich amber-orange custard. Cool with cling film on surface. Refrigerate until completely set — minimum 6 hours.
  3. 3Fill donuts. Unlike the ganache, this custard is pipeable but slightly loose — fill confidently and quickly. Seal the filling hole with a touch of glaze.
  4. 4Thai tea glaze: mix 50 ml strained Thai tea liquid with sifted icing sugar. Add orange food colouring if the natural colour needs boosting. The glaze should be a striking copper-orange. Dip the tops of filled donuts. Set on rack.
  5. 5The brûlée moment: while the glaze is fully set, spoon ½ tsp caster sugar on top of each donut in a thin even layer. Torch immediately with a small kitchen blowtorch until the sugar caramelises into a cracking amber dome. Do this to order — the brûlée dome softens within 15 minutes and must be served immediately after torching.
  6. 6Finish: drizzle condensed milk across the brûlée dome in a fine zigzag. Dust lightly with ground star anise. The condensed milk drizzle is the authentic Thai tea signal — every Thai person recognises it immediately.
Michelin Plating Moment

The brûlée must be done tableside or at the pass immediately before service — the cracking sound when the guest breaks through the sugar dome is the entire experience. Present on a ceramic slate with a small pot of condensed milk on the side. The visual: deep amber glaze, cracked caramel dome, white condensed milk drizzle. Smells like a Bangkok iced tea cart. Tastes like fine pastry. That tension is the point.

White Miso Salted Caramel
Miso caramel crème · toasted rice crunch · dark chocolate mirror glaze · fleur de sel
Caramel 118°C · Cool before adding miso Prep 35 min · Set 4 hrs Fills 6 donuts
Ingredients
Miso Caramel Custard
Caster sugar120 g
Double cream, warm200 ml
White miso paste (Marukome Shiro)35 g
Unsalted butter40 g
Egg yolks3 large
Cornflour15 g
Fleur de selgenerous pinch
Dark Chocolate Glaze
Dark chocolate 70% (Valrhona Guanaja)120 g
Double cream60 ml
Glucose syrup15 g
Neutral oil1 tsp
Toasted Rice Crunch
Japanese short-grain rice (cooked & dried overnight)60 g
Neutral oil (for frying)500 ml
Sea salt flakespinch
To Finish
Fleur de sel3–4 crystals per donut
Edible gold dust (optional)light dusting
Method
  1. 1Miso caramel: dry caramel — heat sugar in a heavy saucepan without stirring until it reaches a deep amber at 170°C (use thermometer). Remove from heat. Slowly pour warm cream in — it will bubble violently. Return to heat and stir until smooth. Cool to 60°C. Whisk in miso paste and butter. The miso adds a savoury depth that makes this caramel taste unlike anything else. Season with fleur de sel. Cool completely.
  2. 2Whisk egg yolks and cornflour together in a separate bowl. Heat the miso caramel until steaming. Temper into the egg mixture. Return to pan, cook to 80°C until thick. It will be a deep amber-brown with an extraordinary aroma. Cool with cling on surface. Refrigerate 4+ hours.
  3. 3Toasted rice crunch: day-old cooked rice, spread on a tray and dried overnight. Deep-fry in batches at 180°C until each grain puffs and turns golden, 30–45 seconds. Drain. Season immediately with sea salt flakes. These toasted rice crisps are Japan's answer to Rice Krispies — more flavour, more texture, less sugar.
  4. 4Dark chocolate glaze: heat cream with glucose. Pour over chopped dark chocolate. Stir from centre. Add oil for fluidity. Keep at 35–38°C for dipping. This glaze sets with a slight sheen rather than a hard crack.
  5. 5Fill donuts. Dip in chocolate glaze. While still wet, press toasted rice crunch liberally onto the top and sides of the glaze — they should cover the glaze in a crunchy, irregular layer. Set 5 minutes. Finish with 3–4 crystals of fleur de sel and a very light dusting of gold dust if desired.
Michelin Plating Moment

The paradox of this donut — sweet caramel, savoury miso, bitter dark chocolate, salty fleur de sel, crunchy toasted rice — is what makes it the most sophisticated flavour in the collection. Serve on dark slate. When cut, the amber caramel custard spills. The toasted rice crunch has an audible, satisfying snap. Present with a few loose toasted rice crisps scattered on the plate for textural context.

Lychee & Rose
Lychee compote core · rose mascarpone cream · rose water glaze · freeze-dried lychee · micro rose petals
Compote 75°C · Mascarpone cold Prep 25 min · Set 3 hrs Fills 6 donuts
Ingredients
Lychee Compote (jam core)
Fresh lychees, peeled, pitted (or canned)300 g
Caster sugar60 g
Lemon juice1 tbsp
Rose water1 tsp
Pectin NH (or 2 gelatine sheets)4 g
Rose Mascarpone Cream
Mascarpone, cold200 g
Double cream, cold100 ml
Icing sugar, sifted40 g
Rose water (quality — not perfume)1½ tsp
Pink food colouring (gel)1 small drop
Rose Water Glaze
Icing sugar, sifted200 g
Rose water2 tsp
Hot water2 tbsp
Pink gel food colouring2–3 drops
To Finish
Freeze-dried lychee slices3 slices per donut
Dried edible rose petals (food grade)small pinch per donut
Lychee syrup (from canned lychees)light brush
Method
  1. 1Lychee compote: roughly chop lychees. Combine with sugar, lemon juice, and rose water in a small saucepan. Cook over medium heat until the lychees break down and the mixture thickens — 15 minutes. Mix pectin (or squeezed gelatine) in. Stir 1 minute more. Pour into a shallow tray to set. Once cold and firm, cut into small blocks that can be pushed into the centre of the donut before the mascarpone cream. This double-filling technique — jam core inside cream — is what elevates this to fine pastry territory.
  2. 2Rose mascarpone cream: whip cold mascarpone and cream together on medium speed until soft peaks — 2–3 minutes. Add sifted icing sugar, rose water, and one small drop of pink gel colouring. Whip to firm peaks. The colour should be a blush pink — barely there, not neon. Taste and adjust rose water — it should be fragrant but not soapy. Pipe into a bag with a large star tip.
  3. 3Dual-fill method: make a wider hole in the top of each donut (use a 1.5 cm round cutter to cut a shallow well). Push one lychee compote block into the bottom of the well. Pipe rose mascarpone cream over the top in a generous rosette swirl using the star tip — building height above the surface of the donut by 2–3 cm. This is now an open-top filled donut, more patisserie than traditional donut.
  4. 4Rose water glaze: mix icing sugar, rose water, and hot water. Add pink colouring to a soft blush. Drizzle around the base of the mascarpone rosette rather than dipping — the cream is the visual centrepiece, not the glaze.
  5. 5Finish: place 2–3 freeze-dried lychee slices tucked into the mascarpone rosette. Scatter a pinch of dried rose petals. Brush the base of the donut with lychee syrup for a subtle gloss and fragrance.
Michelin Plating Moment

This is the most visually feminine donut in the collection and the most delicate in flavour. Present on white porcelain. The blush pink mascarpone rosette, pale freeze-dried lychee, and pastel rose petals create a dessert that photographs like something from a Tokyo patisserie. The lychee-rose combination is a classic of Chinese fine dining. On a donut, it becomes unexpected and beautiful. Serve with a small glass of chilled lychee juice on the side.

The Philosophy
A Michelin chef approaches a donut the way they approach any dish: with respect for the ingredient, precision in technique, and a clear point of view about what the eating experience should feel like. The six flavours in this collection are not random. They represent the full emotional spectrum of Asian pastry — the dark drama of black sesame, the tropical nostalgia of pandan, the ceremony of matcha, the street food warmth of Thai tea, the intellectual sophistication of miso caramel, and the delicate romance of lychee rose.

A donut at this level is not a snack. It is a dessert course that happens to be round. Treat it that way and your guests will too.
