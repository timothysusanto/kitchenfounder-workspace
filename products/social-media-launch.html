Phase 1 — Pre-Open (Days 1–14)

Build anticipation before you open. Tease, reveal, and create FOMO. Goal: 200+ followers before day 1.

Phase 2 — Launch Week (Days 15–21)

Maximum output. Post every day. Stories all day on open day. Goal: first 50 Google reviews.

Deepen relationships. Show behind the scenes. Drive repeat visits and UGC. Goal: first viral post.

✍️ 15 Real-World Caption Templates

Written the way a real social media manager writes — not corporate. Not generic. Adapt to your voice.

1. 🎉 Grand Opening Announcement High Priority

We're open. [Restaurant Name] is now serving at [address], [city]. [2 sentences about what makes you different — not fluff, something real.] First week only: [offer — e.g. complimentary amuse-bouche / free dessert / house cocktail on us]. Reservations: [link or phone]. Walk-ins welcome. See you tonight. 🔥
Post this at 7am, 12pm, and 6pm on Day 1. Use your best photo. Tag your location every time.

2. 🍽️ Dish Feature (The Right Way)

The [Dish Name]. This one took [X] attempts to get right. [Protein/hero ingredient] — [how it's sourced or why it's special]. [Technique] — [1 sentence on the cooking method]. The result: [3 sensory words — e.g. smoky, rich, impossible to share]. Available [lunch/dinner/daily] at [Restaurant Name]. 📍 [Address] | 📞 [Phone] | 🔗 Link in bio
One dish per post. Don't list the whole menu. Go deep on one thing.

3. 🎬 Behind the Scenes (Performs Best on TikTok)

5pm. Two hours until service. This is what 80 covers of prep looks like. [Short video or carousel — mise en place, sauce reducing, bread baking] Every single element on that plate was touched by someone on this team today. Nothing came out of a packet. Nothing was rushed. That's the standard. Every service. 📍 [Restaurant Name] — [City] #cheflife #behindthescenes #[city]food
This is your highest-performing content type. Film it raw — not polished. Authentic > aesthetic on BTS content.

4. 👨‍🍳 Chef / Staff Spotlight

Meet [Name]. [Their role] at [Restaurant Name]. [X] years in hospitality. Came up through [notable background]. Ask them about [their signature move / favourite dish / something fun]. They're the reason [specific dish or element] tastes the way it does. We're lucky. 🙏 [Tag them if they have IG]
Staff content drives massive engagement. Your team has fans before they know it. Post one per week.

5. 📸 Reposting a Customer Photo (UGC)

When guests do this, we don't take it for granted. 📸 [Quote from their caption or review] Thank you [tag them] for sharing this. It genuinely means a lot to this team. If you've been in recently — post your shot and tag us. We'll feature it here. 📍 [Restaurant Name] | [Address]
Always DM for permission before reposting. UGC is your most trusted content — never skip the ask.

6. 🔥 Weekly Special / Limited Run

THIS WEEK ONLY: [Dish Name]. [What makes it special — seasonal ingredient, special technique, one-time thing] [Price] per person. Available [days] only. Once it's gone, it's gone — we're not making more. [Photo of the dish] Book or walk in: [details]
Scarcity is real if you mean it. Don't fake "limited" — people notice. Only post this for genuinely limited items.

7. ⭐ Responding to a 5-Star Review

[Guest name] — thank you for this. I'm showing this to the team at briefing tonight. They work hard and this is exactly the kind of feedback that makes it worth it. The [dish they mentioned] is [Chef's name]'s personal project — glad it landed. Come back soon. First round is on us. 🥂
Always respond within 24 hours. Personalise every single response. Copy-paste responses destroy trust.

8. 😤 Responding to a Negative Review (Public)

Thank you for taking the time to write this. What you described is not the experience we want anyone to have here, and I'm genuinely sorry it went that way. I'd like to understand what happened and make it right. Please DM us or email [email] — I'll respond personally. Your feedback has already been shared with the team.
Never be defensive publicly. Never argue. The response isn't for the reviewer — it's for everyone else reading it.

9. 📍 Local Collab / Neighbourhood Post

Shoutout to [local business name] — [one sentence about who they are]. We [source our X from them / partner with them on Y / just love what they do]. Supporting the people who support this neighbourhood matters to us. [Tag them] — worth a visit. 📍 We're at [address] | They're at [address]
These posts get reshared by the business you tag = free reach into their audience. Do one per month minimum.

10. 🎊 Milestone Post (100 guests / first month / first review)

[X] guests in [X] days. We didn't expect that. We opened on [date] with [X] staff, [X] seats, and more nerves than we'd like to admit. [X] services later — we're finding our rhythm. To everyone who's come in, left a review, or sent us a message: this team sees it and it means everything. We're just getting started. 🔥
Vulnerability performs. Don't fake confidence you don't have. Real > polished every time.

11. ❓ Engagement Post (Ask a Question)

Hot take: [bold opinion about food/dining/your cuisine]. Agree or disagree? 👇 (We have a view on this. Drop yours in the comments and we'll reply to every one.) [Photo that matches the topic]
Engagement posts hack the algorithm. More comments = more reach. Your opinion needs to be bold enough to get a reaction.

12. 📖 Origin Story (Post Once, Pin It)

Why I opened this restaurant. (Thread 🧵) [3-5 sentences on your real story — not the polished version. The struggle, the why, the moment you decided.] I've worked in hospitality for [X] years. I've watched people open restaurants and lose everything. I've also watched people build something that outlasts them. [Restaurant Name] is my version of that second story. Come find out if we got it right. 📍 [Address]
Pin this to your Instagram profile. This is the post that converts a viewer into a follower. Write it once, write it right.

13. 🌙 End of Service Post

Service done. [X] covers. [X] hours. Tonight's highlight: [something specific — a table's reaction, a dish that flew, a team moment] Back tomorrow at [time]. [Candid kitchen photo — team at end of service, clean pass, empty dining room]
Post these 3-4 times per week in the first month. They humanise the brand and create a narrative your followers follow like a TV show.

14. 🎁 Giveaway / Competition

GIVEAWAY: Dinner for two at [Restaurant Name]. 🍽️ To enter: 1. Follow [Instagram handle] 2. Like this post 3. Tag the person you'd bring in the comments Winner announced [date]. Open to [city] residents only. [Best photo you have — make it worth entering for]
Run this in week 2-3. Don't run it before you've proven your food. The prize needs to mean something first.

15. 📱 Story CTA (Convert Followers to Guests)

Haven't been in yet? We've had [X] guests through the door in [X] weeks. Here's what people keep coming back for: [top 3 dishes with one line each] Book your table — link in bio. Or just walk in. We'll look after you. [Restaurant Name] — [Address] — Open [hours]
Post this as a Story with a link sticker. Do it every Friday and Sunday. It directly drives bookings.
🏷️ How Hashtags Actually Work in 2025
⚠️ The truth most guides won't tell you: On Instagram, hashtags matter less than they used to. The algorithm now distributes content based on interest graphs — who engages with similar content — not just hashtags. But they still help for discoverability, especially local search. Use them smart, not spam.
✅ Hashtag Best Practices
• Use 5–10 hashtags max on Instagram (not 30 — that's 2019)
• Mix: 2-3 broad + 3-4 niche + 2-3 local
• Avoid hashtags with 100M+ posts — you drown
• Sweet spot: 50K–5M posts per hashtag
• On TikTok: 3–5 hashtags only — algorithm is content-first
• Research what your top 5 competitors use
• Rotate hashtag sets — don't use the same 10 every post
• Local hashtags outperform generic ones for restaurants
• Add hashtags in the caption, not comments (2025 best practice)
🚫 Stop Using These
Shadowbanned, oversaturated, or algorithmically penalised:
#food #restaurant #instafood #foodie #yummy #delicious #foodporn #instagood #follow #like4like
These have 100M–2B posts. Your content disappears in seconds.

🎵 TikTok — Different Game, Different Rules
Top Restaurant TikTok Hooks
"POV: You're about to try the best [dish] in [city]..."
"Things nobody tells you about opening a restaurant..."
"Watch us prep [X] covers in 2 hours..."
"I spent $[X] opening a restaurant. Here's week 1..."
"Rating my competitor's menu so you don't have to..."
"Day [X] of owning a restaurant — it went like this..."
"The dish that sells out every single service..."
"What $[price] gets you at [Restaurant Name]..."
TikTok Posting Rules
• Post 3–5 times/week minimum in launch month
• First 3 seconds = everything — hook hard and fast
• 7–15 second videos have highest completion rate
• Add text overlay on every video (many watch on mute)
• Use trending audio where it fits naturally
• Post between 6–10am or 7–11pm user local time
• Reply to every comment in first hour (signals algorithm)
• Hashtags: #restauranttok #cheflife #foodtok max 5
• Don't delete low-performing videos — they can resurge

🎯 Platform-by-Platform Strategy (Real Talk)
📸 Instagram — Your Brand Home

Who it's for: People who already know you or discovered you. It's a credibility check. Before anyone books a table, they check your Instagram first.

What to post: Beautiful food photos, staff stories, behind the scenes Reels, customer features. Aesthetic consistency matters here more than TikTok.

Frequency: 4–5 feed posts/week + daily Stories. Stories are more important than feed for restaurants — they show the living, breathing version of your brand.

🎵 TikTok — Your Discovery Engine

Who it's for: People who've never heard of you. TikTok's algorithm shows your content to strangers — even with zero followers. One video can bring 10,000 new people to your page overnight.

What to post: Raw, real, entertaining. Kitchen prep videos. Day-in-the-life. Opinion takes on food. "What $45 gets you at [restaurant]" style content. Don't make ads — make content.

Frequency: 5–7 times/week in your first 60 days. The algorithm rewards consistency. Post even when you're tired. One video at midnight can go viral by morning.

🔍 Google Business Profile — The Conversion Tool

Who it's for: People actively searching for somewhere to eat. They type "best burger [city]" — do you show up?

What to do: Claim and complete your profile 100%. Add photos weekly. Post updates monthly. Respond to every review within 24 hours. Ask every happy guest to leave a review.

The truth: A restaurant with 50 Google reviews at 4.7★ beats a restaurant with 1,000 Instagram followers every single time. Google drives walk-in traffic. Instagram drives awareness. Both matter.

📧 Email List — Your Most Valuable Asset

Why it matters: Instagram can suspend your account tomorrow. TikTok could be banned. Your email list is yours — nobody can take it. It's also the highest-converting channel for restaurants.

How to build it: QR code on table → "Join our VIP list for exclusive offers." Ask at checkout. Run a launch giveaway that requires email entry. Start building from day 1. Even 200 emails is powerful.

💡 The Content Pillars Framework

A real social media manager doesn't post randomly. They rotate through 5 content pillars. Aim for each pillar 1–2× per week.

🍽️ Pillar 1: The Food
Hero dish shots. Process videos. Ingredient stories. What makes it different. How it's made.
👨‍🍳 Pillar 2: The People
Chef stories. Staff spotlights. Founder content. Team moments. The humans behind the brand.
🎬 Pillar 3: Behind The Scenes
Prep footage. Delivery day. End of service. The unseen work. Real over polished.
❤️ Pillar 4: Social Proof
Customer photos. Review screenshots. Milestone posts. Testimonials. Let others sell for you.
💬 Pillar 5: Engagement
Opinion posts. Questions. Hot takes. Polls. Conversations that invite responses.
📣 Pillar 6: Offers & CTAs
Specials. Events. Giveaways. Booking CTAs. Limited-time reasons to act now. Max 1–2× per week.
