🚨
Nut allergies can be life-threatening. There is no cure — only prevention. In Australia, anaphylaxis from food allergy affects approximately 1 in 70 children and 1 in 150 adults. Tree nuts and peanuts are among the most common triggers. A trace amount — even residue on a shared utensil — can trigger a severe reaction. Every team member is responsible for keeping our guests safe.

⚠️ Important: Peanuts are a LEGUME (not a tree nut) but are among the most dangerous allergens in Australia. Always treat peanut and tree nut allergies as equally life-threatening. A guest with a peanut allergy may NOT be allergic to tree nuts — always ask specifically which nuts they are allergic to.

🆘 Anaphylaxis Symptoms — Act Fast
😮
Mild
Tingling lips, hives, runny nose, stomach pain
😰
Moderate
Swelling face/tongue, vomiting, dizziness, wheeze
😱
SEVERE
Throat closure, breathing difficulty, collapse, loss of consciousness
⚠️ Symptoms can appear within minutes of exposure or be delayed up to 2 hours. Do not wait — act at the first sign of a reaction.
✅ When a Guest Declares a Nut Allergy
  • Inform the kitchen immediately — verbal + written
  • Check ALL components of the dish including garnish, sauce, dressing, oil
  • Use clean utensils and separate boards — never reuse
  • Change gloves before preparing the allergen-free dish
  • Prepare on a clean, sanitised surface
  • Plate separately — never on the same pass as other dishes
  • Deliver directly to the guest — confirm with them before serving
  • Document the allergy request and which staff member handled it

🚨 Emergency Response — Suspected Anaphylaxis

1
Lay guest flat. Do NOT let them stand or walk.
2
Use EpiPen if available. Outer thigh. Hold 3 seconds.
3
Call 000 immediately. Say "anaphylaxis".
4
Stay with guest. Monitor breathing until ambulance arrives.
5
Notify manager. Document the incident. Preserve the food.
✗ Never Do This
  • Say "I think it's fine" without checking
  • Remove nuts from a dish that already contains them
  • Use the same tongs/spoon after touching nuts
  • Assume cooking destroys the allergen (it does NOT)
  • Tell a guest "just a little bit won't hurt"
  • Ignore a guest's allergy declaration
  • Allow cross-contamination in storage (store nuts in sealed, labelled containers, away from other ingredients)
⚖️ Legal Obligations — Australia
FSANZ Food Standards Code Standard 1.2.3 requires all packaged food to declare the presence of peanuts and tree nuts. For unpackaged / menu items, the Food Act in each state requires that allergen information be provided on request — and staff must be able to accurately answer.

Penalties: Failure to disclose allergens that causes serious injury can result in prosecution under Australian Consumer Law and state food safety legislation. Venue and individual staff can be held liable.

Best practice: Treat every allergen declaration as a legal obligation, not a preference.
