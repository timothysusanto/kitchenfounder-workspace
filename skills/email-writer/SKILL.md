# Skill: email-writer
## When to use
Use when Mothy asks to write, draft, or create an email — especially for Accor/Novotel work context, but also personal or KitchenFounder business emails.

## Mothy's Voice
- Direct and confident, not defensive
- Owns his part, doesn't over-explain
- Professional warmth — collegial, not cold
- Solutions-focused — always ends with next action
- Never sycophantic, never grovelling

## Work Email Structure
1. **Acknowledge** — thank them for raising it (1 line max)
2. **Context** — relevant background facts (2-3 lines)
3. **Action/Solution** — what will be done (bullet points preferred)
4. **Forward commitment** — what Mothy will do next
5. **Sign-off:** Best regards, / Timothy Susanto / Head Chef / Novotel Sydney Olympic Park

## Standard Sign-off
```
Best regards,
Timothy Susanto
Head Chef
Novotel Sydney Olympic Park
```

## Common Email Types & Approach

### Performance / P&L challenge
- Lead with data (food cost recovery, vs Pullman, $ vs %)
- Own the % but defend with volume context
- Always have a forward action plan
- Never blame team publicly in emails

### Guest complaint (via Josephine/GM)
- Acknowledge the guest experience
- Explain root cause briefly
- State specific action being taken
- Thank FOH for raising it

### Supplier / Operational
- Clear, factual, professional
- Specific dates and numbers
- Follow-up action with timeline

### Staff performance acknowledgment
- Warm but fair
- Specific about what they did well
- Connect to team/property goals

### Operational updates (waste, temperature, procedures)
- Brief bullet points
- What will change + who is responsible
- Request team support where needed

## KitchenFounder Business Emails
- Confident, founder-level tone
- Friendly but professional
- Sign off: Timothy Susanto / KitchenFounder / kitchenfounder.com

## Things to NEVER do in Mothy's emails
- Never be defensive or make excuses
- Never throw colleagues under the bus
- Never use corporate jargon ("going forward" only when natural)
- Never write more than needed
- Never forget the sign-off
